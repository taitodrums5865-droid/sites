Is CMMC Phase 2 Suspended? What the Official Sources Say

Yes, CMMC Phase 2 is suspended by Department memo and class deviation, even though the regulations themselves still print the original phases. That split explains why two people can read official pages and reach opposite conclusions.

A limit on this check: war.gov, the CIO site and the acq.osd.mil PDFs blocked our automated fetch. The memo and deviation details here therefore come from law-firm summaries, compliance-vendor summaries and search excerpts. Open the originals before you rely on an exact phrase.

To repeat this check yourself:

  1. Open the DoW CIO CMMC page and read the status notice and library documents.
  2. Open the DARS class deviations page and look under Part 240 for a revision newer than Rev. 3.
  3. Search FederalRegister.gov for "Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification" and sort by newest.
  4. Read the amendments on your own solicitation. This step outranks the other three for your bid.

Common mistake: treating a forum post or vendor blog as the status. Fix: note the date of your check and cite a government document or your solicitation text in your bid file.

What Does the CMMC Phase 2 Suspension Mean for Your Bids?

The CMMC Phase 2 suspension means fewer third-party requirements in new DoD solicitations, but it does not remove the self-assessment gate or your safeguarding duties. The confusion runs both ways. Some pages say CMMC is gone, while others still present Nov 10 as a live deadline or quote the unchanged rule.

What changes for a bidder:

  • New solicitations should name only Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) during the suspension, according to Latham's memo summary.
  • Active solicitations with C3PAO or Level 3 requirements are to be amended as soon as possible.
  • Existing contracts get modified before the next option period or scheduled administrative modification.
  • No waivers will be granted during the review period.

What does not change:

  • The contracting officer still checks SPRS before award and before an option or extension when a CMMC status is required.
  • Annual affirmations and flowdowns continue where the clause applies.
  • DFARS 252.204-7012 still governs covered defense information.

For example, suppose a small machine shop finds an RFQ posted before July 13 that names Level 2 (C3PAO), with no amendment yet. The shop should not assume the requirement has dropped or give up on the bid. It should send a written question before the Q&A cutoff and bid only on the answer or amendment it receives. As one law firm notes, the clause controls until a modification issues, so ask for the change in writing.

CMMC Phase 1 vs Phase 2 vs Phase 3: What Starts Nov 10, 2026?

Nothing new starts on Nov 10, 2026, because the Phase 2 requirements are suspended and Phase 1 conditions simply continue. The table separates what the rule says from where things stand now.

CMMC Phase 1 vs Phase 2 vs Phase 3: What Starts Nov 10, 2026?. Source: SAM.gov
PhaseDate in the rule's scheduleWhat 32 CFR 170.3(e) describesStatus as of Nov 6, 2026
Phase 1Nov 10, 2025Level 1 (Self) or Level 2 (Self) as a condition of awardActive
Phase 2Nov 10, 2026Adds Level 2 (C3PAO), generally at award; Level 3 possibleSuspended
Phase 3Nov 10, 2027Extends Level 2 (C3PAO) to all applicable buys; Level 3 at award, deferrableOn hold
Phase 4Nov 10, 2028Applies to all applicable solicitations and contracts, including older contracts' optionsOn hold

Phase 3 and Phase 4 dates are calculated. The rule sets each phase one calendar year after the last, starting from the DFARS rule's effective date.

Two details matter for your calendar. First, the clause trigger still has its own dates in the DFARS. Until November 9, 2028, 252.204-7021 goes in when a program office determines a specific level is required, including FAR Part 12 buys. From November 10, 2028, the test becomes whether you will use your own systems to process, store or transmit FCI or CUI. Summaries of Rev. 3 report the same dates in deviation section 240.371-5.

Second, buys solely for commercially available off-the-shelf items do not use the clause or the provision.

Is CMMC Level 2 Suspended? Level 2 Self-Assessment vs C3PAO Certification

No, CMMC Level 2 is not suspended; what is suspended is the requirement for C3PAO assessment, while Level 2 (Self) remains available to buyers. "CMMC Level 2 suspension" is a misleading phrase. The 110 requirements still exist, and Department enforcement during the pause runs through self-assessments and selected government-led assessments.

Lawyers do not describe this point identically. Latham quotes the "must only include" self-assessment language, while Holland & Knight says DoW may still require a C3PAO assessment under its Phase I discretion. You don't have to settle that dispute. If C3PAO is in your document, treat it as live until an amendment or written answer changes it.

Common mistake: cancelling a C3PAO plan because a headline says "suspended." If your CUI work is steady and a prime wants the certificate, weigh the plan against that customer's terms. A completed Level 2 (C3PAO) status satisfies lesser designations under the "or higher" language, and Covington notes there may be benefits to a third-party assessment even when it isn't required. If nobody is asking for one, Level 2 (Self) is the sensible pick for now, because the Task Force outcome could still reshape the requirement.

How to Tell if a Solicitation Requires CMMC Level 1 or Level 2

To tell if a solicitation requires CMMC Level 1 or Level 2, find DFARS 252.204-7021 and 252.204-7025, then read the level filled in. Level 1 generally matches FCI work and Level 2 matches CUI work, but the contracting officer's fill-in is what you bid against.

  1. Open the notice on SAM.gov and download everything: the base document, every attachment and every amendment. A later amendment may remove a level, as the implementing instructions contemplate.
  2. Search each file for CMMC, 7021, 7025, 7012, 7997, 7019, 7020, 52.204-21 and 52.240-93. Clause numbers vary because the deviation moved some text to Part 240 numbering.
  3. Read the fill-in. The clause and provision offer Level 1 (Self), Level 2 (Self), Level 2 (C3PAO) or Level 3 (DIBCAC). A clause listed by number with no level is a question for the contracting officer.
  4. Check for a COTS-only buy. Solely COTS acquisitions fall outside the CMMC clause.
  5. Read the proposal instructions and evaluation factors. In a Uniform Contract Format RFP, that means Sections K, L and M. An RFQ may put these in one short document, and our RFQ vs RFP guide shows how to tell. Note where CMMC UIDs must go, since offerors must list them in the proposal.
  6. Read the SOW or PWS for the data. Look for CUI markings, drawings, technical data and any DD Form 254 or data-handling attachment. Ask what you would store on your own systems.
  7. Match your finding to the table below.
  8. Ask if the answer is still unclear, using the sample question after the table and the method and deadline the solicitation gives.

Sample question to the contracting officer (example; edit to fit):

"Solicitation [number], Section [x], cites DFARS 252.204-7021/252.204-7025 and states CMMC [level]. Given the July 13, 2026 CIO memorandum and Class Deviation 2026-O0025, Rev. 3, please confirm whether this level remains the requirement at award. If it will change, please confirm whether an amendment will be issued before the closing date."

A sources sought notice can also reveal the level early. If the notice asks about cyber capability, our sources sought template gives you a place to state your current status accurately.

Common mistake: waiting for award to post, because the provision says the status is needed before award. SPRS generates the UID after you enter results, and the provision wants UIDs in your proposal. Unless the solicitation says otherwise, post before you submit.

CMMC Level 1 vs Level 2: Which Does Your Small Business Need?

Your small business needs the level matching its data and contract, not its size: FCI points to Level 1, while CUI points to Level 2. A five-person firm handling CUI drawings faces more work than a 200-person firm that handles only FCI.

Read those dollar figures with care. They come from DoD's rulemaking analysis as relayed by secondary sources, and we couldn't open the Federal Register text, so check the final rule for the current figures. Secureframe describes the estimates as assessment, reporting and affirmation only, assuming the security work is already done, and it reports ranges rather than single figures. Fixing gaps costs extra, so real totals may run higher. SBA Administrator Kelly Loeffler cited Level 2 (C3PAO) costs of up to $600,000, which shows how wide the estimates can get.

As an example of the decision: a janitorial firm that receives only a base access schedule and invoice data is likely in FCI territory, so Level 1 is the realistic target. A subcontractor that receives CUI-marked drawings on its own laptop is a Level 2 case. If you can route all work through a government-provided system and avoid storing the data yourself, ask the contracting officer how that affects your scope before you spend money.

How to Check and Post Your CMMC Status in SPRS Before You Bid

Check and post your CMMC status in SPRS through a PIEE account with the SPRS Cyber Vendor User role, then have your Affirming Official affirm it. Budget extra time for access, because approvals go through your company's account administrator.

The labels below come from the SPRS access page and excerpts of the Level 1 and Level 2 quick entry guides (V4.0, dated Dec 2024 and Feb 2025). Screens may have changed, so check the current guide.

  1. Confirm SAM is ready. You need an active SAM registration with an Electronic Business POC. If you aren't there yet, use our SAM.gov registration guide.
  2. Get into PIEE. Your CAGE code must be in the PIEE Vendor Group Structure, and each CAGE needs a Contractor Account Administrator (CAM). Use the Find my Account Administrator button on the PIEE Vendor Customer Support page if you don't know yours.
  3. Request the role. Ask for SPRS Cyber Vendor User, which is required to enter CMMC or NIST SP 800-171 Basic assessments. Contractor/Vendor (Support Role) is for monitoring data only.
  4. Check approval. Go to My Account, then Manage Roles. The SPRS tile may appear before it is active.
  5. Open the right tab. Go to CMMC Assessments, then CMMC Level 1 (Self) or CMMC Level 2 (Self), and add a new assessment.
  6. Enter the required data. Level 1 needs level, status date, scope, all in-scope CAGE codes and result. Level 2 adds the overall score and any POA&M use. On Level 2, work through each family with Save and Continue.
  7. Affirm. The Affirming Official reviews the details, checks the certification box and selects Affirm. The Affirming Official also needs the SPRS Cyber Vendor User role. The affirmation must state the company has implemented and will maintain all applicable requirements.
  8. Copy the CMMC UID into your proposal. The contracting officer checks SPRS for each UID you provide, so a mistyped UID can cause trouble. Update the list if new UIDs are generated.

Common problems and fixes:

  • Affirming Official can't see the assessment. SPRS pulls the CAGE hierarchy from SAM, and a wrong hierarchy can hide it. Correct the SAM record first.
  • Status disappeared after an edit. The Level 1 guide warns that editing a Final assessment can turn it to "No CMMC Status". Re-check and re-affirm after any change.
  • Status expired. Affirmations lapse annually, and a three-year-old assessment shows as No CMMC Status (Expired). Put both dates on your calendar.
  • Wrong record sent to a prime. A NIST SP 800-171 score and a CMMC status are separate records. Ask which one is wanted.

What Happens if You Bid Without CMMC Status on Nov 10, 2026?

If a solicitation names a CMMC level and you have no valid status in SPRS at award, you are ineligible. That result comes from the provision itself, and it has nothing to do with Nov 10. You may still submit under most solicitations' terms, but the award check would fail unless your status is current by then.

A Conditional status can keep a Level 2 bidder eligible. 32 CFR 170.21 sets these limits:

  • Minimum score: at least 0.8 of the Level 2 requirements, which works out to 88 of 110.
  • Point limit: POA&M items generally cannot be worth more than 1 point. The exception is SC.L2-3.13.11 CUI Encryption, when encryption is in use but not FIPS-validated.
  • Never on a POA&M: AC.L2-3.1.20, AC.L2-3.1.22, CA.L2-3.12.4, PE.L2-3.10.3, PE.L2-3.10.4 and PE.L2-3.10.5.
  • Deadline: closeout within 180 days of the Conditional status date, or the status expires.
  • Level 1: no POA&M at all, and the DFARS requires a final status.

Before you click Affirm, walk through this short review:

  1. Compare your score to a current list of your systems. A score based on a network you have since changed is unreliable.
  2. Confirm that your System Security Plan exists and matches your environment, since CA.L2-3.12.4 can't sit on a POA&M.
  3. Save your evidence for six years.
  4. Have the Affirming Official read the statement and confirm they have authority to make it.

The money risk is in the accuracy of that statement. Latham warns that false or overstated self-affirmations may create False Claims Act liability, plus termination and suspension or debarment risk. The Justice Department's MORSECORP settlement is a real example. The company paid $4.6 million after reporting a score of 104 when a consultant later put it at -142, and it did not update the score until June 2023. A low honest score beats a high wrong one.

The check also repeats after award. Contracting officers review SPRS before exercising an option or extending performance, so a lapsed affirmation can affect work you already won.

Do Subcontractors Need CMMC if the Prime Does?

Yes, subcontractors need CMMC when they handle FCI or CUI, because DFARS 252.204-7021 requires primes to flow down the correct level. Paragraph (d)(1)(ii) sends primes to 32 CFR 170.23, and (d)(4) requires subs to affirm before subcontract award and annually.

Note that primes may keep stricter terms. The suspension memo binds DoD personnel, not your prime's subcontract terms, so a prime worried about liability can still ask for C3PAO. That is a commercial decision you can negotiate.

To turn this into a teaming conversation:

  1. Name the work package and ask what data it involves. "Will we receive CUI, or only FCI?" is a fair first question.
  2. Ask which CMMC level and which NIST record the prime wants to see.
  3. Ask whether the prime can hold the CUI in its own environment so your scope stays smaller.
  4. Get the answer in writing, then match it to your SPRS entry.
  5. Put your current status on your capability statement only if it's accurate today.

For example, you could write: "We're interested in the [work package] under [solicitation]. Based on our review, we'd handle FCI only and hold Final Level 1 (Self). Please confirm the level you intend to flow down." This shows the prime you've read the requirement. Our subcontracting guide covers how to find the primes.

Common mistake: a sub assumes the prime's certification covers it. It doesn't, since the rule applies to any sub that processes, stores or transmits FCI or CUI on its own systems.

Does DFARS 252.204-7012 Still Apply if CMMC Is Delayed?

Yes, DFARS 252.204-7012 still applies if CMMC is delayed, because the suspension doesn't change its obligations. If your contract has this clause and you hold covered defense information, your NIST SP 800-171 duties were in place before CMMC and remain.

Sources disagree on one practical point: whether a Basic score is still owed when 7019 is absent. One analysis says the deviation removes the "Basic" concept from the assessment clause, while another says the SPRS score hasn't changed. Our pick for a CUI-handling firm is to keep a current, accurate score on file, because older contracts and prime templates may still cite the 7019/7020 pair. It's a low-cost precaution. Where your own document names a specific clause, follow that clause.

Common mistake: pausing security work because of the suspension. Government audits may still occur where the safeguarding clause applies, and the 72-hour reporting clock doesn't pause.

FAQ

What is the latest CMMC Level 2 suspension update?

The newest report we found, from October 27, 2026, describes unadopted task force options, including a possible 90-day restart for a narrower population. DoD declined to comment. Level 2 (Self) remains usable, and the C3PAO requirement stays suspended with no published restart date that we could find as of November 6.

Is the CMMC Phase 2 suspended Reddit rumor true?

Yes, the core claim is true, and it traces to the July 13, 2026 Department release. The misleading version is "CMMC is canceled." Level 1 and Level 2 (Self) stay active, so skip the forum summary and read your solicitation.

Does a CMMC phase II suspension change existing contracts?

Not automatically. Contracting officers are told to remove suspended requirements by modification before the next option period or scheduled administrative modification, and existing terms bind you until then. Ask your contracting officer for the timing in writing, and keep complying in the meantime.

Where do I confirm a DoD CMMC Phase 2 suspension notice?

Start with the DoW CIO CMMC page and the DARS class deviation list under Part 240. Then read 32 CFR 170.3 knowing that its printed phases don't reflect the memo. For a specific bid, the amendments on SAM.gov or a written contracting officer answer are the proof to save in your file.
