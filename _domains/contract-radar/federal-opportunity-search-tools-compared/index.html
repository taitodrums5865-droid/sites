GovWin IQ and HigherGov solve the same core problem, finding federal work before your competitors do, but they are bought very differently. HigherGov publishes its prices: $500 a year for one user and $2,500 a year for up to 10 users (HigherGov pricing). Deltek's GovWin IQ is sold in Essentials, Professional and Advanced packages, and you only get a price through a demo request (Deltek GovWin IQ subscriptions). Before you pay for either one, remember that SAM.gov is free. FAR 5.101 requires most proposed contract actions expected to exceed $25,000 to be synopsized there (FAR 5.101), with exceptions listed in FAR 5.202. So a paid tool is worth buying only for what it adds on top: forecasts, award history and agency intel.

What SAM.gov gives you for free

SAM.gov gives you the official list of federal contract notices, free search and free saved-search alerts. You get it all at no cost. FAR 5.101 requires contracting officers to synopsize proposed contract actions "expected to exceed $25,000, by synopsizing in the GPE" (the Governmentwide point of entry, SAM.gov) (FAR 5.101). FAR 5.202 lists cases where that notice is not required.

Here is what the official page confirms (SAM.gov Contract Opportunities):

  • "Anyone may search contract opportunities without an account."
  • With a free account you can save searches, follow changes to opportunities and join interested vendor lists.
  • A "Federal Agency Business Forecasts" link sits under Forecasting.
  • You can download data files and use a public Contract Opportunities API (GSA Opportunities API docs).

SAM.gov notices are not the whole market. FAR 5.202 lets contracting officers skip the synopsis in listed cases, including orders under FAR subpart 16.5 and actions under an existing contract that was already adequately synopsized.

What paid tools add

Paid tools say they add three things, based on their own pages cited below: earlier signals (forecasts and recompetes), award history tied to each opportunity, and agency and competitor profiles. Some also pull in sources other than SAM.gov.

  • Forecasts and early signals. HigherGov's docs describe a Forecasts tab with "forecasts from 40+ sources" (HigherGov docs). GovWin IQ Essentials includes "Analyst-Tracked Opportunities" and the DHS Acquisition Planning Forecast System (Deltek packages).
  • Award history and recompetes. HigherGov has "Potential Recompete," "Vulnerable 8(a)" and "Vulnerable Small Business" filters on contract awards (HigherGov docs). GovSpend's federal product, Fedmine, offers contract search with data back to 2004 and expiring-contract analysis on GWACs and IDIQs (Fedmine platform).
  • Agency intel. In GovWin IQ, Agency Profiles start at the Professional package, and Org Charts and Vendor Program Managers are Advanced-only (Deltek packages).
  • Extra sources. Besides SAM.gov, HigherGov's Opportunities tab lists SBIR.gov, NECO, DIBBS (> $25,000), NIH eBidBoard, JCCS, FedConnect and Unison (HigherGov docs).

Vendor pages describe coverage in their own words, not as audited figures. The trial method below lets you check any coverage claim against your own NAICS code.

Three different jobs, not one

Opportunity search, capture and pipeline management, and proposal writing are three different jobs. Opportunity search finds the notice. Capture management tracks a pursuit for months: who the incumbent is, who to call, and whether to bid. Proposal writing produces the response. Some search tools also list proposal features. For example, GovWin Essentials lists "Proposal Outlines" and "Compliance Matrix" (Deltek). Judge each tool mainly on the first job, though. A dedicated proposal tool is a separate purchase decision.

How each tool handles set-asides, according to its official documentation:

  • HigherGov: go to Business Development → Opportunities → Contracts. Pick Has Set Aside under Quick search, or Set Aside under Search All and choose your categories. Then add NAICS, PSC or Agency (HigherGov docs). The Vulnerable 8(a) filter finds contracts held by 8(a) firms that are nearing graduation.
  • GovSpend / Fedmine: vendor profiles show set-aside contracts, and subcontract data can be filtered by socioeconomic status (Fedmine).
  • GovWin IQ: the package page reviewed for this guide doesn't spell out set-aside filter labels. Ask about them in the demo.

A common mistake is filtering only for your own certification. HZC and SBA are separate set-aside codes (GSA API docs), so a search filtered to HZC alone does not return notices coded SBA (total small business). If you qualify as small under the NAICS code, run one search for your program and a second for SBA and SBP. Also confirm your size under the solicitation's NAICS code using the SBA size standards table.

Free sources: USAspending, SAM.gov saved searches

SAM.gov lists live notices and USAspending.gov shows past awards. Both are free to use.

Set up a SAM.gov saved search:

  1. Sign in at sam.gov. You need an account to save searches.
  2. Open Contract Opportunities search and enter your six-digit NAICS code under the product and service filters.
  3. Add the Set Aside filter for your program, and repeat the search for total small business.
  4. Keep the results to active notices, then save the search and turn on email notifications for it.
  5. On any notice you care about, use Follow so you hear about amendments.

SAM.gov's menu labels change from time to time. If a label doesn't match, use the "How to use Search for contract opportunities" article on SAM.gov Help.

Read award history on USAspending.gov: open USAspending.gov search, limit results to contracts, your NAICS code and recent fiscal years, and review the recipients and agencies. Filter names on the page may change, so use the site's own help if a label differs.

Forecasts for free: acquisition.gov keeps a list of agency procurement forecast links (Agency Recurring Procurement Forecasts), and SAM.gov links to "Federal Agency Business Forecasts." A forecast is a plan, not a promise to buy. When a forecast turns into a sources-sought notice, use the sources sought response template.

How to trial two tools on the same NAICS code

The fair way to compare tools is to run both trials during the same two weeks, with the same NAICS code and the same set-aside filters, and to score them against a free SAM.gov baseline.

  1. Build the baseline first. In SAM.gov, save the search for your NAICS code plus your set-aside and SBA. Write down the active notices it returns.
  2. Pull award history. On USAspending.gov, list the top recipients and agencies in your code for the last three fiscal years.
  3. Start both trials in the same week. HigherGov doesn't state a trial length (HigherGov pricing), so ask when you sign up. Quote-only tools need a demo first, so book it before the trial clock starts.
  4. Use identical filters in each tool: same NAICS, set-aside, place of performance and date range.
  5. Score five things in a spreadsheet:
  6. notices you can bid on that are not in your SAM.gov baseline
  7. forecasts with an expected date
  8. incumbents and expiration dates for recompetes
  9. whether set-aside labels match SAM.gov's codes
  10. export limits and how alerts arrive
  11. Verify every "extra" result at its source. Open the original notice. If it has closed, sits on a contract vehicle you don't hold, or falls outside your size standard, it doesn't count.
  12. Decide on one rule. Pay only if the tool surfaced work you could realistically bid on, or recompete intel you couldn't get from USAspending in reasonable time.

Common mistakes and fixes:

  • Testing two tools in different months. The notices change. Run the trials side by side.
  • Counting forecasts as opportunities. Keep them in their own column.
  • Letting a trial auto-renew. Put the end date in your calendar the day you sign up.
  • Comparing a vendor's top package demo with another vendor's entry plan. Ask which package the demo shows.

Who should pay and who should wait

Wait if you aren't yet active in SAM.gov, haven't submitted a bid, or work in one NAICS code where SAM.gov returns a manageable number of notices. Pay when you spend more time hunting for opportunities and incumbents than writing responses.

  • Wait: you're still registering, still drafting your capability statement, or bidding fewer than a few times a quarter. The free stack is enough for now.
  • A published-price plan makes sense: you bid regularly, track recompetes, and want a fixed annual cost. HigherGov Starter at $500/year (HigherGov pricing) fits this case (as of November 2026; check the official page).
  • A quote-only platform makes sense: you run capture on large programs and need Analyst-Tracked Opportunities or Org Charts (GovWin IQ) or SLED purchasing data (GovSpend).

None of these tools makes you eligible for a set-aside or wins the work for you.

Next steps

Do these today:

  1. Save one SAM.gov search: your NAICS code plus your set-aside, and a second one for total small business.
  2. Pull three years of awards for that NAICS code on USAspending.gov and note the top five recipients.
  3. Check your size under that code before you bid (size standards).

If you'd rather not do it yourself, the official routes are:

  • Self-serve paid plans: HigherGov Starter is $500/year (pricing). It suits solo bidders or small teams that will log in weekly. It doesn't suit someone who bids once a year.
  • Vendor-led onboarding: HigherGov Standard includes a dedicated success manager and unlimited training, and GovWin IQ lists Research Support in every package (Deltek). They suit teams that want guided setup. They don't suit anyone who needs to see a price before a sales call (GovWin is quote only).

FAQ

Is GovWin better than HigherGov for small businesses?

This guide doesn't rank them because they fit different buyers. HigherGov publishes plans from $500/year (HigherGov pricing), while GovWin IQ is quote only and puts features like Agency Profiles in higher packages. If you need a known annual cost, start with HigherGov's trial. If you run large capture efforts, ask Deltek for a demo.

Does GovWin IQ have a free trial?

Deltek's page has a "Try GovWin IQ Risk Free" section, but it is a contact form that leads to a demo request (Deltek). No self-serve trial terms are published. Ask the sales representative directly.

Can I find set-aside contracts on SAM.gov for free?

Yes. Search is free without an account, and a free account lets you save searches and get notifications (SAM.gov). Filter by set-aside type, and run a separate search for total small business set-asides.

How much does GovSpend cost?

GovSpend doesn't publish prices. Its site offers a demo request instead (GovSpend). Ask for a written quote that names the product (GovSpend or Fedmine), the seat count and the renewal terms.

Is USAspending.gov enough for competitor research?

It's enough to see who won past contracts in your NAICS code and at which agencies, and it is free. It doesn't show open solicitations or link forecasts to incumbents. In this guide's view, the main thing a paid tool adds here is time saved on that link.
