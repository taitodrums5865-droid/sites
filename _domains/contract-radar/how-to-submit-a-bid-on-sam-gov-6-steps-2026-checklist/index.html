To submit a bid on SAM.gov, you usually don't upload anything to SAM.gov. You find the notice there, open the solicitation, and send your offer the way it says: by email, an agency portal, GSA eBuy, DoD's PIEE Solicitation Module, or another agency system. FAR 52.212-1(b) tells offerors to "submit signed and dated offers to the office specified in this solicitation" (acquisition.gov, FAR 52.212-1). GSA's guidance agrees: the solicitation contains the instructions for submitting your offer (GSA, Respond to a solicitation).

Key points

  • SAM.gov posts notices; the solicitation says where to send the offer
  • FAR requires SAM registration at offer time and at award
  • Late electronic offers are usually rejected; plan to submit a day early

Best for: first-time U.S. small business bidders

How to Submit a Bid Through SAM.gov (and Where You Actually Submit It)

You usually can't submit a bid directly on SAM.gov. Contract Opportunities is where agencies post notices, and the offer goes wherever the solicitation tells you to send it.

SAM.gov's Contract Opportunities page lists the notice types agencies post: pre-solicitation, solicitation, award, and sole source notices (SAM.gov, Contract Opportunities). An account also lets you join an Interested Vendors List. That list is a networking feature, not a submission, so being on it doesn't mean you have bid.

These are the channels you'll meet most often:

In PIEE, the offer has to be submitted by a user with the Proposal Manager role. "Proposal View Only" users can see offers but can't submit them (PIEE Solicitation Module vendor access instructions). If you request that role the week of the deadline, you're taking a risk, so request it as soon as you start targeting DoD work.

How to Find Bids on SAM.gov

Search Contract Opportunities on SAM.gov without logging in. Filter by notice type, NAICS code, set-aside, place of performance, and response date. "Anyone may search contract opportunities without an account," and a free account adds saved searches and following (SAM.gov, Contract Opportunities).

These are the steps as of November 2026. SAM.gov sometimes renames filters, so look for the closest label if one has moved.

  1. Go to SAM.gov and choose Search. Set the domain to Contract Opportunities.
  2. Under Status, keep Active checked so expired notices drop out.
  3. Under Notice Type, pick Solicitation and Combined Synopsis/Solicitation if you want things you can bid on now. Add Sources Sought and Presolicitation if you want early signals you can respond to before the RFP comes out.
  4. Under the product and service filters, enter your 6-digit NAICS code. If you aren't sure which codes fit, the NAICS-for-government-contracts guide on this site covers picking 3 to 5.
  5. Under Set Aside, choose the programs you qualify for, such as Total Small Business or 8(a).
  6. Narrow by Place of Performance and by response date.
  7. Sign in with Login.gov, then save the search and use Follow on any notice you might bid. Following is how you'll hear about amendments.

For example, a janitorial firm in Ohio might set NAICS 561720, notice type Solicitation, set-aside Total Small Business, and place of performance Ohio. That's an example search, not a recommendation for your firm.

One practical point: third-party write-ups say saved-search emails arrive in batches on SAM.gov's schedule, not in real time (Visualping blog). I couldn't confirm that on an official SAM.gov page. Either way, check short-deadline searches by hand. If you want paid search tools, the opportunity search tools comparison covers GovWin, HigherGov, and GovSpend. Those tools suit firms that bid often. For a firm testing one or two bids, the free SAM.gov search is usually enough.

How to Bid on Federal Government Contracts: 6 Steps for Small Firms

The full sequence has six steps. Register, confirm your eligibility, find a matching notice, read the solicitation, submit through the stated channel, then track the result.

StepWhat you doWhere it's covered
1. RegisterGet a UEI and an active SAM.gov entity registrationSection on registration below
2. Confirm eligibilityCheck the size standard for the NAICS code and your set-aside statusSet-aside section below
3. Find a noticeSearch Contract OpportunitiesPrevious section
4. Read the solicitationPull out the channel, deadline, format, and evaluation factorsSubmission instructions section
5. SubmitSend it through the stated channel a day earlyChecklist section
6. TrackWatch for amendments and award notices, and study past awardsLast body section

Two federal numbers help explain why small firms see so many set-asides. Since October 1, 2025, the micro-purchase threshold has been $15,000 and the simplified acquisition threshold (SAT) $350,000 (Federal Register, Aug 27, 2025). Buys between those two figures are generally set aside for small business when the contracting officer expects offers from at least two small firms (FAR 19.502-2(a)).

Most solicitations over the SAT must allow at least 30 days to respond (FAR 5.203). For commercial buys, the contracting officer only has to set a "reasonable" response time. Response windows on commercial RFQs can therefore be much shorter. Have your registration and capability documents ready before you find the notice. For a cost-focused version of this process, see How to Bid on Government Contracts: 7 Steps, 2026 Costs.

Do I Need a SAM.gov Registration to Bid on a Government Contract?

Yes, in most cases. You can browse notices without registering, but FAR 52.204-7 generally requires you to be registered in SAM when you submit the offer and again at award. A November 2024 FAR rule clarified this: a registration that lapses after you submit and is fixed before award doesn't make you ineligible (Federal Register, Nov 12, 2024).

Timing matters more than anything else here:

  • Registration is free. NSF says it "takes only 10-15 minutes to initiate but can take up to one month to complete" (NSF SBIR/STTR, Unique Entity ID). Free help is available from the Federal Service Desk and from APEX Accelerators. If anyone charges a fee just to register you, that service is optional, not required.
  • You need a Login.gov-based SAM.gov account. You "must renew your registration every 365 days to keep it active" (SAM.gov, Entity Registration).
  • A Unique Entity ID by itself only needs your legal business name and physical address, and it is not a full registration (SAM.gov, Entity Registration). Prime contract offers expect the full registration.
  • Delays often come from TIN or CAGE validation problems and from information that is incomplete or inaccurate (NSF). If yours is stuck, see the SAM.gov registration guide.

A note on the FAR overhaul: under the Revolutionary FAR Overhaul (RFO), agencies are applying a rewritten FAR Part 4 through class deviations. One example is DHS Class Deviation 25-19, Revision 1, effective November 2025, which moves the SAM exceptions list into 52.204-7 (DHS deviation). FAR Case 2026-001 also proposes more changes to SAM representations (Sheppard Mullin summary). Whether that rule is final as of November 2026 is unconfirmed. Read the 52.204-7 version attached to your own solicitation, because that's the version that applies to you.

How to Read the Solicitation's Submission Instructions (Email, Portal, eBuy, or Agency System)

The submission instructions are in the solicitation itself. Check the notice description, the attachments, and the instructions to offerors. That means Section L in a Uniform Contract Format RFP, or the 52.212-1 addendum in a commercial RFQ.

Here's where each item usually lives:

How to Read the Solicitation's Submission Instructions (Email, Portal, eBuy, or Agency System)
What you needWhere to lookRule if the solicitation is silent
Submission channel and addressSection L, the 52.212-1 addendum, or the notice descriptionYou still must deliver to "the office specified" (52.212-1(b))
Due date and time zoneThe notice's "Response Date" plus the solicitation form4:30 p.m. local time at the designated office (52.212-1(f)(1); FAR 15.208(a))
Questions deadlineSection L or the addendumNone by default. Ask early
Format (page limits, file types, volumes)Section L or the addendumFollow anything stated. Missing it can make your offer non-compliant
How you'll be scoredSection M, or 52.212-2 for commercial buysLowest price isn't always the winner. GSA notes that cost is often not the most important factor (GSA)
Who to contactThe notice's primary point of contactSend questions only to the named POC
AmendmentsThe notice's attachments and historyYou usually have to acknowledge each one

Not sure whether you're answering an RFQ or an RFP? The two are treated differently. See RFQ vs RFP: 5 FAR Clues and What to Submit.

Take an example sentence from an instructions section: "Quotes shall be emailed to the Contract Specialist no later than 2:00 p.m. Eastern on the response date; files may not exceed 10 MB." That one line gives you the channel (email), the deadline (2:00 p.m. ET, not your local time if you're in another zone), and a format limit. The text is an example, not from a real notice.

Watch out for the "late is late" rule. Under FAR 52.212-1(f) and FAR 15.208(b), a late electronic offer can be considered only in narrow cases. The main one is when it reached the government's "initial point of entry" by 5:00 p.m. one working day before the deadline, and the contracting officer agrees that considering it won't unduly delay the buy. Sealed bids follow the same rule under FAR 14.304. In practice, an email that hits the agency server at 2:05 p.m. for a 2:00 p.m. deadline is almost always late. Plan to submit the business day before the due date. That day is also the cutoff the rule protects.

How to Bid on a Government Contract: Pre-Submission Checklist for First-Time Bidders

Before you hit send, check four things. Your registration is active, you meet the set-aside and size standard, every required form and price item is complete, and the offer will arrive before the deadline.

Copy this checklist:

  1. Log in to SAM.gov and confirm your entity status shows Active, with an expiration date after the expected award date. (52.204-7 requires registration at offer and award.)
  2. Confirm the solicitation's NAICS code and its size standard. Then use SBA's Size Standards Tool to check that your average employees (over 24 months) or receipts (over five years, including affiliates) fall under it (SBA, Size standards).
  3. Confirm your set-aside status matches the notice. For WOSB, HUBZone, 8(a), or SDVOSB, check that your certification is current.
  4. Fill out the cover form the solicitation uses (often SF 1449 for commercial buys) and sign it. Unsigned offers draw questions. 52.212-1(b) asks for "signed and dated offers" that show the solicitation number and your name and address.
  5. Complete the representations and certifications in FAR 52.212-3. Many are pulled from your SAM annual reps, but the solicitation may ask for specific paragraphs. Read which ones.
  6. Fill every line item in the price schedule. Leave no blank cells. If a line is no charge, write "$0" or "NC" as the instructions allow.
  7. Sign and return each amendment acknowledgment.
  8. Check page limits, file names, file types, and the email size cap against Section L or the addendum.
  9. Re-read the evaluation factors and make sure each one has a clearly labeled answer in your response.
  10. Submit by close of business the working day before the deadline. Then save your sent email, portal receipt, or PIEE confirmation. The PIEE Offer tab shows a time and date stamp, and you get an email confirmation once you submit (NAVSUP e-Business briefing).

If you find a mistake after submitting, the rules allow modifications or withdrawals before the deadline (FAR 15.208). In eBuy, sellers can revise a quote before the RFQ closes (eBuy Contractor User Guide). Send a modification. Don't send a second full offer with no explanation.

The opportunities you can realistically win are set-asides where your firm is small under the stated NAICS code and holds any required certification. Check both before you write a single page.

How the "rule of two" plays out: above the SAT, the codified FAR text says the contracting officer must set a buy aside when there's a reasonable expectation of offers from at least two capable small businesses at fair market prices (FAR 19.502-2(b), FAC 2026-01, effective March 13, 2026). Between $15,000 and $350,000, small business set-asides are the default unless that expectation is missing. Under the RFO, agencies are applying a rewritten Part 19 through class deviations, and commentators disagree about how the rule of two applies to orders under multiple-award contracts (Holland & Knight). Check your agency's deviation if a task order matters to you.

Size has its own traps. The size standard that counts is the one for the NAICS code the contracting officer assigned, not the code you list most often. If you think the assigned code is wrong, you can appeal to SBA's Office of Hearings and Appeals within 10 days of the solicitation's publication (SBA, Size standards). Thresholds by industry are in SBA Size Standards by NAICS.

How to Submit a Bid: What Happens After You Submit and How to Follow an Award on SAM.gov

After you submit, watch the notice for amendments and award information. Then use SAM.gov Contract Awards and USAspending.gov to see who won and at what price. Timing and level of detail vary by agency.

  1. Keep following the notice on SAM.gov. Amendments, including extended deadlines, show up on the notice.
  2. If you lose, you're usually told so. For negotiated buys under FAR Part 15, the rules on notices and debriefings are in FAR 15.5. Ask for a debriefing within the time the notice gives you.
  3. Look for an Award Notice. Agencies must post award synopses for contracts over $25,000 that are covered by a trade agreement or likely to produce subcontracts. For trade-agreement buys, the deadline is 60 days after award (FAR 5.301). Small awards outside those rules may never get an award notice.
  4. In SAM.gov, sign in, choose the Contracting domain, and select Contract Awards. You can filter by agency, entity, place of performance, dates, and competition (SAM.gov, Contract Award Data). You need a free user account for this data.
  5. On USAspending.gov, go to Award Search > Advanced Search. Set the award type to contracts, add your NAICS code and the agency, and review past winners and award amounts (USAspending Federal Spending Guide).

For example, if you lost a custodial RFQ at a VA medical center, search awards under that NAICS code at that agency over the last few years. You'll see which firms win repeatedly and the range of award values. Use what you find to adjust your pricing and your teaming strategy. Award totals include options and modifications, though, so don't read a total as an annual price. If you keep losing to the same primes, subcontracting may be a faster route.

Next steps

Three things you can do yourself today:

  1. Log in to SAM.gov and check your entity status and expiration date. If you aren't registered, start now, because it can take up to a month.
  2. Build one saved search in Contract Opportunities: your top NAICS code, an active set-aside you qualify for, and Solicitation plus Combined Synopsis/Solicitation as notice types. Then follow two notices.
  3. Open one live solicitation and fill in the "where to look" table above for it. Note the channel, the deadline and time zone, the format limits, the questions deadline, and the evaluation factors. Even if you don't bid, this is the skill that keeps first offers from being rejected.

If you'd rather not do it alone:

  • APEX Accelerator (free for most services). DoD funds this network, which operates 90+ Accelerators in about 300 offices (DoD APEX one-pager, July 2026). They help with registration, readiness checks, and finding bids. It's a good fit for first-time bidders on a tight budget. It's a poor fit if you need someone to write your proposal for you: they advise, they don't write. Some offices only serve certain counties, as Wayne State's does (Wayne State APEX).
  • SBA resources. SBA lists free contracting help and 2026 APEX webinars (SBA, Federal contracting assistance). It suits owners who want to learn the process themselves.
  • Paid proposal writers or consultants. They're priced by quote, and I couldn't find an official standard rate to cite. They suit firms with a specific large bid and no time to write it. If you're still testing whether federal work fits your business, they usually aren't worth it. Check credentials and never pay anyone just to "register" you, since SAM.gov registration is free.
  • Paid search and capture tools. Compare options and limits in the opportunity search tools comparison (prices change; check each vendor's page, as of November 2026). These tools suit firms bidding every week. If you're placing one or two test bids, they mostly add cost.

FAQ

How do I find federal bids that match my business on SAM.gov?

Search Contract Opportunities without logging in. Filter by your NAICS codes, the set-asides you qualify for, notice type, and place of performance. Sign in with Login.gov to save the search and follow notices so you see amendments.

Is it free to bid on federal government contracts?

SAM.gov registration is free, and so is searching. Submitting an offer has no government fee. What costs you is the time spent writing proposals, plus any optional tools or consultants you choose. Some certifications and outside services have their own costs, so check each program's official page.

Can I bid on a government contract without a SAM.gov registration?

For most federal prime contracts, no. FAR 52.204-7 generally requires you to be registered when you submit the offer and at award. There are exceptions, and the RFO deviations moved the exceptions list into 52.204-7 itself, so check the version in your solicitation.

What happens if I submit my bid after the deadline?

It's normally not considered. FAR 15.208, 14.304, and 52.212-1(f) allow narrow exceptions. The main one is an electronic offer that reached the government's initial point of entry by 5:00 p.m. one working day before the deadline. Even then, the contracting officer has to decide that accepting it won't unduly delay the buy. Submit a day early.

How long does SAM.gov registration take before I can be awarded a contract?

NSF says starting a registration takes 10 to 15 minutes, but finishing it can take up to one month, and longer if your information is incomplete or validation fails. Start well before you plan to bid, and renew every 365 days.
