SAM.gov entity validation is the free check that confirms your business's legal name, physical address and IRS tax ID match official records. Your registration can't go Active until it passes. Most delays come from one of three problems: a name or address that doesn't match your documents character for character, an EIN that's too new or doesn't match IRS records, or a document upload sent to the wrong place. Each one has a specific fix, and the faster you apply it, the more of your bid deadline you keep.

Key points

  • New EINs can take up to 2 weeks to pass IRS TIN matching
  • Attach validation documents in SAM.gov, not an FSD.gov ticket
  • Registration is free; renew every 365 days to stay Active

Best for: first-time federal bidders and small business owners

What is SAM.gov entity validation and what does the Federal Service Desk check?

Entity validation is the free step where the GSA Federal Service Desk (FSD) confirms your business is who it says it is before your SAM.gov registration can become Active. It checks your legal business name and physical address, your IRS TIN/EIN data, and, when requested, supporting documents.

Two checks run during a typical registration, and they come from different places:

A federal grant FAQ, a secondary source rather than SAM.gov itself, also describes a CAGE validation step that keeps a registration in Submitted until it finishes (agency NOFO FAQ).

GSA says the documents you submit must prove your legal business name and physical address (GSA bulletin). In a 2022 stakeholder forum, GSA also said that bank statements and utility bills must be less than five years old and that document review "may take up to a few weeks" depending on workload (GSA Stakeholder Forum, Aug 3, 2022).

Things to watch for:

  • An existing Active registration usually isn't re-validated mid-year. GSA says name and address validation happens during your annual renewal, and it advises against making updates you don't need (GSA).
  • A CAGE code is a separate identifier from the UEI. If you aren't sure whether you already have one, see CAGE Code Lookup: 4 Steps to Find or Request One.

How to register an entity in SAM.gov (step by step)

Create a SAM.gov login, start Register Entity to get your Unique Entity ID (UEI), complete and submit the registration, then wait for FSD validation and IRS TIN confirmation. Each step below lists the point where it most often stalls.

Before you start: gather your EIN confirmation (CP 575 or Letter 147C), your state formation document, your bank's routing and account numbers for payments, and your NAICS codes. SAM.gov links to an Entity Registration Checklist on its registration page.

  1. Sign in through Login.gov. SAM.gov uses Login.gov for usernames and passwords, and then you complete your SAM.gov profile (SAM.gov).

Where it stalls: using a personal email you'll lose access to. Use a durable work address, because that address becomes how the government reaches you.

  1. Choose full registration, not "UEI only." SAM.gov offers a UEI-only option that needs just your legal business name and physical address, but it does not let you apply directly for federal awards (SAM.gov). Bidders need the full entity registration.

Where it stalls: people pick UEI-only, then can't figure out why they can't bid.

  1. Enter entity information and pass validation. On the Enter Entity Information screen, type your legal business name and physical address exactly as they appear on your documents. If SAM.gov can't match them automatically, it prompts you to upload documents through the Create Incident button on the validation page (GSA).

Where it stalls: a missing "LLC," an extra comma, or a dropped suite number.

  1. Complete the core data, assertions, reps and certs, and points of contact, then submit. This is where your NAICS codes and small business size representations go. Check them against SBA size standards by NAICS before you submit.

Where it stalls: leaving the draft unfinished. A Bureau of Reclamation grant FAQ, not SAM.gov itself, says drafts in Work in Progress are held for up to 90 days and then removed (Bureau of Reclamation NOFO FAQ). Treat that as supplementary information and don't count on having the full 90 days.

  1. Wait for validation, then confirm Active status. While checks run, the record shows Submitted. Use SAM.gov's Check Entity Status tool to track progress (SAM.gov). A federal grant FAQ (secondary source) says the record moves from Submitted to Active Registration once TIN and CAGE validation finish (agency NOFO FAQ).

Where it stalls: an IRS mismatch. Watch your inbox, including spam, for the validation email.

What documents are needed for SAM.gov entity validation?

FSD needs proof that your legal business name and physical address match what you entered in SAM.gov. Usually that's a formation record, a business license, or a tax or bank document, depending on your entity type. Always follow the exact request in your ticket.

Vendor summaries of FSD guidance say P.O. boxes, screenshots, company letterhead, notary or administrator letters, and USPS.com address lookups are not accepted as validation documents (FedFinder, a private vendor; USFCR). Treat those lists as secondary sources and confirm them against KB0055230.

Example (illustrative): Say you entered "Ridgeline Facility Services LLC, 410 Main St Ste 200, Dayton OH." A clean submission would be your Ohio articles of organization showing "Ridgeline Facility Services LLC," plus a 2026 utility bill addressed to that exact name at Suite 200. One document proves the name. The other proves name and address together.

Common mistakes:

  • Uploading to the wrong place. GSA says not to attach validation documents to a regular FSD.gov ticket. Use Create Incident in SAM.gov (GSA).
  • Submitting an incident with no documents. GSA says it will be closed, and you'll have to start a new one (GSA).
  • Opening several incidents. Every duplicate slows the queue (GSA).

How to fix a legal business name or address mismatch

Make the legal business name and physical address in SAM.gov match your source records character for character, including punctuation, suffixes like LLC, and suite numbers. Then resubmit with the document FSD requested.

  1. Pull your official record. Search your state Secretary of State business database and copy the legal name exactly as filed. For example, a state record might show "Acme Builders, L.L.C." while your bank statement says "Acme Builders LLC." Choose one exact form and make every document match it, or send documents that show both forms.
  2. Compare SAM.gov to that record field by field. Check punctuation, "Inc." versus "Incorporated," ampersands, and street abbreviations. One consultancy notes that a single comma or "LLC" versus "L.L.C." can trigger a failure (USFCR).
  3. Fix the physical address. Use a street address, not a P.O. box. If you have no separate office, a home address can work (FedFinder, vendor). Include the suite or unit number exactly as it appears on your utility bill.
  4. Re-enter the data on the Enter Entity Information screen and let the match run again.
  5. If it still fails, click Create Incident and attach one document showing the current legal name and physical address together, plus a formation document if your name changed. State the exact correction you need in the comment. Don't just write "see attached." Then wait on that single incident (see "Common mistakes" above).

Pitfall: if your shared office building has several registrants, the validation service may drop your suite number and treat you as a duplicate. In the incident comment, explain that it's a false duplicate and point to the suite number on your document (USFCR).

Pitfall: if you recently moved or renamed the company, update the state record first. GSA says your documents must prove your legal business name and physical address (GSA), so you need a current official record to upload.

How to fix an IRS TIN/EIN mismatch in SAM.gov

The legal name and TIN you enter must match the IRS record exactly, as shown on your CP 575 notice or Letter 147C. Correct the SAM.gov entry or get the IRS confirmation letter, then resubmit.

  1. Check the EIN's age. The IRS says you can use a new EIN right away for most purposes, but you should wait up to 2 weeks before it can pass a TIN Matching check (IRS). If your EIN is younger than that, a failure may clear on its own. Wait, then resubmit.
  2. Find your IRS name of record. Look at the original CP 575 notice. If you don't have it, the IRS offers three options: request an entity transcript, download a digital CP 575 from your Business Tax Account if you're eligible, or call 800-829-4933 (Monday to Friday, 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time) and ask for Letter 147C, EIN Previously Assigned (IRS).
  3. Match the SAM.gov legal name to the IRS name exactly. For example, if the IRS has "Smith Consulting LLC," don't enter "Smith Consulting, LLC."
  4. Decide which record is wrong. If SAM.gov is wrong, edit it and resubmit. If the IRS record is outdated, for example from a name change never reported to the IRS, fix the IRS record first. Resubmitting SAM.gov won't help until the IRS side is correct.
  5. Resubmit and watch for the follow-up email. SAM.gov emails you instructions if validation fails again (SAM.gov status tracker).

How to get the SAM.gov notarized letter right

Only prepare a notarized letter if FSD asks you for one. The rules have changed over time, so use the current FSD template exactly.

What changed. In spring 2018, after fraud cases, GSA briefly required an original notarized Entity Administrator letter before any new registration could go Active (PilieroMazza). GSA then announced that from June 29, 2018, non-federal entities would no longer need an approved letter on file before activation (WISPRO notice, June 2018). Guides written in that period can still turn up in search, so check the date on anything that says the letter is mandatory for every registration.

When to use it:

  1. Confirm FSD actually asked for it. A notarized letter is not a substitute for name and address documents. Vendor summaries list notary letters among documents FSD won't accept for entity validation (FedFinder).
  2. Download the template from FSD.gov. The required wording is published on the FSD site (PilieroMazza).
  3. Have the new administrator create an individual SAM.gov account first, using the same email address that will appear in the letter (NotaryLive).
  4. Fill it in so it matches SAM.gov exactly: legal business name, physical address, the full UEI, and the administrator's name and email.
  5. Sign in front of a notary, then upload the notarized scan through FSD.gov as FSD instructs.

Why letters get rejected: they leave out the required statement about third-party administration preference (authorization or denial), they contain details that don't match SAM.gov, or the UEI is incomplete (PilieroMazza; NotaryLive).

Is the SAM.gov entity validation service free (and what about paid helpers)?

Yes. Registration and entity validation through SAM.gov and FSD are free, with no fee to register, validate or renew. Third-party firms may offer help for a fee, but you never need to pay one to get validated.

GSA states plainly that "Registration in SAM.gov is always free" and that any email or site asking for money to register, update or renew isn't an official government source (GSA, May 4, 2023). GSA's red flags:

  • Official emails come from a .gov address. Watch for lookalikes such as domains ending in "gov.com."
  • SAM.gov never sends links that don't end in .gov.
  • The government never asks for payment by gift card, cryptocurrency or wire transfer.

To report a scam, use ftc.gov/complaint, or IC3.gov for phishing. If you think there's a real problem with your registration, sign in to SAM.gov directly instead of clicking an email link (GSA).

Paid help isn't a scam by default. It's a convenience. Just know what you're paying for: a paid firm can prepare documents and file tickets, but it can't make FSD review your ticket faster.

How long does SAM.gov entity validation take before a set-aside bid deadline?

A clean registration often finishes in a few business days, but document review and IRS fixes can take weeks. Start at least 4 to 6 weeks before the bid due date and plan backward from it.

The deadline that matters is legal, not practical. FAR 52.204-7(b)(1) requires an offeror to be registered in SAM "when submitting an offer or quotation and at time of award" (Acquisition.gov, FAR 52.204-7, Nov 2024). On a small business set-aside, the registration also carries the size representations the contracting officer relies on. See Total vs Partial Small Business Set Aside for how set-aside type affects who can bid.

Official numbers to plan around (as of November 2026; check the official pages):

  • Up to 2 weeks for a new EIN to pass TIN matching (IRS)
  • Up to 3 business days average processing, and up to 10 business days for external reviews (SAM.gov)
  • Up to a few weeks for document review, depending on workload (GSA forum)

Next steps

Do this today:

  1. Sign in to SAM.gov and check your status. If you see "Work in Progress," finish and submit it this week.
  2. Put your SAM.gov legal name and address next to your CP 575/147C and your state business record, and fix any character that differs.
  3. If you're already in validation, confirm your open incident has a matching document attached.

If you'd rather not do it yourself:

  • APEX Accelerators (free). They offer free, hands-on help with SAM registration (SBIR.gov). Best for first-time bidders who want someone to review documents. Not ideal if you need same-day help, since counselors set their own schedules.
  • FSD (free). Best for technical problems and stuck tickets (contact details are in the status table above). FSD won't prepare your documents or review your bid strategy.
  • Paid registration consultants such as US Federal Contractor Registration. Fees vary, so get a written quote (as of November 2026; check the official page). This can suit busy owners who want someone else to do the paperwork. It doesn't suit tight budgets, and as noted above, it won't get your ticket reviewed faster.

FAQ

Do I need a UEI before entity validation can start?

No. You get the UEI by passing validation. SAM.gov assigns it automatically once your legal business name and physical address are validated, whether you choose the UEI-only option or full registration (SAM.gov). For bidding, you still need to finish the full registration after that.

Can I bid on a federal contract while my SAM.gov registration is still Submitted?

The rule is to be registered when you submit the offer. FAR 52.204-7(b)(1) requires registration "when submitting an offer or quotation and at time of award" (Acquisition.gov). A Submitted registration isn't Active yet, so don't count on it. Ask the contracting officer if your deadline is close, and keep working on validation in the meantime.

How often do I have to renew my SAM.gov registration?

Every 365 days. SAM.gov says you must renew within that window to keep your registration active, and you can update it at any time (SAM.gov).
