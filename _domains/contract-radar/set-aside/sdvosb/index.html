What an SDVOSB set-aside is

A service-disabled veteran-owned small business (SDVOSB) set-aside is a federal contract that only SDVOSBs may bid on. At the Department of Veterans Affairs, a contracting officer must reserve a contract this way when they expect offers from at least two capable SDVOSBs at a fair price; other agencies may choose to. This page lists 237 of those notices that are open on SAM.gov today, with the soonest deadline first.

To bid on an SDVOSB set-aside you need SBA certification through VetCert. Self-certification stopped counting for set-asides on January 1, 2024, so a firm that only ticked the box in SAM.gov is no longer eligible. If your certification is pending or has lapsed, sort that out before you spend a week writing a proposal.

Reading the numbers above

The counts come from the public SAM.gov extract we download each morning. Right now 168 of the 237 notices close within 14 days. Department of Veterans Affairs posts the most, at 82% of the list. The Department of Veterans Affairs often leads because of that rule, set out in 38 U.S.C. 8127 and often called Vets First.

A notice is not always a contract you can win straight away. Sources sought notices and presolicitations are market research. Answering one costs little, and a short, specific capability statement can be the reason a future solicitation becomes a set-aside at all. Combined synopsis/solicitations and solicitations are the ones that take offers.

Before you bid

Check three things on every notice. First, the NAICS code and its size standard: you must be small under that code, not just in general. Second, the place of performance, because a contract three states away changes your price. Third, the limitations on subcontracting. On a set-aside services contract, you generally cannot pay more than half of the contract amount to subcontractors that are not themselves similarly situated. The exact rule is in 13 CFR 125.6.

Deadlines on SAM.gov are in the time zone the notice states, and many are set for the middle of the working day. Count business days, not calendar days, and leave time for the questions period, which usually closes well before the offer deadline. Our bid deadline calculator skips federal holidays.

How this list is built

We keep only notices that are still open and marked as SDVOSB set-asides in the SAM.gov data, and we drop notices once their response date passes. The agency, NAICS and state tables link to narrower lists. We are not part of SAM.gov or the government, and the official notice always wins if it disagrees with us.
