To meet WOSB certification requirements, your business must be small under SBA's size standard for its primary NAICS code. It must also be at least 51% unconditionally and directly owned and controlled by one or more women who are U.S. citizens, and a woman must run day-to-day operations and make long-term decisions (SBA). You can't self-certify anymore. You get certified either free through SBA's MySBA Certifications portal or through one of four SBA-approved third-party certifiers that charge a fee. Either way, your certification has to show up in MySBA Certifications before you can bid on WOSB set-asides.

Key points

  • SBA certification is free; third-party fees run $275 to $1,250
  • EDWOSB: net worth under $850K, AGI ≤ $400K, assets ≤ $6.5M
  • SBA aims to decide in 90 days once your package is complete

Best for: Women business owners applying for WOSB or EDWOSB status for the first time

Which option is right for you?

For most first-time applicants, the free SBA application is the best place to start. Pay a third-party certifier only if you also want a private-sector WBE certificate or a faster review.

What Is WOSB Certification?

WOSB (Women-Owned Small Business) certification is an SBA designation. It lets women-owned businesses compete for the federal contracts set aside under the WOSB Federal Contracting Program. Without it, a contracting officer can't count your firm toward a WOSB set-aside, even if women own 100% of the company.

The program exists to help agencies reach a statutory goal: 5% of federal prime contracting dollars should go to women-owned small businesses (SBA). Agencies often fall short. The Congressional Research Service reports that the 5% prime goal was met in only two of the five years from FY2019 to FY2023, and reached 4.91% in FY2023 (CRS R46322, June 2024). That gap gives contracting officers a reason to look for certified WOSBs.

Certification gives you access to three things:

  • WOSB set-asides. These are competitions limited to certified WOSBs, in NAICS codes where SBA has found women-owned businesses are substantially underrepresented.
  • EDWOSB set-asides. These are limited to economically disadvantaged WOSBs, in industries where women-owned firms are underrepresented.
  • Sole-source awards. Since October 1, 2025, the ceilings are $5.5 million for most contracts and $8.5 million for manufacturing, after the FAR inflation adjustment (Federal Register, Aug. 27, 2025; CRS IF13123).

The industry restriction matters. As of SBA's March 2022 list, 646 of 1,023 six-digit NAICS codes qualified for WOSB set-asides, and another 113 qualified for EDWOSB-only set-asides (CRS R46322). Before you apply, check your primary NAICS code against SBA's current eligible NAICS list. For example, if your code is on neither list, WOSB set-asides in that code won't exist. Certification could still help you in other codes you work in.

SBA WOSB Certification Requirements

To qualify, a business must be at least 51% owned and controlled by one or more women who are U.S. citizens, and it must meet SBA's size standard for its industry. The details in 13 CFR Part 127 are what decide most applications.

Some points tend to surprise applicants.

You don't need the technical license. The woman in charge doesn't have to hold the electrical license or engineering credential herself. She does have to show "ultimate managerial and supervisory control" over the people who do (13 CFR 127.202).

Community property is ignored. If you live in a community property state, SBA disregards your spouse's community interest when it counts ownership (13 CFR 127.201).

Stock options count against you. Unexercised options held by women are ignored. Options held by men are treated as if they had already been exercised, which can push the women's share below 51% (13 CFR 127.201).

Size is set by NAICS code, not by the program. Check your number before you apply. If you're close to the limit, use the method in SBA Size Standards by NAICS.

EDWOSB Certification Requirements

EDWOSB starts with every WOSB ownership and control rule. On top of that, each qualifying woman owner has to pass three personal financial tests set by SBA. If you're certified as an EDWOSB, you're also eligible for WOSB set-asides.

EDWOSB Certification Requirements. Source: SAM.gov
TestLimitWhat's excludedRule
Personal net worthLess than $850,000Your ownership interest in the business, equity in your primary residence, and funds in an IRA or other official retirement account13 CFR 127.203
Adjusted gross income$400,000 or less, averaged over the previous three yearsS-corp, LLC, or partnership distributions that were reinvested in the business or paid only to cover the taxes on that income13 CFR 127.203
Total assetsFair market value of $6.5 million or lessRetirement accounts13 CFR 127.203

(Limits as of October 2026; check the SBA program page.)

Here's an example with illustrative numbers. Your house has $400,000 in equity, your 60% stake in the company is worth $1.2 million, your 401(k) holds $300,000, and you have $250,000 in brokerage and savings with no other debts. Your counted net worth is about $250,000, which is under the limit. Your total assets for the $6.5 million test, without the retirement account, come to roughly $1.85 million. Both tests pass.

Several things trip up EDWOSB applicants:

  • AGI over $400,000 creates a presumption, and you can rebut it. It doesn't automatically disqualify you. If one year spiked because of a one-time pass-through distribution, you can document why it isn't typical (13 CFR 127.203).
  • Assets you recently gave to family can still count. Transfers to relatives within two years before you apply are counted as yours. The exceptions are payments for education, medical care, and customary gifts for special occasions (13 CFR 127.203).
  • SBA may look at your spouse's finances. This applies if your spouse has a role in the business, has given it financial support, or runs a similar business (13 CFR 127.203).
  • You have to keep qualifying. Certified firms go through a program examination every three years (SBA). If your net worth grows past $850,000, plan on losing EDWOSB status while you keep WOSB.

How to Get WOSB Certification

Get an active SAM.gov registration and gather your ownership and control documents. Then apply free through MySBA Certifications at certifications.sba.gov or through an approved third-party certifier. Both paths end in MySBA Certifications.

Path A: free SBA application

  1. Confirm your NAICS code and size. Check your primary code against the size standard and the WOSB eligible NAICS list.
  2. Activate your SAM.gov registration. You need a Unique Entity ID and an active entity registration. The SAM.gov Registration Guide covers each screen. WBENC warns that SAM validation, normally 3–5 business days, has been taking several weeks because of notarization requirements (WBENC).
  3. Make sure SAM matches your governing documents. The legal name, address, and ownership details should agree with your operating agreement or bylaws, because SBA declines applications when the information is inconsistent (13 CFR Part 127, Subpart C).
  4. Gather the documents listed in the table below.
  5. Sign in at certifications.sba.gov with Login.gov. The Login.gov email should be one you'll still be checking in six months.
  6. Pick the program. Choose WOSB, or EDWOSB if you meet the financial tests. Answer the eligibility questions about ownership, control, and citizenship, and upload your documents.
  7. Submit, then watch for requests. SBA tells you whether your package is complete. If it isn't, it tells you what's missing (13 CFR Part 127, Subpart C). Slow replies stall your file.

Path B: third-party certifier

  1. Do steps 1–3 above.
  2. Apply with one of the four SBA-approved certifiers: El Paso Hispanic Chamber of Commerce, National Women Business Owners Corporation (NWBOC), U.S. Women's Chamber of Commerce (USWCC), or WBENC (SBA).
  3. Complete their review. WBENC, for example, does a document review and then a site-visit interview with the woman owner (WBENC).
  4. Upload your proof of citizenship and the certifier's documentation to MySBA Certifications before you bid (SBA). If you skip this step, contracting officers can't verify your status.

Common process mistakes

  • Applying with an expired SAM registration. Fix: renew in SAM first. The registration has to be renewed every year.
  • Uploading unsigned tax returns. SBA's rule asks for signed copies, so re-scan them with signatures.
  • Uploading only the original operating agreement. Fix: include every amendment. An amendment you leave out can change who holds control.
  • Getting third-party certified and then bidding right away. Fix: finish the MySBA Certifications upload first.

How Long Does It Take to Get WOSB Certification?

SBA's rule says it will decide within 90 calendar days of receiving a complete package "whenever practicable." In practice, the clock starts only once SBA considers your file complete. Third-party timelines depend on the certifier.

SBA doesn't publish a live average processing time. Some private guides report waits of 6–8 months for SBA decisions, but those figures don't come from SBA (UnderstandMyCert), so treat 90 days as the best case. For example, if you're aiming at an agency's fourth-quarter buying season (July–September), submitting by the previous winter gives you room for a round of document requests.

If you're declined, you can reapply 90 days after the decline date. You'll need to have fixed every reason SBA gave, and SBA must give those reasons in writing (13 CFR 127.304). A decline costs you at least three months, so it's usually faster to send a complete package the first time.

To keep your certification, plan on a program examination every three years. SBA's annual attestation requirement is currently in abeyance, so you don't have to file an attestation every year (SBA). WBENC recommends submitting recertification 90 days before expiration (WBENC).

Free SBA Certification vs. Third-Party WOSB Certifier: Which Should You Choose?

For most applicants, SBA's free certification is the better pick: it costs nothing and it's what federal set-asides require. Third-party certifiers cost money and add a reviewer who answers to you. A WBE certificate also helps with corporate supplier programs. Self-certification ended on October 15, 2020, so none of these options is "self-certifying" (CRS R46322).

Decision tree

  1. Do corporate customers ask you for a WBE certificate? If yes, go with WBENC, or NWBOC's combined package, so one review produces both. If no, go to question 2.
  2. Do you qualify for EDWOSB? If yes, use SBA (free) or USWCC/NWBOC. All of them handle EDWOSB. If no, go to question 3.
  3. Are your operating agreement or bylaws clean, with no supermajority or unanimous-consent clauses favoring men and no outside job for the woman CEO? If yes, use SBA's free path. You'd be paying a fee for a review your documents don't need. If you aren't sure, fix the documents first (see the next section). A paid certifier reviews against the same Part 127 rules and can't approve documents that fail them.

No certifier can lower your risk of rejection by bending the rules. What a paid reviewer adds is structured follow-up. Your eligibility depends only on what your documents say.

Ownership and Control Documentation SBA Rejects Most Often

SBA declines applications over control clauses in operating agreements and bylaws, men who hold financial or signing power, and ownership percentages that don't match equity records. SBA doesn't publish decline statistics. The items below come from the specific rules applications are judged against in 13 CFR 127.201–127.202, along with the rule that inconsistent information leads to a decline.

1. Supermajority and unanimous-consent clauses

An LLC agreement might require approval from 75% of members or a unanimous vote to borrow money, sign contracts, or hire staff. If women own 60%, that clause gives the 40% male member veto power. Women must be the managing members "with control over all decisions" (13 CFR 127.202). The fix is to amend the agreement so ordinary and major business decisions need only a majority the women control. Keep the minority-protection votes, such as approving a sale of the whole company or a dissolution, limited to what's commercially standard.

2. A man holds the highest title or the real authority

Here's an example. The woman owner is "President," but her husband is "CEO," holds the line of credit, and is the only signer on the bank account. The rule requires that a woman hold the highest officer position and run day-to-day operations (13 CFR 127.202). To fix it, change the officer titles, add her as the primary signer on bank and credit accounts, and make sure board minutes or resolutions reflect the change.

3. The woman in charge has another full-time job

A W-2 from another employer in her personal tax return undercuts the requirement that she generally work full time during normal hours (13 CFR 127.202). Resolve it before you apply, or be ready to explain any part-time outside role in detail.

4. Ownership percentages that don't match across documents

The operating agreement says 51%, the K-1s show 50/50, and the SAM profile lists two equal owners. SBA declines applications when it can't determine eligibility because of inconsistent information (13 CFR Part 127, Subpart C). Put the stock ledger, operating agreement, K-1s, tax returns, and SAM record side by side, and correct the one that's wrong before you apply. Don't explain the mismatch afterward.

5. Indirect or conditional ownership

The usual problems are ownership held through a holding company, a trust where the woman isn't grantor, trustee, and sole beneficiary, a buy-sell agreement that triggers on a contingency, and voting rights assigned to someone else. Each one breaks the "unconditional and direct" requirement (13 CFR 127.201). The fix is to move the shares into the woman's own name and strip any conditions that don't follow normal commercial terms.

6. Distributions or pay that favor men

If distributions aren't at least 51% to the women, or a male employee earns more than the woman CEO without a justification, SBA reads the arrangement as control sitting elsewhere (13 CFR 127.201; 13 CFR 127.202). If a higher salary has a legitimate reason, such as a licensed professional's market rate, write that reason down.

7. Options and the stock ledger

Options held by men count as already exercised (13 CFR 127.201). For example, a woman owns 55% and a male partner holds options for another 10%. On a fully diluted basis she's at 50%, which fails. Recalculate on a fully diluted basis before you submit.

Next steps

Your first step is to check your NAICS code and SAM status today. Then compare your governing documents against the control rules before you open any application.

Do it yourself today:

  1. Look up your primary NAICS code on SBA's eligible NAICS list and confirm your SAM.gov registration is active.
  2. Print your operating agreement or bylaws and highlight every vote that needs more than a simple majority, every officer title, and every bank signer. Compare them against sections 1–3 above.
  3. Put your ownership percentages from the ledger, K-1s, and SAM side by side. If anything doesn't match, fix it before you sign in to MySBA Certifications.

If you'd rather not do it alone: APEX Accelerators are federally funded and offer free or low-cost help with SAM registration and certification paperwork. They suit first-time bidders who want someone to walk through the documents with them. They aren't the right choice if you need legal advice on restructuring an operating agreement. That's attorney work, and attorney fees vary widely. A paid third-party certifier such as USWCC ($275–$350) or NWBOC ($400) gives you a structured review (as of October 2026; check the official pages). It's not worth the fee if your documents are already clean and you only need federal status, because SBA's path is free.

FAQ

What documents do I need for WOSB certification?

You'll need proof of U.S. citizenship for each qualifying woman, signed personal and business federal tax returns, and bank statements. Add your formation documents (articles, plus the operating agreement, bylaws and stock ledger, or partnership agreement) and any assumed-name or joint venture agreements (13 CFR Part 127, Subpart C). EDWOSB applicants also need three years of personal tax returns and a personal financial statement.

Is WOSB certification free?

Yes, when you apply through SBA at certifications.sba.gov. Third-party certifiers charge fees. USWCC charges $275–$350, NWBOC charges $400, and WBENC includes WOSB with WBE fees of $350–$1,250 (as of October 2026; check the official pages).

What is the difference between WOSB and EDWOSB?

EDWOSB adds three personal financial limits for each woman owner on top of the WOSB ownership and control rules. Her net worth must be under $850,000, her three-year average AGI must be $400,000 or less, and her total assets must be $6.5 million or less (13 CFR 127.203). EDWOSBs can also compete in the 113 NAICS codes reserved for EDWOSB set-asides, a figure from SBA's March 2022 list.

Does WOSB certification expire?

Certified firms go through a program examination every three years. SBA's annual attestation requirement is currently in abeyance (SBA). Third-party certifiers have their own renewal cycles. NWBOC, for example, charges an annual renewal fee.

Can I self-certify as WOSB?

No. Self-certification for WOSB set-asides ended on October 15, 2020 (CRS R46322). You now need certification from SBA or an approved third-party certifier, and it has to appear in MySBA Certifications.
