The EU Funding and Tenders Portal helpdesk is the IT Helpdesk. You contact it through an online contact form, which you find under Guidance & documents > Helpdesk & support > IT Helpdesk. You can also reach it from your user icon once you are logged in. It handles technical problems with portal tools such as submission, registration and EU Login. Questions about eligibility or what to write in a proposal go to a different service: the Research Enquiry Service, or your National Contact Point (NCP) for Horizon Europe (Online Manual – Help). If a submission fails because of a fault on the portal's side, you have 4 calendar days after the call closes to file a complaint through the same form (Complaints about failed submission).

Key points

  • Failed-submission complaints must reach the IT Helpdesk within 4 days
  • Submit at least 48 hours early; each resubmission replaces the last
  • Content and eligibility questions go to NCPs or RES, not IT

What is the EU Funding and Tenders Portal Helpdesk?

The EU Funding and Tenders Portal Helpdesk is the IT Helpdesk "for IT-related questions" about the portal's tools and processes. The Online Manual lists it next to separate services for validation, Horizon Europe enquiries and general EU questions (Online Manual – Help).

The portal's Help page lists four routes:

  • IT Helpdesk: for IT-related questions, reached through the contact form.
  • Participant Validation Helpdesk: "for legal questions relating to participant validation and LEAR appointment if you have NOT registered an organisation yet". Organisations that are already registered use the messaging feature in Manage my Organisations instead.
  • Research Enquiry Service or your National Contact Point: for Horizon Europe questions.
  • Europe Direct: for general questions about the EU. Programme-specific questions go to the functional mailbox given in the call documents.

(All four routes are from the Online Manual – Help, as of October 2026.)

The IT Helpdesk staff work Monday to Friday during Central European Time working hours (SEP user manual mirror). Plan around that. If you open a ticket on Friday evening for a Monday deadline, nobody will read it on Saturday or Sunday.

Pitfall: people treat "helpdesk" as a single inbox. If you ask the IT Helpdesk whether your startup counts as an SME, or whether a cost is eligible, you will usually be sent somewhere else, and that costs you days. Work out which kind of question you have before you write. For sorting out the SME question itself, see EU Funding for SMEs: 7 Open 2026 Calls and How to Qualify.

How to submit a ticket to the EU Funding and Tenders Portal Helpdesk

Log in to the portal, open Guidance & documents > Helpdesk & support, click IT Helpdesk, choose the right domain, describe the problem and send it. The five steps below follow the labels used in the official IT How To pages.

  1. Log in with EU Login. Sign in at the Funding & Tenders Portal. If two-step verification is what's blocking you, try the fixes in EU Funding and Tenders Portal Two Factor Authentication 2026 before you write a ticket.
  2. Open the helpdesk page. From the top menu choose Guidance & documents, then Helpdesk & support, then click the IT Helpdesk icon. The Register an organisation guide describes exactly this path. If you are already logged in, the SEP manual also documents a shortcut: User icon > IT Helpdesk at the top right (SEP user manual mirror).
  3. Select the domain. For problems with the proposal submission system, the official instruction is to select the domain Submission of proposals (Submit a proposal – IT How To). Pick whichever domain matches where the error happened (registration, grant management and so on). A wrong domain can delay routing.
  4. Describe the problem and attach evidence. Enter "the details of the problem" in the form (Register an organisation) and attach a screenshot if you need to (SEP user manual mirror). The next section lists what to include.
  5. Submit and keep the reference. Send the form and save the acknowledgement or ticket number. Quote it in every follow-up message, and in any later complaint about a failed submission.

Example (illustrative): a university research office sees an error when it clicks Submit on a Horizon Europe proposal. It selects Submission of proposals, pastes the exact error text, attaches a screenshot showing the system clock, and gives the proposal ID and call identifier. That one message lets the helpdesk find the session in its logs without asking for anything more.

Write the description in this order: what you were doing, what you expected, what happened instead, and what you have already tried. Pitfalls and fixes

  • Vague subject lines like "Urgent!!" or "Portal not working". Fix: put the deadline and the proposal ID in the first line of the description.
  • Screenshots that hide the URL bar or clock. Fix: capture the full window. If you later file a complaint, you'll need these screenshots as proof of the failure (Complaints about failed submission).
  • Sharing your password. Your EU Login username and password are personal, and the manual says not to share them with anyone (Online Manual – EU Login). Never put them in a ticket.

How long does the EU Funding and Tenders Portal Helpdesk take to respond?

The portal pages reviewed for this guide publish no general response-time commitment for ordinary IT tickets. The one written timing is for failed-submission complaints, which get an acknowledgement "the same or next working day" (Complaints about failed submission, as of October 2026).

What the official sources do say:

  • Hours: Monday to Friday, during working hours CET (SEP user manual mirror). Weekends and EU public holidays are not covered.
  • Complaints: an acknowledgement the same or next working day. EU services then check the application and access log files, and tell you the outcome "as soon as possible" (Complaints about failed submission).
  • Submission timing advice: submit "at least 48 hours before the deadline" so you avoid problems with system requirements, local configuration settings and system congestion (Electronic proposal submission).

Read those three facts together. The portal assumes you'll have about two working days to spare if something breaks. If you open a ticket 6 hours before the deadline, you are relying on someone picking it up the same afternoon.

Pitfall: treating a ticket as a deadline extension. Opening a ticket doesn't stop the clock. The Submit a proposal guide says no changes are allowed once the closure date is reached. Keep trying alternatives (another browser, another network, a colleague's machine with their own login if they have a role on the proposal) while you wait for a reply.

IT Helpdesk vs National Contact Point: which one to contact

Contact the IT Helpdesk when the portal itself fails. Contact your NCP when you need advice on eligibility, fit or proposal content. NCPs are national structures that give "guidance, practical information and assistance on all aspects of participating in Horizon Europe" (R&I Contact us).

NCPs exist in EU Member States and in countries associated with Horizon Europe. You find yours by country and theme in the NCP directory. Some NCP information is visible without logging in, and the rest only after login (UFUK Avrupa portal guide). One limitation: NCPs only cover Horizon Europe (and related programmes in some countries). For calls under other programmes, such as Digital Europe or LIFE, check the call documents for the right contact.

The Research Enquiry Service is the Commission-level alternative. It covers questions "about research in Europe, Horizon 2020 or other research programmes and funding instruments" (R&I Contact us). National agencies such as Austria's FFG send legal and financial Horizon Europe questions there (FFG).

Example (illustrative): a Spanish startup preparing an EIC application can't tell whether its pitch deck upload failed or was never needed. The upload error goes to the IT Helpdesk. The question "is a pitch deck required at this stage?" goes to the NCP, and the call documents answer it too. Dates for that programme are in EIC Accelerator Deadlines: 2026 Cut-Off Dates and Steps.

When should you escalate a helpdesk ticket?

Escalate when the problem blocks submission, the deadline is close, and you have had no useful answer. If the call has already closed and the portal was at fault, escalate by filing a formal failed-submission complaint within 4 calendar days.

The documentation reviewed here describes no "urgent" button or priority flag. In practice, escalating means adding information to the same ticket and, after closure, switching to the formal complaint route:

  1. Reply on the same ticket. Don't open a duplicate. Quote the original reference and add new facts: a new error text, a second browser tested, how many hours are left before the deadline.
  2. Name the deadline in the first line. For example: "Call closes 2026-10-28 17:00 Brussels time. Submission blocked."
  3. Keep preparing. Save timestamped PDFs of Part B and the annexes. File attributes that show when each file was created and last modified are what the complaint procedure asks for (Complaints about failed submission).
  4. After closure, file a complaint through the IT Helpdesk within 4 calendar days, with screenshots and the timestamped files. If the complaint is upheld, the files you supplied become your proposal for evaluation. If you supplied no files, the last version recorded in the system is used.
  5. Ask the right people in parallel. If the block is really a validation issue (for example a PIC that can't be attached), use the Messages tab in your organisation's record. If it's a programme question, contact the call's functional mailbox. Both are listed in the Online Manual – Help.

Pitfalls

  • Escalating a problem on your side. Complaints are checked against IT logs to confirm "technical problems on the Portal side". A slow office network or a corrupted PDF won't count.
  • Missing the 4-day window. Calendar days include weekends. If a call closes on Thursday, the window ends on Monday.
  • Asking an NCP to escalate a bug. NCPs can advise you, but they don't run the submission system.

Common errors reported to the EU Portal Helpdesk and how to fix them

Most tickets involve EU Login and two-step verification, PIC or LEAR validation, upload or submission errors near the deadline, and confusion about proposal status. Several of these can be fixed before you write a ticket.

EU Login and two-step verification. Lost phones, codes that are rejected and locked accounts are the most common blockers. Start with the official EU Login help FAQ. Two-step verification can be requested when you start a proposal or open drafts (Submit a proposal – IT How To), so set it up days in advance. Our two-factor authentication guide covers the setup.

PIC and LEAR problems. If your organisation hasn't registered yet, legal questions about validation go to the Participant Validation Helpdesk form, not to IT. Once you have a PIC, talk to the validation team through the Messages tab of your Organisation Data Update screen (Register an organisation). If a form crashes during registration, that is an IT ticket. The full registration flow is in EU Funding and Tenders Portal Registration: PIC in 6 Steps.

Upload and submission errors. The manual names three causes: system requirements, local configuration settings and system congestion. Before you open a ticket:

  • Try a second supported browser and switch off extensions.
  • Re-export the PDF. Flatten forms and remove encryption or passwords.
  • Check the page limits and file formats in the call's templates.
  • Submit early and resubmit if you need to. "Every submitted version will replace the previous one" (Electronic proposal submission).

Page-limit problems often surface only at the last validation step. Horizon Europe Proposal Template Part B: 2026 Scoring Map shows where the limits apply.

"Did it actually submit?" Many tickets are really status questions. Check the proposal's status in My Proposals before you write. Our My Proposals status table explains what each status means.

Pitfall: sending one ticket that mixes several problems, for example "my 2FA fails and also is my budget eligible?" Split it. Send the technical part to IT and the rest to the NCP or RES.

What to do if the portal is down near a submission deadline

Record the failure with screenshots and timestamps, open an IT Helpdesk ticket that states the deadline, keep retrying, and watch for an official extension. If the call closes before you get through, file a complaint within 4 calendar days.

  1. Capture evidence straight away. Take full-screen screenshots with the time visible and note the exact error text. The complaint procedure names screenshots as proof of the failure (Complaints about failed submission).
  2. Freeze your files. Save the final Part B and annexes as PDFs before the deadline and don't edit them afterwards. The created and modified timestamps are your evidence.
  3. Open the ticket under Submission of proposals, with the proposal ID, call identifier and deadline in the first line.
  4. Retry from a different setup. Use another browser, another network, or another team member who has a role on the proposal. You can resubmit as many times as you like before closure.
  5. Watch for official notices. Check the call's topic page and the programme's news channels. The Commission has extended deadlines after portal failures before. For example, in May 2021 the EIC Pathfinder Open deadline was moved to 25 May 2021 at 17:00 CET after submission problems (ChatEIC report). Don't count on an extension. It is an exception, not a rule.
  6. After closure, complain within 4 calendar days if the portal was at fault, attaching your evidence. You'll get an acknowledgement the same or next working day.

Pitfall: waiting for an outage notice before you act. Document and submit tickets as it happens, because evidence gathered afterwards is weaker. And because the manual recommends submitting at least 48 hours early, a last-hour outage is the case where a complaint has the best chance. A problem on your own side does not qualify.

Next steps

You can sort out most helpdesk issues yourself today with three actions, and free official advisers cover most of the rest.

Do this today (DIY):

  1. Bookmark the IT Helpdesk contact form. Save a ticket template in your notes containing your PIC, EU Login e-mail and the proposal ID and call identifier, so you can paste it in seconds.
  2. Find your NCP for your theme in the NCP directory and save their e-mail address now, before you have a question.
  3. Put a reminder in your calendar 48 hours before your call deadline as a "submit by" date, in line with the Online Manual advice.

If you'd rather not do it yourself:

  • National Contact Points: free (as of October 2026; check the official page). They suit first-time applicants who want advice on eligibility and fit in their own language. They are not for anyone who wants their proposal written, and they cannot fix portal bugs.
  • Research Enquiry Service: free (as of October 2026; check the official page). It suits consultants and research offices with precise legal or financial questions about Horizon Europe. It isn't built for urgent technical fixes on deadline day.
  • Private grant consultants: paid, and priced by quote. Fees vary widely by scope and many combine a fixed fee with a success fee, so ask for a written quote (prices change; check with each provider). They suit teams with no time or experience, or ones applying to competitive calls such as the EIC. They are not for applicants who only have a technical portal problem, which the free IT Helpdesk handles. A consultant also can't get you faster IT support than you could get yourself.

FAQ

Is the EU Funding and Tenders Portal Helpdesk free to use?

Yes. The IT Helpdesk is a European Commission service reached through a free online form. NCPs and the Research Enquiry Service are free too. You only pay if you choose to hire a private consultant.

Can you contact the helpdesk by phone instead of a ticket?

The contact form is the documented main channel. A phone number (+32 2 29 92222) and the e-mail address EC-FUNDING-TENDER-SERVICE-DESK@ec.europa.eu appear in the Commission's SEP evaluation quick guide for technical SEP issues (SEP Expert Evaluation guide). Even if you call, open a written ticket as well, because complaints depend on a written record.

What is the difference between the IT Helpdesk and the Research Enquiry Service?

The IT Helpdesk handles IT-related questions about portal tools, such as submission errors, registration forms and login problems. The Research Enquiry Service answers questions about research programmes and funding instruments, including legal and financial points for Horizon Europe (R&I Contact us).

Do you need an EU Login account to submit a helpdesk ticket?

The documented route to the IT Helpdesk is through the portal, and the form is easiest to reach once you are logged in through the user icon. If EU Login itself is the problem, start with the EU Login help FAQ. The Guidance & documents > Helpdesk & support menu can be opened without logging in.
