The EU Funding and Tenders Portal Participant Register is the part of the Portal where your organisation is registered, given its PIC (Participant Identification Code) and later validated. It is also where your LEAR and other authorised users are managed. You need a 9-digit PIC to submit a proposal (Online Manual). Getting from zero to grant-ready takes five steps: search for an existing PIC, register, pass legal validation, appoint a LEAR, and assign the signatory roles.

Key points

  • The PIC is a 9-digit code; the Participant Register holds the profile
  • Only one LEAR per organisation, validated by the Central Validation Service
  • Financial capacity checks don't apply to requests of EUR 60,000 or less

Best for: first-time EU grant applicants and the people who manage their PIC

What is the Participant Register and how is it different from a PIC?

The Participant Register is your organisation's full profile in the Funding & Tenders Portal. The PIC is just the 9-digit ID created when that profile is registered. You need both to apply. The register holds your legal data, contacts, documents, financial capacity results and user roles. The PIC is the key you type into a proposal so the system can pull all of that in (Online Manual).

People mix them up because the PIC appears straight away, at the end of the wizard. A freshly created PIC is provisional (not yet validated), though. It lets you apply, but it doesn't let you sign a grant (Online Manual, Registration and validation).

Example: A small biotech startup registers on Monday and copies the PIC into a Horizon Europe proposal on Tuesday. Nobody checks its legal documents at that stage. The Central Validation Service only asks for them if the proposal is selected. Validation happens after evaluation results, and only for beneficiaries, affiliated entities and contractors. Associated partners and subcontractors don't need it (Online Manual).

Common mistakes:

  • "We have a PIC, so we're done." You still need validation and a LEAR. Without a LEAR you can't update legal entity data or sign contracts electronically (Online Manual).
  • Assuming the PIC is the only identifier you'll ever need. Check your call document. If it asks for a different organisation identifier, use the code it asks for.

How to register an organisation in the Participant Register

You register an organisation once, through the "Register your organisation" wizard, signed in with EU Login. You'll need the legal name, VAT or registration number, address and legal form. If you have your official documents in front of you while you fill it in, you're less likely to declare something (legal form, non-profit, public/private) that later fails validation (Online Manual).

Before you start

  1. Create or sign in to an EU Login account. Two-factor authentication is now part of signing in. If it blocks you, see EU Funding and Tenders Portal Two Factor Authentication 2026.
  2. Go to How to Participate > Participant Register > Search a PIC and search for several spellings of your name, so you don't create a duplicate (Online Manual).
  3. Have these at hand: your company registry extract, VAT certificate (or proof of VAT exemption), statutes, and the founding act if you're a public body.

The wizard, step by step

The current IT guide describes these screens (IT How To, Registration steps):

  1. Provide organisation identification details. Enter the Legal name, VAT number and Registration country. For EU countries the VAT number is checked against databases. A warning icon appears if the VAT number isn't found or the legal name doesn't match.
  2. Check the duplicate list. The system shows "possible duplicates based on the provided Legal name and registration country." Click Use this PIC if your organisation is listed. Click Continue registration only if it's really not there.
  3. Provide organisation data. Enter the main contact details and the official language. If your VAT was validated but the address is different, a mismatch message appears. Fix it now, not later.
  4. Provide legal information. Choose the Legal form from the dropdown. Fill in the optional extra fields if they apply to you.
  5. Define authorised users. Your EU Login becomes the main authorised user, with the Self-registrant role. You can add a backup authorised user, which is a good idea if you're a consultant or might leave the organisation.
  6. Summary. Check everything. Use Edit to correct entries and Print to keep a copy.
  7. Success. Your PIC is displayed. Continue to update takes you to the data update screens.

You can save a draft after the organisation data page and pick it up later under My Organisations > Continue Registration. Unfinished drafts are deleted after one year (Online Manual). Once a PIC exists, the wizard can't be run again for that organisation. Every later change goes through data updates (IT How To, Participant register).

Pitfalls and fixes

  • Registering as an individual. Natural persons can register, but some labels don't fit. The manual tells you to use your country of residence instead of the country of registration, and your passport number instead of a registration number (Online Manual).
  • Using a trading name. Enter the legal name exactly as it appears in the registry extract. A mismatch is one of the first things validation flags.
  • Ticking "non-profit" or "public body" because it feels right. You'll have to prove that status with statutes or a founding act. Only declare it if those documents say so.

How to appoint a LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative)

Once the Central Validation Service asks for it, the Self-registrant enters the LEAR's details in the Portal. The system then generates an appointment letter. Your legal representative signs it, and you upload it with both people's ID and proof of the representative's authority. The LEAR appointment is mandatory for every validated participant (Online Manual, LEAR appointment and validation).

Most PIC guides stop before this step, and it's usually what delays grant signature. Here is the official sequence:

  1. Wait for the notification. The Central Validation Service contacts the Self-registrant, the person who registered the organisation, to start the LEAR procedure. Validating the organisation comes first: it's a prerequisite for validating the LEAR (H2020 Online Manual).
  2. Choose two people. You need the LEAR, who will handle daily administration, and the legal representative, who has authority to bind the organisation and will appoint the LEAR.
  3. Enter the data. Go to My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details > Authorised users/Lear section > Add Lear. Enter the LEAR's name and contact details and the legal representative's details (Online Manual).
  4. Download the generated LEAR appointment letter. It lists the LEAR's roles and duties and includes consent to the Portal Terms and Conditions.
  5. Sign it. The legal representative dates and signs the letter, either by hand in ink or with a qualified electronic signature (QES) under the eIDAS Regulation (Online Manual).
  6. Upload three items: the signed letter; copies of valid ID for both the legal representative and the LEAR (photo and signature pages); and a document proving the legal representative's authority that shows their function and names them, such as statutes, a board decision or a registry extract.
  7. Activate the account. After validation the LEAR gets an email with activation instructions or a PIN code by SMS. If the PIN doesn't work, call the IT Helpdesk on +32 (0)2 299 2222 (Online Manual).

Keep the original signed documents. The manual says you must keep them for controls and audits.

Example (illustrative): At a mid-sized NGO, the executive director is the legal representative under the statutes. The grants officer becomes the LEAR. The director signs the letter with a QES, and the officer uploads the letter, both IDs and the statutes page naming the director's powers. If the statutes only say "the Board" represents the NGO, the officer also uploads the board resolution appointing the director. Without that link in the chain, the reviewer can't see why this person had authority to sign.

Pitfalls and fixes

  • The legal representative appoints themselves as LEAR. This is allowed, but the LEAR handles routine admin work. Most organisations choose someone in the grants office.
  • The proof of authority doesn't name the signatory. A generic statute clause isn't enough on its own. Add a document that names the person.
  • A scanned QES. A printed and re-scanned e-signature is no longer a qualified signature. Upload the original signed PDF, or sign in ink.

How does organisation validation work and how long does it take?

The Central Validation Service, run by the Research Executive Agency (REA), compares your uploaded documents with what you declared and then marks the PIC as validated. Validation only starts when you're selected for funding. It happens once and is reused for all later EU grants and tenders (REA Central Validation Service presentation).

How it runs:

  1. You receive an evaluation result letter or invitation to grant preparation.
  2. The Central Validation Service contacts the Self-registrant through the Participant Register (Online Manual).
  3. You upload the requested documents and answer questions about legal personality and any special status: non-profit, public/private, international organisation.
  4. The PIC is validated, followed by the LEAR appointment.
  5. If the call requires it, a financial capacity assessment follows. Coordinators also have bank account validation (Online Manual).

How long it takes: The Online Manual doesn't publish a fixed processing time (as of October 2026). The REA's guidance points to two things that actually set the pace: how quickly you answer, and whether your documents match what you declared. In practice, an organisation whose registry extract, VAT record and declared data all match is a simple case. Branches, recently merged entities and entities with an unusual legal form usually need extra rounds of questions. Plan around your grant preparation deadline rather than an assumed number of days.

The rule that catches most people out: all communication with the Central Validation Service has to go through the Participant Register (the My Organisations page). Emails and other channels are not accepted (Online Manual). If a request lands while the Self-registrant is on holiday, the backup Self-registrant you added in the wizard can answer it.

Pitfalls and fixes

  • The Self-registrant has left the organisation. Nobody can see the requests. Fix: ask the IT Helpdesk to help add a new authorised user. Our guide EU Funding and Tenders Portal Helpdesk: Ticket in 5 Steps shows how to file the ticket.
  • Watching the proposal status instead of the organisation messages. Grant preparation and validation run side by side. Check your status in My Proposals and check the PIC's messages too.

What documents are needed for validation (legal entity and financial capacity)?

Legal validation needs a Legal Entity Form plus official proof of your legal name, legal form, address and VAT status. A financial capacity assessment needs your recent accounts, but it's only requested when the grant amount and your entity type call for one. The two checks are separate and use different documents.

Legal entity validation

How old the documents can be: The H2020 manual said VAT documents and registry extracts "cannot be older than 6 months." More recent REA presentations allow extracts and VAT documents under one year old (REA presentation). Because the sources disagree, the safe option is to upload an extract issued within the last six months. Documents in any official EU language are accepted, and an English translation is recommended. Each uploaded file can be at most 6 MB (Online Manual, Data updates).

SME status: If being an SME is an eligibility condition, the Portal asks you to complete an SME self-assessment when you don't have one from the last two years. You enter figures from your last approved accounting period (Online Manual). For SME-specific calls, see EU Funding for SMEs: 7 Open 2026 Calls and How to Qualify.

Financial capacity assessment

The Online Manual says financial capacity is assessed for beneficiaries and affiliated entities, with these exceptions: public bodies, international organisations, individual grant requests of EUR 60,000 or less, and programmes with their own rules (Online Manual, Financial capacity assessment). In Horizon Europe the check is "normally" done only for the coordinator, and only when the requested amount is EUR 500,000 or more (Horizon Europe Work Programme 2026-2027, General Annexes).

  • Documents: usually profit and loss accounts and balance sheets for the last two closed financial years, a business plan, and an audit report from an approved external auditor where one exists.
  • Where to upload: My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details > Financial capacity.
  • How it's judged: liquidity, financial autonomy, solvency and profitability, plus dependency on EU funding and past deficits or revenue.
  • What happens if it's weak: the measures include joint and several liability, prefinancing in instalments, a prefinancing guarantee, no prefinancing, replacing the partner, or rejection (Online Manual).

An assessment stays valid for 18 months after the close of the most recent accounting period it covered (REA presentation).

Example (illustrative): A two-year-old startup coordinating a EUR 600,000 Horizon Europe grant has only one closed financial year. It uploads that year's accounts and a business plan. If the result is weak, it should expect measures such as a prefinancing guarantee rather than automatic rejection. For EIC calls, check the programme's own rules. See EIC Accelerator Deadlines 2026.

What roles exist in the Participant Register besides LEAR?

Besides the LEAR, an organisation can have a Self-registrant, Account Administrators, Legal Signatories (LSIGN), Financial Signatories (FSIGN), a Primary Audit Contact and Audit Contacts. Each role is linked to an EU Login account (Online Manual, Roles and access rights).

Signatories are nominated at organisation level first. The coordinator or the organisation then assigns them to individual projects through My Projects > Actions > Project Consortium (Online Manual, Data updates). When nominating a signatory, the LEAR can add a comment that limits the scope, such as "Horizon Europe projects only."

Example setup for a university (illustrative): The LEAR is the head of the research office. Two Account Administrators in that office handle routine role requests. The vice-rector for research is an LSIGN, and the finance director plus one deputy are FSIGNs. Faculty members don't hold organisation roles at all. They get project-level roles from the coordinator.

How to update or correct your organisation's data and roles after validation

Small corrections can be made in the Participant Register, but any change to legal data needs supporting documents and re-checking by the Central Validation Service. Replacing the LEAR means going through the appointment procedure again. After validation, only the LEAR and Account Administrators can make changes. Before validation, the Self-registrant can (Online Manual, Data updates).

Change organisation data

  1. Go to My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details > Edit Organisation Data. Older guides call this Actions > Modify Organisation.
  2. Edit the field, for example the legal address after a move.
  3. Upload the supporting document: a new registry extract, VAT certificate or board decision, each under 6 MB.
  4. Submit, then watch the organisation's messages for questions from the Central Validation Service.
  5. If you're in ongoing grants, ask your coordinator to tell the Project Officers. Changes such as partial or universal transfers of rights may also need grant amendments (Online Manual).

Change roles

  1. The LEAR or an Account Administrator opens My Organisations > Actions > Edit Roles.
  2. Add or revoke Account Administrators, LSIGNs and FSIGNs.
  3. Revoke access as soon as someone leaves. The manual warns that if grants or contracts are signed by people whose access should have been revoked, the organisation is responsible.

Replace the LEAR

Appoint the new LEAR using the same process as the first appointment, starting from Authorised users/Lears > Replace LEAR (Online Manual). You'll need a new letter signed by the current legal representative, new ID copies and new proof of authority. If only the LEAR's email address has changed, the Central Validation Service must be told through the same section.

Common mistake: waiting until the LEAR has already left. There is only one LEAR, so while that seat is empty nobody can change legal data. Start the replacement during the notice period.

How to find a lost or forgotten PIC

Use How to Participate > Participant Register > Search a PIC. Search for the full or partial legal name and filter by country before you think about registering again (Online Manual).

  1. Open Search a PIC and type part of the legal name. Try a few variants: with and without "GmbH"/"Ltd", the name in your national language, older names.
  2. Filter by country.
  3. Check under My Organisations while signed in. Every PIC where your EU Login holds a role is listed there.
  4. Search old proposal submission receipts or grant documents for a 9-digit number.
  5. Universities and hospitals: ask the research or grants office. Departments should use the central PIC.

If you find out you have two PICs, don't keep using both. Report the duplicate to the Central Validation Service through Portal messaging in the Participant Register (Online Manual). Keep the PIC with the most validated history, which will usually be the older one.

Next steps

For the formal rules behind every check in this guide, read the Commission's Rules for Legal Entity Validation, LEAR Appointment and Financial Capacity Assessment. The Online Manual pages tell you which screens to use. The Rules document is the reference to check when your case doesn't fit the standard pattern.

If you'd rather not do it yourself:

  • National Contact Points (NCPs) for Horizon Europe are free, government-funded advisers in each country. They're good for first-time applicants with procedural questions. They won't fill in the Portal for you, and how much time they can give varies by country (as of October 2026; check your NCP's page).
  • Enterprise Europe Network advisers are also free and aimed at SMEs. They're less suited to universities, whose research offices usually handle this already.
  • Private grant consultants can act as backup Self-registrant and prepare documents. Fees vary widely and are set by each firm, either fixed or tied to success, so ask for a written quote. They're useful if you're applying under time pressure. They're not for you if you just want a PIC, since that's free. And they can't replace your legal representative's signature or act as your LEAR.

FAQ

What is a PIC number in the EU Funding and Tenders Portal?

A PIC (Participant Identification Code) is the 9-digit code your organisation gets when it finishes the Participant Register wizard. You enter it in proposals so the Portal can pull in your organisation's data. It starts out unvalidated. It's validated only if you're selected for funding.

Who can act as LEAR for a university or NGO?

Any person the legal representative appoints can be the LEAR. Usually it's someone in the research office or grants department, not the rector or director. The appointment needs the system-generated letter signed by the legal representative, both people's ID, and proof of the representative's authority. There is only one LEAR per organisation.

Can one organisation have two PICs?

It shouldn't. Duplicates are a known problem, which is why the wizard shows possible matches based on legal name and country. If you find a duplicate, report it to the Central Validation Service through Portal messaging and keep using the PIC with the validated history.

Why was my organisation's validation rejected?

The usual reasons are a legal name or address that doesn't match the registry extract, out-of-date VAT or registry documents, a declared status (non-profit, public body) that the statutes don't support, or no answer through the Participant Register. Correct the data under My Organisations, upload fresh documents and reply only through Portal messaging, because emails aren't accepted.
