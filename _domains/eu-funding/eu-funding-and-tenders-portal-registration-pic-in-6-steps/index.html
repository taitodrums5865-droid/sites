EU Funding and Tenders Portal Registration: PIC in 6 Steps

EU Funding and Tenders Portal registration has two parts. First you create a personal EU Login account. Then you register your organisation in the Participant Register through a 6-step wizard, and that wizard gives you a 9-digit PIC (Participant Identification Code) straight away. Legal validation and the LEAR appointment usually come later, once a proposal you are part of has been selected, so a new PIC is enough to join and submit a proposal (Online Manual; IT How To: Registration steps).

Key points

  • Create an EU Login first, then run the 6-step wizard to get a PIC
  • Legal validation starts only after your proposal is selected
  • Sole traders register as natural persons and act as their own LEAR

Best for: first-time EU grant applicants and their advisers

Which option is right for you?

Start by checking whether your organisation already has a PIC. Registering a second one is the most common avoidable mistake, and most universities and many established companies are already in the register.

The reasons, one line each. Beginners need EU Login before anything else works. Institutions almost always have a PIC already. SMEs lose nothing by registering themselves. Sole traders follow a separate document path as natural persons. Consultants need their own access rights. Selected applicants have a validation clock running.

How to Register on the EU Funding and Tenders Portal (Step-by-Step)

There are two layers. An EU Login personal account comes first, then a separate organisation registration that creates your PIC, and you need both before you can submit a proposal. You can browse calls and read call documents on the Portal without logging in. The registration only matters when you want to join or submit a proposal.

Part A: Create your EU Login

  1. Go to the EU Login page and click Create an account.
  2. Enter your First name, Last name and E-mail, confirm the email and choose your E-mail language.
  3. Type the on-screen verification code, accept the privacy statement and click Create an account (EURES guide to EU Login).
  4. Open the activation email and set your password from the link.
  5. Set up two-factor authentication. The Portal's IT guide says 2FA on EU Login became mandatory over the course of 2026. Register a method (the EU Login Mobile App, a security key, a passkey or eID), enable it in your security settings and answer three security questions. The guide recommends registering more than one method so you don't get locked out.

Use a work email address, not a personal one. The account is yours personally, but organisation roles get attached to it.

Part B: Register the organisation and get the PIC

  1. Open the Participant Register page and search for your organisation first.
  2. If nothing comes up, sign in and click Register your organisation (IT How To).
  3. Go through the six wizard steps: identification details, organisation data, legal information, authorised users, summary and success. Each step is covered in the section on registering an organisation below.
  4. Copy the PIC from the success screen into your proposal team's shared notes.

Once you finish, the organisation shows up under My Organisations with the status Declared, meaning not yet validated. Your EU Login gets the Self-registrant role (IT How To).

For example, a two-person software startup could create EU Logins on Monday morning, register the company before lunch and give the PIC to a consortium coordinator the same day. Nobody at the Commission has to approve anything at that point.

EU Funding and Tenders Portal Sign Up: EU Login vs Organization Registration

EU Login is your personal identity, an email and password you use across EU systems. Organisation registration in the Participant Register happens once per legal entity and issues the PIC. People often mix the two up. "Signing up" to the Portal means creating an EU Login, and that alone doesn't make your organisation a participant.

Common pitfalls:

  • Registering the organisation under one person's private account. If that person leaves before validation, nobody else can update the record. Fix: add a backup authorised user in wizard step 4 (Registration steps).
  • Treating the PIC as a personal ID. A researcher who moves universities doesn't take the PIC along. They use the new institution's PIC.
  • Several colleagues each registering "just in case". The wizard flags possible duplicates by legal name and country, but similar spellings can still get past it. Agree internally on who registers.

How to Register an Organization in the EU Funding & Tenders Portal

You enter the legal name, VAT or registration number, address, language and legal form, then name the authorised users. Supporting documents are uploaded later, when the Central Validation Service asks for them. The wizard marks mandatory fields with a red asterisk, shows errors in red, and has a Save button so you can keep a draft and come back to it (IT How To: Participant register).

The six wizard steps and their labels

Based on the official Registration steps page (as of October 2026):

  1. Organisation identification. Enter the Legal name and VAT number. For EU countries the wizard checks the VAT number against national databases and shows a warning icon if it can't find it. It also lists "possible duplicates based on the provided Legal name and registration country". If one of them is yours, click Use this PIC to contact that organisation's current LEAR instead of carrying on.
  2. Organisation data. Main contact details, legal address and the organisation's official language. If the address doesn't match the VAT register, a message tells you. For Spain and Germany the check covers only the VAT number.
  3. Legal information. Pick the Legal form from the dropdown and fill in the extra legal data it asks for, such as registration authority, registration date and registration number.
  4. Authorised users. Your EU Login is added automatically. You can add a backup user with self-registrant rights, who can update data or upload documents after registration.
  5. Summary. Every field is shown with Edit buttons. Read the legal name one letter at a time here.
  6. Success. Your new PIC appears, and you can use it in a proposal immediately.

What to have open before you start

For validation, the Commission compares your data with official documents. The REA Central Validation Service lists a legal entity form (dated, stamped and signed), a VAT extract less than 1 year old (or proof of VAT exemption), a registration extract less than 1 year old for private bodies, the law or decree for public bodies, and statutes for non-profits (REA Central Validation Service presentation). You don't upload these in the wizard. Having them open while you type, though, keeps what you register identical to what you'll later prove.

Example: if your trade register extract says "Nordlicht Analytics GmbH" and your website says "Nordlicht", type Nordlicht Analytics GmbH in Legal name. The short brand name goes in the business name field, if you use one.

Pitfalls and fixes

  • VAT warning icon in step 1. Check the format first (country prefix, no spaces). If your national VIES record is out of date, fix it with your tax office. Don't type a different number to make the warning go away.
  • Address mismatch message in step 2. Use the registered legal seat, not your office or co-working address.
  • Wrong legal form. A foundation registered as a company, or a public university registered as a private body, causes problems at validation because the status (public/private, for-profit/non-profit) is checked against your statutes (Rules on LEV, LEAR and FCA).

If you are about to use the PIC in a Horizon Europe proposal, the participant table in Part A of the form pulls data from this register. Our Horizon Europe Part B scoring map covers the narrative half of that same submission.

What Is a PIC Number and How Long Does It Take to Get One

A PIC (Participant Identification Code) is a 9-digit ID that the wizard issues instantly when you submit it. Legal validation is a separate, later step, and for grants it is triggered only after a proposal you're in has been evaluated successfully (Online Manual).

So there are two different timelines:

Status "Declared"From registration until validatedDefault for new records

How long validation takes depends on how fast you send correct documents and whether the Central Validation Service needs to ask more questions. The official pages don't give one fixed number of days. The deadline that applies to you is in the request message, so read it the day it arrives.

Not everyone needs validation. It applies to beneficiaries and affiliated entities in grants and to contractors in procurement, but not to associated partners or subcontractors (Online Manual).

Example: an NGO that joins a consortium as an associated partner gets a PIC, appears in the proposal, and may never go through full legal validation for that project.

Why Was My EU Portal Registration Rejected? Common Validation Errors

Most problems come from four things: a VAT number that doesn't match national records, a legal name or address that differs from your official documents, documents that are expired or the wrong type, and LEAR paperwork signed by someone who can't prove their authority. The wizard almost never "rejects" you. What stalls is validation, when the Central Validation Service compares your registered data with your documents. And all messages go through the Participant Register only, never by email (Online Manual).

How to reply to a validation request:

  1. Sign in, open My Organisations and find the organisation.
  2. Open the message or document request from the Central Validation Service.
  3. Upload exactly the document type asked for, in a legible scan.
  4. If data needs to change too, edit it and click Submit changes. Changed fields show a pencil icon until they're validated (IT How To).

Example: a small Portuguese company that registered its office street instead of its registered seat would upload the current certidão permanente and correct the address in the same reply. That's one round instead of two.

If you're using an IT problem as an excuse to miss a deadline, don't. Contact the Portal IT helpdesk and keep the ticket number.

How to Appoint a LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative) After Registration

Your organisation's legal representative appoints the LEAR with a signed appointment letter that you upload to the Participant Register. The Central Validation Service has to validate the appointment before your organisation can sign grant agreements electronically. Without a LEAR, "it will NOT be possible to update the legal entity data in the Participant Register, nor sign any contracts electronically in the Portal" (LEAR appointment and validation).

The LEAR is the person formally responsible for everything your organisation does in the Portal. The LEAR can also appoint Legal Signatories and Financial Signatories and manage access rights.

Steps, using the official menu labels

  1. Wait for the trigger. The self-registrant gets a notification from the Central Validation Service asking them to start the LEAR procedure. Organisation validation has to come first.
  2. Choose two people. You need the LEAR candidate and a legal representative with authority under your statutes, typically a CEO, rector or director-general.
  3. Enter the data. Go to My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details > Authorised users/Lear section > Add Lear and enter names and contact details for both people.
  4. Generate the letter. The system produces the LEAR appointment letter (the official template includes the LEAR roles and duties). It also checks whether the LEAR already has an EU Login.
  5. Sign. The legal representative and the LEAR both date and sign, either in blue ink or with a qualified electronic signature under eIDAS.
  6. Upload. Upload the signed letter, ID or passport copies for both signatories, and a document that names the legal representative and shows their power to act.
  7. Activate. After validation, the LEAR gets activation instructions by email and can get a PIN by SMS.

Pitfalls and fixes

  • A simple scanned signature or a non-qualified e-signature. Only blue ink or a qualified e-signature is accepted. Re-sign.
  • Proof of authority that doesn't name the person. A generic board resolution isn't enough. The document has to name the legal representative and their role.
  • Joint representation. If your statutes need two directors to sign together, both have to sign.
  • Picking someone who's about to leave. Choose a stable role such as a research office head or finance manager, and plan backups through Account Administrator appointments (Online Manual roles).

The organisation has to keep the original signed documents for as long as it uses the Portal and through the EU control retention periods (LEAR page).

EU Funding and Tenders Portal Registration for Individuals, Startups, and NGOs: Does It Differ?

Every entity type goes through the same wizard. What changes is the legal form, the status you declare, and the documents that validation asks for. The biggest split is between natural persons and legal persons.

The validation rules say that self-employed people, such as craftspeople, liberal professions or traders, count as natural persons, not private bodies. Their business doesn't have a legal personality separate from them. A natural person needs an official VAT document, or proof of VAT exemption, not older than 6 months, plus a legible copy of a valid ID card or passport. For natural persons, the participant is automatically the LEAR and the role can't go to anyone else (Rules on LEV, LEAR and FCA).

How to Find and Edit Your Organization's PIC After Registration

You find an existing PIC with the search on the Participant Register page. You edit it from My Organisations: the self-registrant can do that while the record is unvalidated, and only the LEAR or an Account Administrator can request changes once it's validated (Online Manual).

Find a PIC

  1. Open the Participant Register page.
  2. Search by organisation name (try the legal name and the short name) and narrow by country.
  3. If you're still not sure, ask your finance or research office. Large organisations sometimes hold more than one historical record.
  4. If the PIC belongs to your organisation but you have no access, use the contact option shown for that record, or Use this PIC in the wizard, to reach the current LEAR (Registration steps).

Edit organisation data

  1. Sign in and open My Organisations.
  2. Next to the organisation, open Actions and choose View organisation details (older guides call it Modify organisation).
  3. Use the left menu to find the data group, such as address or legal data.
  4. Change the fields. A yellow or red pencil marks unsubmitted or unvalidated changes, and a green tick marks validated data.
  5. Click Submit changes. For a validated entity, upload a supporting document for legal changes such as a new name or seat (IT How To).

Common mistake: registering a new PIC because the old one has an outdated address. Update the existing record instead. A new PIC means another duplicate to sort out later.

Next steps

You can do the free part today, and the paid options are only worth it if your time is worth more than the fee.

What to do today

  1. Create your EU Login and register two 2FA methods (2FA guide).
  2. Search the Participant Register for your organisation. If it isn't there, gather your trade register extract and VAT document, then run the 6-step wizard.
  3. Put a short note in your shared files with the PIC, the self-registrant's name, the backup user, and who could become LEAR.

If you'd rather not do it yourself

  • National Contact Points (free). Every Horizon Europe country has NCPs that give free, confidential advice on administrative questions like this (Ideal-ist: What is an NCP; example: FFG Austria). They suit researchers and first-time applicants. They're less useful if you want someone to do the clicking for you, because NCPs advise rather than register on your behalf.
  • Enterprise Europe Network (free for SMEs). EEN offices help SMEs with access to EU funding and partner search (EEN on the Portal; EEN access to EU funding). They fit startups and SMEs. Universities and large firms usually have in-house support instead. Capacity differs between regional offices.
  • Private grant consultants (paid). Fees vary widely and there's no official price list, so get a written quote (as of October 2026; check each provider). A consultant makes sense if you're also buying proposal-writing help. Paying only for registration rarely makes sense, because the wizard itself is free. One limitation: a consultant can't be your legal representative or, for a natural person, your LEAR, so you'll still sign and verify the documents yourself.

FAQ

How do I register on the EU Funding and Tenders Portal?

Create an EU Login at webgate.ec.europa.eu/cas, set up two-factor authentication, then sign in to the Participant Register and click Register your organisation. The 6-step wizard ends with a PIC on screen. Validation and the LEAR appointment come later, if your proposal is selected.

How do I sign up for the EU Funding and Tenders Portal?

Signing up means creating a personal EU Login with your name, email and a verification code, then activating it through the email link. That lets you log in, but it doesn't register your organisation. For that you need a PIC from the Participant Register.

What is the EU Funding and Tenders Portal Participant Register?

It's the Portal tool where organisations register, get a PIC and update their legal data. It also handles all communication with the Central Validation Service, including document requests and LEAR appointments (Online Manual).

How do I register an organization on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal?

Search for an existing PIC first. If there isn't one, enter the exact legal name and VAT number, the registered address and language, the legal form, and the authorised users, then check the summary and submit. Use the legal name from your trade register extract, not your brand name, so validation goes through later.
