EU funding for SMEs in late 2026 comes in three main forms. There are direct grants from Brussels-managed programmes (EIC Accelerator, Digital Europe, Horizon Europe, the Single Market Programme), small vouchers such as the EUIPO SME Fund, and EU-backed loans and equity that you get through your local bank or fund. As of 20 October 2026, at least seven calls are open or still reachable for SMEs, with deadlines running from 4 November 2026 to 2 February 2027. To qualify you must meet the official SME definition (fewer than 250 staff, plus ≤€50M turnover or ≤€43M balance sheet), be established in an eligible country, and register a PIC on the Funding & Tenders Portal.

Key points

  • EIC Accelerator grant is under €2.5M; last 2026 cut-off is 4 Nov
  • SME = under 250 staff and ≤€50M turnover or ≤€43M balance
  • AI4SME cyber call pays 75% for SMEs, deadline 14 Jan 2027

What Is EU Funding and How Does It Work for SMEs?

EU funding means grants, equity or guarantees paid from the EU budget. It reaches you either directly from the Commission and its agencies or through national and regional authorities. Which route applies decides where you apply and which rules you follow.

Direct (centralised) management. The Commission or an executive agency publishes calls, evaluates proposals and signs grant agreements. EISMEA, for example, runs the EIC and the Single Market Programme's SME strand. All of these calls sit on one site, the Funding & Tenders Portal. The calls in this guide all use this route, except the EUIPO SME Fund, which runs on its own EUIPO site.

Shared management. Cohesion funds such as the ERDF are spent by national or regional managing authorities, so their calls appear on national portals, not the EU portal. These schemes usually count as state aid. Small amounts often go out under the de minimis rule, which caps aid at €300,000 per undertaking over any three-year rolling period (EUR-Lex summary of Regulation 2023/2831).

Financial instruments. Loans, guarantees and equity under InvestEU never come from Brussels directly. They go through more than 1,000 partner banks, funds and investors that you find via access2finance (Enterprise Europe Brussels).

How a direct grant moves, in order:

  1. The Commission adopts a work programme listing topics and budgets.
  2. Each topic opens as a call on the portal with a fixed deadline (17:00 Brussels time in all the calls below).
  3. You submit through the portal, and independent experts score your proposal against published criteria.
  4. If you are selected, your organisation is validated, a LEAR is appointed, and you sign the grant agreement electronically.

For example, a company applying to the AI4SME cyber call submits by 14 January 2027, and evaluation is planned for February to April 2027 (ECCC call document via FFG). Plan your cash flow around a gap of several months between submission and money.

What each programme is for

EIC Accelerator. The 2026 budget is €634M: €414M for Accelerator Open and €220M for Challenges (Alien Technology Transfer summary of WP 2026). The minimum equity component went up from €0.5M in 2025 to €1M in 2026 (same source). This route suits companies that want investors and can show a path to scale. It is not for service businesses or projects that are still lab research.

Digital Europe. The programme funds the deployment of digital technology, not research. The ECCC's September 2026 round is worth €96M across seven topics, and €20M of it goes to AI4SME (ECCC). Note that the 10th DIGITAL round, worth about €45.4M, closed on 1 October 2026 (ONE Spain). If you were planning around it, wait for the next round.

Single Market Programme (SMP COSME). The 2026 SME calls have already closed. Tourism SMEs (SMP-COSME-2026-TOURSME-01) closed on 20 May 2026 and Agri-food Biotech Scaling-up on 2 June 2026 (EuroAccess; EISMEA). For the rest of 2026, the SMP mostly reaches SMEs through the Enterprise Europe Network and Erasmus for Young Entrepreneurs, not through new grant calls.

Horizon Europe Cluster 4. These calls usually need a consortium and a Part B written to the scoring criteria. If you are new to that format, read our Horizon Europe Proposal Template Part B: 2026 Scoring Map before drafting.

EU Funding for Small Business: Eligibility by Size and Country

Small-business eligibility depends on two things: the official SME definition and whether your country takes part in the specific programme. Being "small" under your national rules does not count.

Source: Commission SME definition, Recommendation 2003/361. The staff ceiling always applies. Then you need to pass only one of the two financial tests, either turnover or balance sheet (same source).

The Commission has also introduced a "small mid-cap" category for firms that have outgrown SME status (same source). The EIC Accelerator accepts small mid-caps with up to 499 employees, but for the investment component only (EIC).

One consequence trips people up. Cluster 4 is Pillar II, so a Canadian or Korean SME can join a Cluster 4 project. The EIC Accelerator sits in Pillar III, so the same company cannot apply to it from home. The UK, Switzerland, Norway and Ukraine are on the associated list for the whole of Horizon Europe (same sources).

Pitfalls and fixes

  • You count only your own figures. If another company holds a stake in yours, its headcount and finances may have to be added to your figures (Commission). Fix: map your shareholders before you start the SME self-assessment.
  • A venture-backed startup assumes it is automatically an SME. A large corporate shareholder can push you over the thresholds. Fix: read the linked and partner enterprise chapter of the SME User Guide, which is linked from the same Commission page.
  • You rely on an old associated-country list. Association agreements change. Fix: check the official PDF on the call date, not a blog post.

EU Grants for SMEs vs. Loans and Equity: Which Should You Apply For?

Apply for a grant if you have a risky innovation project and can wait months for a decision. Go for an EU-backed loan or guarantee through a local intermediary if you need working capital or investment money sooner.

How to decide:

  • Choose a grant if the project would not happen without public money and you can pay the uncovered share. At 75% funding, a €4M AI4SME project still leaves you €1M to fund yourself (example calculation).
  • Choose InvestEU-backed finance if the investment is commercially sound but your bank wants more collateral. The EU guarantee is designed to cover exactly that gap (EURA AG).
  • Choose EIC blended finance only if you are comfortable with the EIC Fund becoming a shareholder.

Pitfalls. Don't treat a grant as fast money: with the cyber calls, submission and signature are months apart. Also, access2finance does not lend anything itself. It lists intermediaries, and you apply directly to the institution you pick (Enterprise Europe Brussels).

EU Support for SMEs Beyond Grants: Advisory and Network Services

The cheapest EU support for SMEs is free advice. The Enterprise Europe Network gives partner matchmaking, internationalisation advice and funding guidance at no cost. It is active in more than 60 countries, with around 3,000 experts in more than 600 member organisations (Wikipedia summary; Lithuanian Innovation Centre, an EEN member).

What EEN can do for you:

  • Tell you which calls fit your company and country, including national schemes outside the EU portal.
  • Find consortium partners for Horizon or Digital Europe topics through its partnering database.
  • Review an application idea before you spend weeks writing it.

What it won't do: write your proposal for you. Its advisers are spread thin, so you need to come to them with a specific question.

Other no-cost routes:

  • National Contact Points (NCPs) for Horizon Europe and Digital Europe. They explain topic scope and eligibility. Flanders' NCP, for example, publishes notices about new Digital Europe calls (NCP Flanders).
  • Partner search on each topic page of the Funding & Tenders Portal, under "Partner search announcements".
  • Erasmus for Young Entrepreneurs, for founders and for experienced host entrepreneurs, with EU support towards travel and living costs (European Commission).

How to get EEN help:

  1. Open the Enterprise Europe Network site and find the local contact point for your region.
  2. Send a short email: your sector, headcount, TRL, and the call ID you are considering.
  3. Ask two questions: "Are we eligible?" and "Who else in the network is preparing a proposal on this topic?"

For example, a 30-person Polish cybersecurity firm could ask its local EEN office whether to apply to AI4SME alone or join a consortium. The call allows single applicants but says collaboration can strengthen implementation (EuroAccess).

EU Funding Requirements: Documents and Eligibility Checklist

Every direct EU call requires the same basics: an EU Login account, a 9-digit PIC, an SME self-assessment where SME status matters, and a proposal written to the topic text. Validation documents and a LEAR come later, only if you are selected.

Setup steps on the Funding & Tenders Portal

  1. Create an EU Login. On the portal, click "Login", then "Create an account". Use a work email that more than one person can reach if staff change.
  2. Search for an existing PIC. Under "How to participate" → "Participant Register", search your organisation's legal name first. Duplicate PICs cause delays (Funding & Tenders Online Manual).
  3. Register your organisation. The registration wizard asks for legal name, address, registration country and legal form. You get a 9-digit PIC straight away, but it stays provisional until validated (same source).
  4. Run the SME wizard. If you have no SME self-assessment from the last 2 years, the submission system prompts you to complete one. You enter headcount and financial figures for your last approved accounting period and for any linked enterprises (SME wizard guide; H2020 Online Manual).
  5. Prepare validation documents in advance. If you are selected, the Central Validation Service asks for proof of legal existence and, depending on the entity, financial or legal-status documents (Online Manual).
  6. Appoint a LEAR. Without a LEAR, you cannot update your legal entity data or sign contracts electronically (LEAR appointment).

Call-specific documents

  • Digital Europe AI4SME: Part B of at most 70 pages, plus a mandatory ownership control declaration (EuroAccess).
  • EIC Accelerator short proposal: a 12-page form, a 10-slide pitch deck and a 3-minute video (EIC).
  • EUIPO SME Fund: apply before you pay any IP fees, because paying first loses your right to reimbursement (marchistorici.com).

How to Check If Your SME Qualifies for a Specific 2026 Call

Open the topic page on the Funding & Tenders Portal, read the "Conditions and documents" section, and check it against your country, legal form, TRL and consortium setup before you write a word. This takes about an hour and can save you weeks of drafting.

Step-by-step check

  1. Go to the Funding & Tenders Portal → "Search Funding & Tenders" → "Calls for proposals".
  2. Under "Submission status", tick "Open for submission". Add "Forthcoming" if you want to plan ahead.
  3. Under "Programme", choose for example "Digital Europe Programme (DIGITAL)" or "Horizon Europe (HORIZON)". Or paste a topic ID such as DIGITAL-ECCC-2027-DEPLOY-CYBER-11-AI4SME into the search box.
  4. On the topic page, read "Topic description" for the expected outcomes and scope. If your project doesn't produce those outcomes, stop here.
  5. Open "Conditions and documents". Check:
  6. Admissibility: page limits (70 for AI4SME).
  7. Eligible countries: EU/EEA for AI4SME; EU plus associated countries for Horizon.
  8. Other eligibility: single applicant allowed or minimum consortium, and any ownership control rules.
  9. Financial and operational capacity: SMEs often have to show their accounts.
  10. Check the "Type of action". An Innovation Action (IA) such as "Proof of market" means near-market work. A Coordination & Support Action (CSA) such as MAT-PROD-47 funds networking and services, not your own product development.
  11. Check TRL fit. The EIC Accelerator expects TRL 6–8 for its innovation activities (EIC).
  12. Check timing against the deadline. Then run steps 1–4 of the requirements checklist above.

Example: three applicants, one afternoon

These are illustrations only.

  • A Norwegian cyber SME with 40 staff. It can apply to AI4SME alone, because the EEA is eligible and single applicants are allowed. Its maximum rate is 75%.
  • A Canadian AI startup. It cannot apply to the EIC Accelerator from Canada because of Pillar III. It could join a Cluster 4 "Proof of market" consortium, because Canada is associated to Pillar II, as long as the topic's own conditions allow it.
  • A Spanish university. "Unlocking academic IP" (MAT-PROD-42) or "Venture Builders Network" (MAT-PROD-50) are a better fit than SME-only instruments. Both close on 2 February 2027 (Commission).

A note on EIC timing

The 4 November 2026 cut-off is for full proposals, and you can only send one after your short proposal is approved. Short-proposal decisions take 4–6 weeks (EIC). If you start today, you will realistically be aiming for a 2027 cut-off. Those dates depend on the next work programme, so confirm them once it is published.

Next steps

Pick one call from the table above and confirm your eligibility for it this week. Leave the drafting until that check is done.

Do it yourself today:

  1. Create your EU Login and search the Participant Register for an existing PIC. Register if there is none.
  2. Run the SME self-assessment wizard with your last approved accounts and any linked-company data.
  3. Open the topic page of your chosen call, read "Conditions and documents", and write down any requirement you don't meet yet.

If you would rather not do it yourself:

  • Enterprise Europe Network and National Contact Points. Free. Good for eligibility checks and partner searches. Not for anyone who wants a proposal written for them, because they don't draft proposals.
  • Private grant consultancies. Most charge a fixed fee, a success fee, or both. There is no official price list, so ask for a written quote (as of October 2026; check each firm's terms). They suit teams with a strong project and no writing capacity. They don't suit very early ideas, because a consultant can't fix weak eligibility or a poor fit with the topic. No consultant can guarantee selection.
  • EIC Business Acceleration Services. Coaching and investor events for companies that already hold EIC funding. Not available before you are selected (EIC).

FAQ

What is the EU definition of an SME for funding purposes?

An SME has fewer than 250 staff and either turnover of €50M or less or a balance sheet of €43M or less, under Recommendation 2003/361. Micro firms are under 10 staff and small firms under 50, with lower financial ceilings. Figures from linked or partner enterprises may have to be added to yours (Commission).

Can non-EU companies apply for EU SME funding?

Yes, if their country is associated to the right programme or part of it. Horizon Europe associated countries such as the UK, Switzerland, Norway and Ukraine can access the EIC. Canada and South Korea are associated to Pillar II only. AI4SME is open to the EU and EEA, and the EUIPO SME Fund to the EU and Ukraine.

How much funding can an SME get from the EIC Accelerator?

A grant component below €2.5M as a lump sum, plus optional equity of €1M to €10M. Larger rounds are possible through STEP ScaleUp (EIC). The 2026 Accelerator budget is €634M in total.

Do I need a partner organization to apply for EU SME grants?

Not always. The EIC Accelerator is for single companies, and AI4SME allows single applicants. Many Horizon Europe Innovation Actions need a consortium, so read "Conditions and documents" on the topic page before you look for partners.

Is EU funding for SMEs taxable or does it affect state aid limits?

How a grant is taxed depends on your national law, so ask your accountant how it will be booked. Funding from national or regional schemes, including cohesion funds, often falls under de minimis, which caps aid at €300,000 per undertaking over three rolling years (EUR-Lex). If your project mixes national co-funding with an EU grant, check the state-aid position with your managing authority or national contact point (Irish DETE guidance).
