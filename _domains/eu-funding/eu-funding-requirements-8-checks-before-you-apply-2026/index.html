For most EU grants, and Horizon Europe in particular, the requirements come down to 8 checks: legal entity status, a PIC, the consortium minimum, operational capacity, financial capacity, SME status where it matters, ownership and control rules for non-EU participants, and the conditions written into the call topic. Miss one and your proposal can be ruled inadmissible or ineligible, or it can stall at grant signature. Do the checks in that order before anyone starts writing Part B.

Key points

  • RIA/IA consortia need 3 entities in 3 different countries
  • Coordinators asking ≥€500,000 face a financial capacity check
  • SME = under 250 staff plus ≤€50M turnover or ≤€43M balance sheet

Best for: First-time and repeat EU grant applicants

What are the EU grant requirements to check before you apply?

Each of the 8 checks is tested at a different stage, and missing each one has a different consequence. Most of them come from Regulation (EU) 2021/695, the Horizon Europe Programme Guide and the Funding & Tenders Portal Online Manual.

The most common pitfall is reading only the General Annexes and skipping the topic text. A topic can add conditions, for example a required partner type, a cap on the requested amount or a restriction under Article 22(5). Read the topic's "Topic conditions and documents" section first. If you want a page-by-page view of the proposal itself, see /horizon-europe-proposal-template-part-b-2026-scoring-map/.

Who is eligible as a legal entity for EU funding?

Any legal entity established in an EU Member State or an associated country can generally take part, whether it is a company, university, research organisation, NGO or public body. Under Horizon Europe, a "legal entity" is a natural or legal person created and recognised under national, EU or international law, with legal personality and the capacity to hold rights and obligations (Regulation (EU) 2021/695, Art. 2).

Taking part and getting funded are different things. As of November 2026, the main cases are:

For example, an Australian university can join a consortium as an associated partner. It contributes work, but its costs are covered by its own resources or national funding. It does not count towards the minimum of three eligible entities.

Pitfalls and fixes:

  • Branch offices. A branch usually has no legal personality of its own, so the parent entity registers instead. Fix: register the parent and report the branch address in the proposal.
  • Affiliated entities. A subsidiary that does part of the work can be declared as an affiliated entity, but it does not count as a separate partner for the consortium minimum. Fix: count only independent beneficiaries when you check the minimum.
  • Sanctions. Entities under EU restrictive measures are excluded. Fix: check every partner on the EU Sanctions Map.

How to get and validate a PIC (Participant Identification Code)?

Register your organisation in the Funding & Tenders Portal Participant Register to get a 9-digit PIC. The Central Validation Service checks it later, usually when you are invited to grant preparation (Online Manual). You need the PIC to submit. Validation and the LEAR appointment must be finished before the grant can be signed.

Steps (portal labels as of November 2026):

  1. Create or sign in to your EU Login account. For help with sign-in, see /eu-funding-and-tenders-portal-two-factor-authentication-2026/.
  2. Open the Participant Register and click Search for an organisation first. Many universities and companies already have a PIC, so do not create a second one.
  3. If none exists, click Register your organisation. Enter the legal name, legal form, registration number, VAT number and legal address exactly as they appear on your official documents.
  4. Complete the SME self-assessment if you are a private company (see the SME section below).
  5. Submit the registration to receive your PIC. Validation comes later (step 6).
  6. When validation is requested, upload the documents under My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details. Typically these are a registration extract and a VAT document, plus the LEAR appointment letter signed by your legal representative.

The full flow is covered in /eu-funding-and-tenders-portal-registration-pic-in-6-steps/ and /eu-funding-and-tenders-portal-participant-register-5-steps/.

Pitfalls and fixes:

  • The person who registered leaves. That person is the "self-registrant", and nobody else can edit the record. Fix: appoint a LEAR early, because the LEAR controls access roles.
  • Data mismatches. If the legal name in the register differs from the registration extract, validation is delayed. Fix: copy the exact name, including the legal form ("GmbH", "S.L.").
  • Validation at the last minute. If you are invited to sign and validation is still open, everything slows down. Fix: respond to Central Validation Service requests within the deadline they give. If you are stuck, open a ticket (/eu-funding-and-tenders-portal-helpdesk-ticket-in-5-steps/).

What is the minimum consortium size for Horizon Europe?

Research and Innovation Actions (RIA) and Innovation Actions (IA) need at least 3 independent legal entities. Each must be in a different Member State or associated country, and at least one must be in a Member State (Regulation (EU) 2021/695, Art. 22(2)). Many other action types allow a single applicant, so the topic text decides.

For example, a German SME, a Spanish university and a UK research institute meet the RIA minimum, because the UK is an associated country. A German SME, a Spanish university and a US university do not. The US partner can still join, but it does not count, so you need one more eligible partner.

Pitfalls and fixes:

  • Two partners under the same control. "Independent" means the entities do not control each other and are not controlled by the same third party. A university and its spin-off that it majority-owns count as one. Fix: check the ownership links before you count.
  • Topics that raise the bar. Some topics require 5 or more partners, or specific types such as cities or end users. Fix: copy the topic's eligibility conditions into your partner tracker.
  • A partner drops out after submission. If the consortium falls below the minimum, the proposal becomes ineligible. Fix: build in a margin of one extra eligible partner where the work plan allows it.

How to prove operational and financial capacity?

Operational capacity is shown in the proposal through the skills, track record and resources of your team. Financial capacity is a separate check during grant preparation. In Horizon Europe it normally applies only to coordinators who request €500,000 or more (EU CAP Network coordinator briefing, 2025; Online Manual, Financial capacity assessment).

Operational capacity

Horizon Europe does not ask for a separate capacity form. Evaluators assess it through Part B, mainly the "Quality and capacity of the consortium" content under Implementation, and the participant information in Part A. Show who does what by naming the key staff, their relevant publications, products or projects, and the significant infrastructure or equipment they will use. If a partner has none of these for a task, that weakens the score.

Financial capacity

The Online Manual's general rule exempts public bodies, international organisations and individual requested amounts of €60,000 or less. It also notes that Horizon Europe applies stricter limits of its own (Online Manual). In practice, as of November 2026:

Coordinator that is a public body or higher/secondary education establishmentNo (EU CAP Network)
Ordinary beneficiary (not coordinator)Normally no, though the Commission may check if there are concerns

Steps to prepare:

  1. Run the financial capacity self-check simulator with your last closed accounts. It is only a simulation, and the Online Manual says it does not cover every programme.
  2. Gather the profit and loss account and balance sheet for the last 2 closed financial years. Larger requests may also need an external audit report (Online Manual).
  3. When the Central Validation Service asks, upload them under My Organisations > Actions > View organisation details > Financial capacity.

The Commission scores liquidity, financial autonomy, solvency and profitability. It also looks at dependence on EU funding and any deficits from previous years. If the result is weak, the outcome can be reduced or staged prefinancing, a guarantee, joint liability, replacement of the coordinator, or rejection. Horizon Europe uses the Mutual Insurance Mechanism, so prefinancing guarantees are normally not required (Online Manual).

Pitfall: a young startup with no closed financial year that wants to coordinate a €2 million RIA. Fix: let an established partner, such as a university (exempt), coordinate, and give the startup a lead role in a work package.

How to check SME status for EU funding?

An SME has fewer than 250 staff and either annual turnover up to €50 million or a balance sheet total up to €43 million. Data from partner and linked enterprises counts towards these limits (Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC). You declare it through the SME self-assessment in the Participant Register.

The staff limit is mandatory. For the financial limits you only need to meet one of the two (Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex Art. 2).

How the counting works, following the EU SME User Guide:

  1. Autonomous: no other enterprise holds 25% or more of you, and you hold less than 25% of any other. Use only your own figures.
  2. Partner enterprise: a holding between 25% and 50% in either direction. Add that share of the other enterprise's figures to yours.
  3. Linked enterprise: more than 50%, or other forms of control. Add 100% of its figures.

There is one more rule. If one or more public bodies control 25% or more of the capital or voting rights, the enterprise is generally not an SME, whatever its size (Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Art. 3(4)).

For example, a 40-person startup in which a 600-employee corporate holds 55% is linked to that corporate. Combined, they have more than 250 staff, so the startup is not an SME and cannot apply to the EIC Accelerator as one.

Pitfalls and fixes:

  • Venture capital investors. Some investors, such as business angels and venture capital funds, can hold 25% to 50% without making you a partner enterprise, but only under the conditions set in Art. 3(2) of the Recommendation. Fix: check those conditions with your cap table in front of you.
  • Using the wrong year. Figures come from the last closed accounting period. Your status changes only if you exceed or fall below the thresholds for 2 consecutive years (Recommendation 2003/361/EC, Annex Art. 4).
  • Old declarations. Update the self-assessment after a funding round that changes your ownership.

The SME schemes this unlocks are listed in /eu-funding-for-smes-7-open-2026-calls-and-how-to-qualify/.

Can non-EU organisations apply? Ownership and control rules explained

Yes. Entities in associated countries are treated like EU entities within the scope of their association. Other third-country entities can join, but are usually not funded unless the topic allows it. On top of that, topics restricted under Article 22(5) can exclude entities controlled from non-eligible countries (Regulation (EU) 2021/695; EC research security page).

Ownership and control

Article 22(5) lets a Work Programme limit certain topics, for example in quantum, space or some digital areas, to entities established in Member States and some associated countries. It can also exclude entities established there but controlled from elsewhere. Control is assessed up to the ultimate owners. It counts even when the owner does not actually use its influence, as long as it could (French Horizon Europe practical sheet, July 2026).

Steps:

  1. Open the topic page and search for "Article 22(5)" or "strategic assets" in the eligibility conditions.
  2. If you find it, map each partner's shareholders up to the ultimate owner.
  3. During grant preparation, complete the Declaration of ownership and control requested through the portal. The procedure is described in the Online Manual, Special procedures: Ownership control check.
  4. If the result is "controlled", the entity is excluded or, where the topic permits, must provide guarantees approved by its country of establishment.

Pitfall: the EU subsidiary of a US or Chinese group joins a restricted quantum topic. It is established in the EU, but it may still be ineligible. Fix: run the control mapping before you put the partner in the budget.

EU funding guidelines vs regulations: which documents apply to your application?

Regulations and the Model Grant Agreement are binding. The Programme Guide and Online Manual are guidance that explains how the rules are applied. Read the topic text first and the guidance second. Where the two conflict, the binding text wins.

EU funding guidelines vs regulations: which documents apply to your application?. Source: EU Funding & Tenders Portal
DocumentBinding?What it governs
Regulation (EU) 2021/695YesHorizon Europe itself and its rules for participation (Title II of the same regulation, not a separate act)
Financial Regulation (EU, Euratom) 2024/2509YesGeneral grant rules: exclusion, financial capacity, award
Work Programme + General AnnexesYes, for that callEligible countries, admissibility, page limits, topic conditions
Model Grant Agreement (annotated version for interpretation)Yes, once signedCosts, reporting, obligations
Programme GuideNo (guidance)Explains the Horizon Europe rules
Online ManualNo (guidance)Portal procedures: registration, submission, validation

Reading order for a real application:

  1. The topic page: "Topic conditions and documents", budget and deadline.
  2. The General Annexes of the current Work Programme, linked from that topic.
  3. The proposal template linked from the submission system. Compare RIA and CSA in /horizon-europe-csa-proposal-template-7-differences-from-ria/.
  4. The Programme Guide, for interpretation.
  5. The Online Manual, for the portal steps.

All of these are collected on the portal's Reference documents page. Always download the version linked from your topic, because the General Annexes change between Work Programmes.

Next steps

Record the results of the 8 checks in one sheet with a row per partner. Use these columns: country and association status, PIC, independence, SME status, control, and coordinator yes/no. If the coordinator requests €500,000 or more, add a financial capacity column too.

If you want help:

  • National Contact Points (NCPs). Free advice paid for by national governments, and a good fit for first-time applicants. They will not write your proposal, and how much they can help varies by country. Find yours via the NCP search (free as of November 2026; check the official page).
  • Enterprise Europe Network. Free partner search and guidance for SMEs through the EEN. It is less suited to universities that need detailed help with legal eligibility.
  • Private grant consultants. These suit teams with no in-house grants office that are aiming for large or single-applicant schemes. There is no official price list. Fees and success-fee terms vary, so get a written quote and check whether the consultant only writes or also handles eligibility. They are not worth it for small CSAs where an NCP review is enough.

FAQ

Can UK organisations apply for Horizon Europe funding?

Yes. The UK has been associated since 1 January 2024, so UK entities apply and are funded on the same terms as EU entities and count towards the consortium minimum. The one exclusion is the EIC Fund (the equity part of the EIC), according to the EPRS briefing.

Do universities and NGOs need a PIC to apply?

Yes. Every beneficiary and affiliated entity needs a PIC (Online Manual). Many universities already have a PIC, so search the Participant Register before you register, which avoids creating a duplicate.

Do I need a consortium to apply for EU funding?

Only for some action types. RIA and IA require at least 3 independent entities in 3 different Member States or associated countries (Regulation (EU) 2021/695, Art. 22(2)). ERC grants, MSCA Postdoctoral Fellowships, the EIC Accelerator and many CSAs accept a single applicant, as set out in their Work Programmes and the General Annexes, available on the Reference documents page.

Can a startup or a single SME get EU funding on its own?

Yes, through schemes such as the EIC Accelerator, which funds single SMEs, including startups, that meet the definition in Recommendation 2003/361/EC. Check your SME status with linked and partner enterprises included before you apply, because a majority-owning corporate group can disqualify you. The EIC Accelerator is not for everyone. Selection is competitive, so expect a demanding proposal. It is closed to large companies and other non-SMEs. It also suits innovations heading towards market rather than teams still at the early research stage, which should look at EIC Pathfinder or ERC instead.
