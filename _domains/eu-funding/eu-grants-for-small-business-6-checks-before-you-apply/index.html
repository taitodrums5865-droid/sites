EU grants for small business come by two routes. Some programmes let a company apply directly on the EU Funding & Tenders Portal: Horizon Europe (which includes the EIC Accelerator), LIFE and Digital Europe. Others are open to you only through a national or regional body (ERDF, ESF+) or through cascade funding (FSTP), where a project that already holds an EU grant runs its own open call. Work out which route an opportunity uses before you write anything, because that decides where you apply, whose rules apply and how much money is on offer.

Key points

  • EIC Accelerator grant: up to €2.5M, equity €1M–€10M
  • Horizon Europe cascade default cap: €60,000 per third party
  • ERDF/ESF+ money is reached via national or regional bodies

Best for: SMEs and startups looking for EU grants they can actually apply to

What type of grants are available for small businesses from the EU?

There is no single EU grant for small businesses. EU money for SMEs arrives as direct grants, as grants run through member states, as cascade sub-grants, or as loans and equity that go through banks. Before you go further, put every opportunity you find into one of the rows below.

The distinction matters because many lists of "EU grants" mix these rows together. A tender is a purchase: you're paid to deliver what the EU asked for, and you have no grant agreement. A guaranteed loan has to be repaid. EIC equity means the EIC Fund takes shares in your company. The EIC Accelerator page describes equity of €1M–€10M sitting next to the grant part.

Most EU grants also co-finance. They pay a percentage of eligible costs, and you cover the rest yourself.

Which EU grants can a small business apply to directly?

A registered SME can apply directly to Horizon Europe (including the EIC Accelerator), LIFE and Digital Europe calls on the Funding & Tenders Portal. Depending on the call you apply alone or in a consortium. The programme summary never settles eligibility. The topic page and the call document do.

EIC Accelerator: the single-applicant route

The EIC Accelerator suits a company that has a working prototype and wants to reach market. As of November 2026, the EIC page says it is open to SMEs established in a member state or associated country. Natural persons who intend to set up an SME can also apply. Small mid-caps with up to 499 employees can get investment-only support. Grant-only support can be awarded only once during Horizon Europe (2021–2027). The short proposal can be sent at any time and has three parts: a 12-page form, a pitch deck of up to 10 slides and a video of up to 3 minutes. The page lists full-proposal cut-offs for 2027 on 20 January, 17 March, 1 September and 4 November 2027, each at 17:00 Brussels time. Our EIC Accelerator deadlines guide explains how the two stages fit together.

One limitation: the Accelerator does not suit service businesses, resellers or companies doing incremental upgrades. The evaluators fund high-risk innovation. For example, an SME building a new type of battery management chip that has been validated in a pilot plant (TRL 6) fits the profile. A web agency that wants to add AI features to its existing offer generally does not.

Horizon Europe collaborative topics

Most Horizon Europe topics need a consortium. As an SME, you join as a partner and get a share of the budget. Funding rates are fixed per action type (FFG). A for-profit SME in an Innovation Action receives 70% of eligible costs. In a Research and Innovation Action it receives 100%. Our RIA template guide covers the proposal format.

LIFE and Digital Europe

LIFE's 2026 calls opened with about €601 million for climate and environment projects (EY). Digital Europe is less well known among SMEs. In its SME support actions an SME receives 75% while other partners receive 50% (Luxinnovation). Simple grants pay 50% whatever the company's size. Check which type of action your topic is before you build the budget.

UK companies can take part in Horizon Europe, but only in the EIC Accelerator's grant-only scheme, not the equity part (ABGI).

EU grants vs cascade funding (FSTP): what is the difference?

A direct EU grant means you sign a grant agreement with an EU funding body. Cascade funding (FSTP) means you sign with a consortium that already holds an EU grant and passes on smaller amounts. In the cascade case you apply to the consortium's open call and follow its rules, but the EU grant rules still set the upper limits.

The €60,000 rule

In Horizon Europe the default maximum is €60,000 per third party unless the call says otherwise (UFM, Denmark). The cap applies to each recipient, not to each sub-project. So if you win in two rounds of the same project's open call, your combined total is what counts against the limit. A higher amount is allowed only where the work programme justifies it as necessary for the action, so the call text of the parent project is the document to check (see the Horizon Europe Model Grant Agreement).

How to apply to a cascade call

  1. Open the Funding & Tenders Portal, go to Search Funding & Tenders and filter for third-party (cascade) calls. Our cascade filter guide shows the exact filters.
  2. Click through to the consortium's own call page and download its Guide for Applicants. Its eligibility rules can be narrower than the EU's. Some are limited to certain countries, sectors or company ages.
  3. Check whether a single cap applies per company across all rounds. If you already got money from the same project, that amount counts against the cap.
  4. Apply through the consortium's platform, which is often an external tool such as F6S, not the EU portal. Save a copy of every answer, because these platforms rarely let you edit after the deadline.

Cascade calls make a good first step. They are short, they move faster than direct calls and they give you a track record. They are a poor fit if you need more than about €60,000, because one sub-grant won't fund a full product launch.

How do I qualify for an EU small business grant?

To qualify, you must meet the EU SME definition, be a legal entity in an eligible country, and meet the call's topic, consortium and budget conditions. Run the six checks below in order. If any one fails, the opportunity is out.

  1. SME status. Under Recommendation 2003/361/EC, an SME has fewer than 250 staff and either turnover of no more than €50 million or a balance sheet total of no more than €43 million. Data from partner and linked enterprises is included in the calculation. A 40-person startup that is 60% owned by a large corporate is usually not an SME. The 2020 user guide has worked examples. The portal also has an SME self-assessment wizard, which you complete during registration.
  2. Country eligibility. Find your country in the "Eligible countries" section of the call document. Member states are always eligible. Associated countries depend on the programme and sometimes on the part of the programme: the UK, for example, can get the EIC grant only.
  3. Topic fit. Read the topic's "Expected outcome" and "Scope" text. A strong company that doesn't match the scope is rejected at evaluation, and no eligibility check will warn you first.
  4. Single applicant or consortium. Check the "Admissibility and eligibility" conditions. They state the minimum number of partners and any country requirements for that topic. The EIC Accelerator and some LIFE topics accept one applicant.
  5. Funding rate and co-financing. Work out your own share. For example, a €500,000 Innovation Action budget at 70% leaves you to finance €150,000 from other sources. Make sure that cash will be available during the project, not only on paper.
  6. Financial and operational capacity. The EU checks whether you can carry the project. For larger grants, that can mean submitting accounts. A company with negative equity, or one founded last month, should plan for questions on this.

If you want a broader checklist covering eight requirement types, see our EU funding requirements guide. Our SME calls guide lists open calls that pass these checks.

How to get grants for your small business: the application route step by step

The route is: register on the Funding & Tenders Portal, get a PIC, have your organisation validated, choose an open topic, find partners if you need them, and submit through the portal before the deadline. Start registration weeks ahead. Validation can't be rushed in the final days.

  1. Create an EU Login account. Go to the Funding & Tenders Portal, click Login and then Create an account. Add two-factor authentication now. Our 2FA guide covers the methods.
  2. Register your organisation. Under How to participate → Participant Register, click Register your organisation. Before you start, search the register for your VAT or registration number, because a colleague may already have created a PIC. Duplicate PICs cause validation delays. The PIC guide walks through each screen.
  3. Prepare for validation. The Central Validation Service asks for legal documents and the appointment of a LEAR (Legal Entity Appointed Representative). Upload company registry extracts that are current and legible. You can submit a proposal before validation, but the grant can't be signed without it.
  4. Find a topic. In Search Funding & Tenders, filter Calls for proposals by Programme, with the status set to Open for submission or Forthcoming. Open the topic page and download the call document and the work programme section.
  5. Find partners if the topic needs them. Topic pages include a Partner Search tab. You can publish your own profile or look at organisations that have flagged interest.
  6. Start the submission. On the topic page, click Start submission, choose the type of action and enter the PICs. Download the Part B template, and fill in Part A (administrative forms) online.
  7. Submit early, then check. Upload Part B as a PDF, click Submit and look for the confirmation in My Proposals. You can resubmit as often as you like until the deadline, and only the last version counts, so send a complete version a day early.

For example, if a three-person startup aims at an EIC Accelerator cut-off, it should register its PIC first and only then begin the short proposal. The short proposal is submitted through the portal too, so a missing PIC holds up everything after it.

EU funding for small businesses in Ireland, Cyprus and Malta: where do you apply?

Direct EU calls are the same in every member state. Advice, co-funding and the ERDF and ESF+ funds, however, are handled by national or regional bodies. In each country, find the National Contact Point (NCP) for the programme you want and the managing authority for cohesion funds.

Ireland

Irish SMEs that want to try Horizon Europe or the EIC should contact Enterprise Ireland's NCP team first. For investment support financed by ERDF, the managing authority depends on your region. The Southern Regional Assembly runs the Southern, Eastern and Midland programme. The Northern and Western Regional Assembly runs its own region's programme, and its schemes are delivered through local authorities and state agencies. Check which region your registered address falls in before you contact either body.

Cyprus

Cyprus puts both routes in one place. The Funding Programmes Portal lists THALIA calls for enterprises alongside RIF's support for Horizon Europe. Questions about RIF's Horizon Europe support can go to support@research.org.cy (RIF). Recent reports name the Ministry of Finance's Directorate General for Growth as THALIA's managing authority (CBN). Confirm this on the portal, because older pages use previous names.

Malta

In Malta, Xjenza Malta hosts the Horizon Europe NCP contact, horizon.malta@gov.mt (EBN). Some older MCST pages still name the previous body, so ignore those. For ERDF, the SME Enhance scheme merged earlier SME and start-up schemes into one. It offers grants from €10,000 to €128,400 and was reported as running until 31 December 2026, subject to funds (Funds for Companies listing). Check the closing date with the administering body before you plan around it.

To find the NCP in any other country, use the portal's NCP search. Choose your country and programme, then email the contact a short paragraph about the project and the topic ID.

Next steps

You can do the first three things today, on your own.

  1. Put the opportunities you're considering into the route table at the top of this guide. Any opportunity that turns out to be a loan or a tender goes on a separate list.
  2. Search the Participant Register for your company's PIC. If you don't have one, start registering now.
  3. Run the six qualification checks on one live topic. Write down your co-financing share in euros, not as a percentage.

Not everyone wants to write the application themselves. Here are the main sources of help:

  • National Contact Points. The portal's NCP search lists the contact points for each country and programme. Ask them about topic fit, partner search and procedure, and ask up front what support they offer. They don't write your proposal, so they suit teams that have time to write but lack experience. The EIC also offers an optional business coach during the short-proposal stage (EIC).
  • Private grant consultants. They may charge a fixed fee, a success fee or both. Rates vary widely, so get a written quote and check what happens to the fee if the proposal isn't funded. Consultants suit companies short on time that are aiming at €500k+ grants. They are a poor fit for €60,000 cascade calls, where the fee can take up much of the award. A consultant can't fix weak topic fit.

FAQ

What are government grants for small businesses?

They are non-repayable payments from a public body that cover part of a project's costs. In the EU, some come straight from EU programmes such as the EIC Accelerator. Others are EU-funded but run by national bodies, such as ERDF schemes like Malta's SME Enhance. Purely national grants funded only from a country's own budget also exist and follow national rules.

Is there a list of grants for small businesses?

The Funding & Tenders Portal is the official, searchable list of direct EU calls and many cascade calls. ERDF and ESF+ schemes are published on national portals, such as Cyprus's Funding Programmes Portal or the Irish regional assemblies' sites. No single official list covers every route.

Is EU funding for small business available to startups with no partners?

Yes. The EIC Accelerator accepts single SMEs, and natural persons who intend to found one can apply too (EIC). Some LIFE topics and many cascade calls also accept one applicant. Most collaborative Horizon Europe topics still need a consortium.

How many companies get EIC Accelerator support in one round?

In June 2026 the EIC announced that 38 start-ups and SMEs had secured support in the latest round of the EIC Accelerator (EIC news). If you are planning for a later round, the EIC Accelerator page lists the 2027 full-proposal cut-offs on 20 January, 17 March, 1 September and 4 November 2027.

Can a small business get both an EU grant and national funding for the same costs?

Check the rules on both sides before you combine them. For a Horizon Europe grant, the rules on which costs are eligible are set out in the Horizon Europe Model Grant Agreement, and a national scheme sets its own conditions. Ask the national body in writing whether its money can sit alongside your EU grant before you build the budget.
