HORIZON-CL6-2027-02-CLIMATE-01-two-stage opens on 4 February 2027. Stage 1 closes on 8 April 2027, stage 2 closes on 14 September 2027, and the topic has €11 million for about 2 projects of around €5.5 million each (REA; Innovate UK Business Connect). Stage 1 Part B is limited to 10 pages. Invited full proposals are limited to 40 pages, or 45 with lump-sum funding (NCP Flanders).

Key points

  • Stage 1 closes 8 April 2027; stage 2 closes 14 September 2027
  • €11M topic budget, about €5.5M each for 2 projects
  • Stage 1 Part B is capped at 10 pages; full proposals at 40 or 45

What is HORIZON-CL6-2027-02-CLIMATE-01 and who can apply?

It is a Cluster 6 open topic that funds water-resilience solutions you can demonstrate. Consortia of legal entities from EU Member States and Associated Countries can apply. The full title is "Open topic: Innovative solutions for the European Water Resilience Strategy". It sits under the destination "Land, ocean and water for climate action".

The REA destination page lists the call HORIZON-CL6-2027-02-two-stage with one topic. The CORDIS topic record groups the scope under the three objectives of the European Water Resilience Strategy. Your proposal can address one or more of them:

  • Restore and protect the water cycle. This covers keeping water in natural ecosystems, restoring river habitats, preparing for water scarcity, droughts and floods, and better hydrological monitoring and data.
  • Build a water-smart economy. This covers water-efficiency indicators by sector, water pricing and certification systems, the "water efficiency first" principle, and more water reuse and recycling.
  • Ensure clean and affordable water for all. This covers public engagement in basin management, fair access for vulnerable groups, and ways to measure water footprints.

Who it fits. The topic requires a multi-actor approach. It names stakeholders such as "farmers, water intensive industries, energy producers, land managers, water governance bodies and local authorities" (CORDIS). A typical split of roles:

  • Universities and research institutes usually lead the method work.
  • SMEs bring technology for the demonstration sites.
  • NGOs, utilities and municipalities act as end users and co-designers.

A hypothetical example. A consortium could bring together:

  • a university hydrology group
  • an SME that makes soil-moisture sensors
  • two farmer cooperatives
  • a river basin authority
  • a consumer NGO

Together they would test drought-retention measures in two pilot catchments.

Pitfalls to avoid:

  • Writing a desk study. Proposals should explain how they will demonstrate, test or validate their tools and solutions (CORDIS).
  • Duplicating related programmes. Show complementarity with the Water4All Partnership and with the relevant EU Missions. Name the specific projects you build on, not only the programmes.
  • Leaving out the collaboration task. Funded projects must include a task to collaborate with the other projects funded under this topic (same source). Budget person-months for it.

Check the exact time. Horizon Europe deadlines normally close at 17:00:00 Brussels time, and the system rejects late proposals (Online Manual). The "Deadline" field on the portal topic page is the only date that binds you. If it changes, follow the portal.

Don't confuse this call with the single-stage one. REA lists a separate single-stage call, HORIZON-CL6-2027-02-CLIMATE. It opens on 20 April 2027 and closes on 23 September 2027 (REA). Its dates don't apply to this topic.

How to plan your time. Only about nine weeks pass between the call opening and the stage 1 deadline. Agree the concept and the partner list before the call opens, and work from the published work programme text.

Criteri.ai notes that the 2026–2027 template has lower page limits than earlier versions. If you reuse a Part B written before 2026, you will have to cut content, not just tighten the formatting.

Which full-proposal limit applies? It depends on whether the topic uses lump-sum funding. This article has not confirmed that against the work programme. Check the conditions table in the Cluster 6 PDF and "Topic conditions and documents" on the portal before you plan the page budget. For a section-by-section split, see the 40-page section map.

Common mistakes and fixes:

  • Pages over the limit. Evaluators are told to disregard excess pages (General Annexes). Fix: count pages in the exported PDF, not in the Word file.
  • Names or logos in stage 1. An applicant summary reports that the abstract and Part B must not contain organisation names, acronyms, logos or staff names, including in titles and figures (MIC). Confirm this in the stage 1 template. Fix: write descriptions such as "Partner 3 (a regional water utility)".
  • A template from another call. Fix: download Part B through "Start submission" for this topic only.

About €5.5 million is not a hard cap. With only €11 million available, though, a much larger request needs a strong justification. Fix the budget split between partners before stage 1, because stage 2 must not differ substantially from what you submitted.

How many teams reach stage 2? The stage 1 threshold is set so that invited proposals request close to three times the available budget, and never less than two times (NCP Flanders). With €11 million available, that is about €22–33 million of requests, or roughly 4–6 consortia competing for 2 grants. This is an illustration of the rule, not an official forecast.

What this means for an SME. If the topic is an IA, the EU covers 70% of a for-profit partner's eligible costs and you cover the rest. Check that your cash flow can carry that gap for the full project before you commit.

What lump sum means, if it applies. You still submit a detailed budget estimate at stage 2. Payments are then tied to completed work packages, not to reported actual costs (General Annexes). Cost reporting is lighter. The downside is that a work package you don't complete may not be paid, so plan deliverables you can realistically finish.

How does the two-stage evaluation process work?

At stage 1, evaluators score only Excellence and Impact, and each needs at least 4 out of 5. An overall threshold also applies, normally 8 or 8.5 out of 10. Only invited consortia submit a full stage 2 proposal, which is scored on all three criteria (NCP Flanders).

Steps on the portal:

  1. Get PICs. Every partner needs a PIC. If yours doesn't have one, follow the registration guide.
  2. Open the topic. In the Funding & Tenders Portal, go to "Search Funding & Tenders", search for "HORIZON-CL6-2027-02-CLIMATE-01-two-stage" and open the topic.
  3. Create the draft. Click "Start submission", enter your acronym and summary, and add partners by PIC.
  4. Complete the forms. Fill in Part A and upload the 10-page Part B as a PDF, following the template's anonymisation instructions.
  5. Submit before the stage 1 deadline. You can resubmit before the deadline. The last version counts (Online Manual).
  6. Wait for the result. Successful applicants get generalised feedback at this point, and the stage 1 ESR only at the end of the procedure (NCP Flanders). Track the status in "My Proposals" (status guide).
  7. Submit stage 2 if invited. Keep the concept, objectives, expected impact and coordinator unchanged.

What you can and can't change between stages:

  • Allowed: one or two stage 1 partners dropping out, and other changes to the consortium, because the consortium is only assessed at stage 2.
  • Not allowed: a different coordinator, a completely new concept or methodology, or significantly changed objectives (same source). A substantially different stage 2 proposal is inadmissible.

Pitfalls with fixes:

  • Leaving stage 2 until after the invitation. If the result arrives in early July, you have about ten summer weeks. Fix: draft the work packages, Gantt chart and risk table in May and June.
  • Neglecting Impact at stage 1. Impact is half of the stage 1 score. Fix: link each measure to one of the three strategy objectives. The Part B scoring map shows what evaluators check.

What are the eligibility and consortium requirements?

The default Horizon Europe rule applies. You need at least three independent legal entities from three different EU Member States or Associated Countries, and at least one must be from a Member State (General Annexes).

Topic-specific conditions:

  • Multi-actor approach. Relevant stakeholders must be involved. You also need a plan for uptake and upscaling at local, national and EU level after the project (CORDIS).
  • JRC. The Joint Research Centre can support the consortium with expertise on droughts and floods and on the Copernicus Emergency Management Service (CORDIS). An applicant summary adds that the JRC doesn't help prepare proposals and can join as a beneficiary with zero funding or as an associated partner (MIC).
  • Satellite data. The same summary reports that if you use satellite data, Copernicus and/or Galileo/EGNOS must be the primary source.
  • China. The same summary reports that legal entities established in China are not eligible. This article has not confirmed it against the work programme. Check the topic's "Eligibility conditions" if any partner has links to China.

A minimum valid consortium could be a Spanish university, a Dutch SME and a utility from a third Member State. Competitive €5.5 million demonstration consortia are usually much larger.

Next steps

Start with two tasks you can do today, then decide whether you need outside help.

Do it yourself today:

  1. Confirm that every partner has a validated PIC.
  2. Write a one-page concept that links your solution to one strategy objective. Name your pilot site, your end users and your current TRL.

If you'd rather not do it alone:

  • National Contact Points. Official national advisers who can check rules and drafts (NCP finder). They don't write proposals.
  • Enterprise Europe Network. Help for SMEs looking for partners (EEN). It is less useful if you need writing support.
  • Private consultants. Ask for a written quote that sets out the scope of work and the fee structure. They suit consortia with no staff time for the bid. They are a poor fit if your team can't explain its own innovation. No one can guarantee an invitation to stage 2.

FAQ

What templates are required for stage 1 vs stage 2 submission?

Stage 1 uses the first-stage Part B template, which covers Excellence and Impact only. Stage 2 uses the full-proposal template for the topic's type of action, in its standard or lump-sum version (NCP Flanders). Download both inside the submission system for this topic. The page limits are in the table above.

Can SMEs apply alone to this topic or do they need a consortium?

No, an SME can't apply alone. The minimum is three independent legal entities from three different EU Member States or Associated Countries, including at least one Member State (General Annexes). For funding you can get as a single company, see our SME calls guide.

What happens if I miss the stage 1 deadline?

You can't take part in stage 2. Only consortia invited after stage 1 can submit, and the portal rejects late proposals (Online Manual). If you hit a technical problem, open a helpdesk ticket before the deadline and keep the evidence.

Is there a resubmission option if rejected at stage 1?

No. A proposal rejected at stage 1 can't be resubmitted to stage 2 of this call. If you think the evaluation contained a procedural error, you can request an evaluation review within the time limit stated in your evaluation result letter (Online Manual). A review examines how the evaluation was carried out. It does not reassess the evaluators' judgement of your proposal's merits. Otherwise, you can rework the idea for a later call with a matching topic.
