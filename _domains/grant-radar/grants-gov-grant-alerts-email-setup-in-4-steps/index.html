The government doesn't offer true real-time grant alerts. Grants.gov does send free email alerts, and the classic site describes them as a nightly email update. They cover new opportunities, searches you save and specific listings you follow. Setup takes four steps: sign in, run a filtered search, save it, and subscribe to each opportunity you plan to apply for.

Key points

  • Grants.gov alert emails are free and need only a free account
  • Saved-search emails don't report package changes to a NOFO
  • A forecast is not guaranteed to become a posted opportunity

Best for: First-time and repeat federal grant seekers

How to set up grant alerts on Grants.gov in 4 steps

You can get free federal grant alerts by email when you save a search or subscribe to specific opportunities on Grants.gov. Both need a free account. One thing to know first: Grants.gov now has two interfaces, and the steps are different in each.

On December 23, 2025, Simpler.Grants.gov became the default search experience, and the classic search is still available after you log in to Grants.gov. Simpler.Grants.gov uses a Login.gov account. Classic Grants.gov uses its own Grants.gov account, which is also the account you'll apply with.

Step 1: Sign in (or create the account)

  • Simpler.Grants.gov: Click sign in. You're sent to Login.gov, where you can sign in or create an account (Simpler.Grants.gov).
  • Classic Grants.gov: Register at grants.gov. If you're applying for an organization, use the same email your organization has on file in SAM.gov (Applicant Registration). Our guide Grants.gov vs SAM.gov: Registration Order in 4 Steps explains which account to set up first.

Step 2: Run a filtered search

On simpler.grants.gov/search, enter keywords and set the filters: Opportunity status, Funding instrument, Eligibility, Agency, Category and Cost sharing. The next section covers which filters work well in an alert.

Step 3: Save the search

  • Simpler.Grants.gov: Save the query from the search page. The official announcement describes it as "save your search query to quickly return to it later" (Simpler.Grants.gov forum announcement). Saved queries are listed under Workspace → Saved search queries. In January 2026, a site admin wrote on the forum that saved searches send email alerts automatically and you don't need to sign up separately (forum thread).
  • Classic Grants.gov: Log in, open the Search Grants tab, enter your criteria, click Save Search, type a Saved Search Name and click Save. Another route is Connect tab → Subscribe to Saved Searches → Add Saved Search (Grants.gov help).

Step 4: Subscribe to opportunities you'll actually pursue

Once you find a listing you're serious about, subscribe to it directly. On classic Grants.gov, go to Connect → Subscribe to Opportunities → Add Opportunity Subscription, enter the Funding Opportunity Number and click Subscribe. You can also click the Subscribe button on the View Grant Opportunity page (Grants.gov help).

Common setup mistakes and fixes

  • No emails arrive. Grants.gov says spam filters can block its notifications, so check your junk folder and add the sender to your safe list (Grants.gov help).
  • You deleted a saved search to "clean up." Deleting a search removes it permanently and stops its alerts. You'll have to rebuild it by hand (Grants.gov help).
  • Some saved searches are missing from the list. A reported Simpler.Grants.gov issue says the Saved Search Queries page shows only the first 25 saved searches (GitHub issue #12543). Stay under 25, or combine overlapping searches.
  • You expected instant alerts. Classic alerts are nightly. Simpler.Grants.gov hasn't published how often it sends saved-search emails (as of November 2026). Leave a buffer in your plans.

What is a federal grant opportunity and how do you find one on Grants.gov?

A federal grant opportunity is a funding announcement an agency publishes on Grants.gov to invite applications for one program. It can show up as a forecast first and as a posted opportunity later.

Grants.gov describes itself as the single government entry point for publishing federal discretionary grant and cooperative agreement funding notices (Grants.gov grant systems). The full document is often called a NOFO (Notice of Funding Opportunity). The Grants.gov glossary calls it a Funding Opportunity Announcement and notes that agencies may also call it a program announcement or solicitation (Grants.gov terminology).

These are the fields to read on every listing:

For example, if an alert shows a listing with status "Forecasted" and eligibility "Small businesses," the listing is a heads-up, not an open call. You can't apply until it's posted.

How to filter federal grant alerts by agency, eligibility and deadline

To filter alerts, use Eligibility, Agency, Category and Opportunity status in the saved search. Don't put deadlines in the alert. Track them on a calendar.

These are the filter options on Simpler.Grants.gov (as of November 2026; search page), along with how well each one works inside a saved alert:

Steps to build a narrow alert

  1. Open simpler.grants.gov/search and sign in.
  2. Under Opportunity status, check Forecasted and Open.
  3. Under Eligibility, check your applicant type. Also check Unrestricted, since those listings are open to any entity type, subject to the eligibility text in the NOFO.
  4. Under Agency, choose one to three agencies. Under Category, choose one or two.
  5. Add one or two keywords, for example rural broadband or opioid prevention (examples only).
  6. Check that the result count looks manageable, then save the query with a descriptive name such as "Nonprofit 501c3 – HHS – Health – open+forecast".

Pitfalls with fixes

  • The deadline filter quietly drops listings. If you save "Closing date range: Next 30 days," a NOFO posted today with a 90-day window won't match. Leave closing date on Any and add every close date to a calendar when the alert arrives.
  • An over-narrow keyword returns nothing for months. Run a second, broader search with no keywords and only Eligibility + Category.
  • The wrong 501(c)(3) box. Groups without 501(c)(3) status have their own eligibility option. See Grants for Nonprofits Without 501c3.

Saved search alerts vs email subscriptions: which should you use?

A saved search tells you about new listings that match your criteria. An opportunity subscription tells you when a specific listing changes. Use saved searches to discover listings, and subscribe by FON once a listing passes your eligibility check.

Classic Grants.gov offers three subscription types (Manage Subscriptions):

TypeWhat triggers an emailNoise levelCatches package changes?
All New OpportunitiesAny recently forecasted or posted opportunityVery highNo
Saved SearchesOpportunities matching your criteriaLow to mediumNo. Grants.gov says it won't notify you of changes such as modified application forms
Selected OpportunitiesUpdates to one specific opportunity, including packages added, modified or deletedVery lowYes

There's a fourth free channel that's easy to miss: Grants.gov RSS feeds. The four feeds are New Opportunities by Agency, New Opportunities by Category, Modified Opportunities by Agency, and Modified Opportunities by Category. The two "Modified" feeds give you a free, broad view of changed listings by agency or category, including listings you haven't subscribed to.

What is the forecast-to-posted lag and how do you track forecasted grant opportunities?

A forecast is an advance notice that an opportunity may be posted. The time between forecast and posting varies, and Grants.gov says "it is not guaranteed that a grant forecast will become an FOA" (Forecast tab help).

Forecast dates are soft because of how agencies create them. On the grantor side, the Estimated Synopsis Post Date is required for a synopsis but optional for a forecast, and agencies can edit a forecast before its post date without applicants seeing the changes (Create Forecast help). A forecast may have no estimated post date at all, and dates that are listed can slip.

Steps to track forecasts

  1. Include Forecasted in the Opportunity status of every saved search.
  2. When a forecast arrives, write down the FON, the ALN and any estimated post or due date.
  3. When the opportunity is posted, subscribe to it by FON on classic Grants.gov (Subscribe to Opportunities help), so you get emails about updates to that listing.
  4. Set a calendar check one week after the estimated post date. If nothing has been posted, search the ALN on Grants.gov and look at the agency's own funding page.
  5. Start registration work while you wait (see the checklist below), because posted windows can be short.

Example: Suppose a forecast lists an estimated post date in March and nothing appears by April. That alone doesn't mean the program was cancelled. Search by ALN, check the agency program page, and email the agency contact listed on the forecast.

Pitfall: Forecast data can produce unexpected emails. On April 3, 2026, Simpler.Grants.gov reported that a data backfill for forecasted opportunities sent users extra forecast-related emails (release notes). If you see a sudden burst of old forecasts, check the release notes before you rebuild your searches.

How to check grant amendments and modifications Grants.gov alerts miss

Before you submit, open the opportunity's Version History, Related Documents and Package tabs, and check the agency's own NOFO page.

The View Grant Opportunity page has a Forecast or Synopsis tab (never both), plus Version History, Related Documents and Package (Grants.gov help). Grants.gov warns that if the grantor changes a form and you don't use the current forms, you can't apply successfully. Workspace users get an alert when forms are updated.

A repeatable pre-submission checklist

  1. Subscribe by FON as soon as you decide to apply, so change emails start right away.
  2. Open Version History and compare the newest version's date with the version you downloaded first.
  3. Open Related Documents and look for anything new: amended NOFOs, FAQs, webinar slides, corrections.
  4. Check the close date on the Synopsis tab again. Don't rely on your calendar entry.
  5. Check the Package tab. If you use Workspace, look for the forms-updated alert. Our guide Grants.gov Workspace: Create and Submit in 5 Steps explains how to refresh forms.
  6. Visit the agency's NOFO page, if it has one, and compare it with the Grants.gov listing.
  7. Repeat steps 2–5 one week before the deadline and again 48 hours before.

Example: If Version History shows a new version two weeks after posting and Related Documents has a new "Amendment 1" PDF, read the amendment first and note what changed before you edit your application.

Pitfall: People often assume the PDF NOFO they downloaded on day one is still current. Re-download it from Related Documents before final edits.

Grants.gov alerts vs paid grant alert tools: when is free enough?

For most small businesses, nonprofits and first-time applicants looking for federal money, free Grants.gov alerts are enough. Paid tools mostly add foundation and state grants plus curation.

Who free is NOT enough for: nonprofits whose budgets depend on private foundations, and teams tracking dozens of state programs. Who a paid tool is NOT for: a small business or first-time applicant looking only at federal programs. In that case you'd be paying for listings Grants.gov already sends for free.

Limitation of free alerts: you have to do the curation yourself. Bad filters mean a flood of irrelevant listings or nothing at all.

What to do when a grant alert arrives: a checklist for first-time applicants

When an alert arrives, confirm eligibility in the NOFO, note the close date, and check your SAM.gov registration that same day.

  1. Open the full NOFO and read the eligibility section, not just the summary filter.
  2. Write down the close date, FON and ALN. Subscribe to the opportunity by FON.
  3. Check that your SAM.gov registration is active. See Check SAM Registration Status. If you aren't registered, start now: Grants.gov says to allow up to 10 business days for SAM.gov registration to become active, warns it "could take weeks or months" if information can't be verified, and you can't apply until it's active (Applicant Registration).
  4. Block out a calendar entry 10 business days before the close date as your internal deadline.

For the full application walk-through, see Federal Government Grant Application Guide: Small Business.

Next steps

You can set up working alerts today with three actions and no spending.

Do it yourself today:

  1. Create one saved search on Simpler.Grants.gov with Forecasted + Open, your Eligibility type plus Unrestricted, and one or two Categories.
  2. Add the "Modified Opportunities by Agency" RSS feed to a feed reader.
  3. Check your SAM.gov status so a good alert doesn't turn into a missed deadline.

If you'd rather not do it yourself:

  • Grants.gov Applicant Support is free and available 24/7 except federal holidays through its support portal and chatbot. It suits people who are stuck on account or subscription problems. It won't find opportunities for you.
  • A paid database such as Instrumentl costs from $299/month billed annually and has a 14-day free trial with no credit card (pricing; as of November 2026, check the official page). It suits nonprofits that need foundation grants alongside federal ones. It's a poor fit if you only want federal grants, because those are free at the source.

FAQ

Are Grants.gov email alerts really free?

Yes. Saved searches, opportunity subscriptions and RSS feeds cost nothing. You need a free Grants.gov account for classic subscriptions (Manage Subscriptions) or a free Login.gov account for Simpler.Grants.gov saved searches. Be wary of any service that charges for "Grants.gov access."

Does Grants.gov send alerts for state or foundation grants?

No. Grants.gov publishes federal discretionary grant and cooperative agreement notices (Grants.gov). For state grants, check your state's grants portal. For foundations, you'll need foundation directories or a paid database.

Why am I not receiving Grants.gov alert emails?

Start with your spam or junk folder, since Grants.gov says filters can block its notifications (help). Then make sure the saved search still exists and that its filters, especially Closing date range, aren't too narrow. If neither fixes it, contact Grants.gov Applicant Support.

Can I get alerts for only small business or nonprofit eligible grants?

Yes. Use the Eligibility filter: "Small businesses" or "Nonprofits non-higher education with 501(c)(3)" (or "without 501(c)(3)") (search filters). Also check "Unrestricted" so you don't miss listings open to all applicant types.

How often does Grants.gov send saved-search notifications?

Classic Grants.gov describes its subscription email as a nightly update (Manage Subscriptions). Simpler.Grants.gov hasn't published a sending frequency for saved-search emails (as of November 2026). Treat both as daily at best, not real time.
