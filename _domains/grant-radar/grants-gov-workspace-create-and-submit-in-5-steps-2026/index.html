A Grants.gov Workspace is the shared online area where your team fills out a federal application package and submits it. To create one, you sign in, open the opportunity, click Apply (or use Apply Now in the Applicant Center), enter an Application Filing Name, and click Create Workspace. The person who builds the Workspace usually isn't the person allowed to submit it. Only a participant with the Standard AOR or Expanded AOR role can click Sign and Submit (Grants.gov Forms Tab help). Grants.gov's Participants help says at least one participant should hold one of these AOR roles "so that the workspace can be submitted" (Participants Tab help), so plan that handoff from the start.

Key points

  • Only a Standard or Expanded AOR can click Sign and Submit
  • Workspace Owner clicks Complete and Notify AOR once forms pass
  • Archived workspaces can't be reopened; use Copy for a new package

Best for: First-time federal grant applicants using Grants.gov

How Does Grants.gov Work?

Grants.gov is the federal portal where you find opportunities and submit applications. You register, find an opportunity, complete its forms in a Workspace and submit, and then the agency retrieves your package. Grants.gov calls Workspace "the standard way for organizations or individuals to apply for federal grants" (What Is Workspace?). It replaced the old single-PDF package with separate forms that several people can work on at once.

The whole process looks like this:

  1. Register your organization in SAM.gov to get a Unique Entity Identifier (UEI). Grants.gov says this takes "up to 10 business days" if your documents are ready (Quick Start).
  2. Create a Login.gov account and a Grants.gov account, then add an applicant profile tied to your organization's UEI (Register).
  3. Find an opportunity and read its Notice of Funding Opportunity (NOFO).
  4. Create a Workspace, add your team and complete the forms.
  5. Submit and track your application until the agency retrieves it.

Grants.gov's own Workspace progress bar uses five stages, and this guide follows them: Created → Fill Out Forms → Complete and Notify AOR → Submit → Agency Received (Manage Workspace help).

A newer interface, Simpler.Grants.gov, has been piloting its own "Apply" workflow. Its roadmap describes two opportunities opened for application there in late 2025 and targets 2027 for Simpler becoming the default Grants.gov experience (Simpler.Grants.gov Roadmap). Grants.gov's help still describes the classic Workspace as the standard way to apply (What Is Workspace?). Follow whatever the "Apply" link on your specific opportunity points to.

If the order of the SAM.gov and Grants.gov registrations is confusing, see Grants.gov vs SAM.gov: Registration Order in 4 Steps.

How to Access Grants.gov Workspace

To use Workspace you need three things: a Grants.gov account linked to Login.gov, an applicant profile tied to your organization's UEI, and a role that can create workspaces. If any one of these is missing, the Apply button turns gray.

Steps to get in for the first time:

  1. Go to grants.gov, click Register, fill out the form and confirm your email (Register).
  2. Sign in with Login.gov, or create a Login.gov account first. Grants.gov says every user who downloads or edits forms online needs a Login.gov account (Quick Start).
  3. Add a profile. Choose an organization applicant profile and enter your organization's UEI, or choose an individual profile if you're applying for yourself.
  4. Ask your organization's E-Business Point of Contact (EBiz POC) to assign you a role. Any EBiz POC who existed on January 21, 2023 was automatically given the Expanded AOR role (Workspace Roles).
  5. When you sign in after that, open the Applicant Center. The Workspace tools are there: Apply Now under Grant Applications, and Manage Workspaces in the left navigation.

Common mistakes and fixes:

  • Different email addresses. Grants.gov tells you to "use the same email address to register your organization's electronic business point of contact at SAM.gov as the one you use to register at Grants.gov" (Quick Start). If the two don't match, the EBiz POC can't be recognized automatically.
  • A gray Apply button. Grants.gov lists four causes (Grants.gov blog, June 2023): no profile or role, a deadline that has passed, a forecast with no package yet, or an agency that didn't post a package on Grants.gov. The fix depends on which one applies. Either get a role assigned, check the Package tab for the closing date, wait for the posting, or read the Synopsis for the agency's own submission site.
  • An expired SAM.gov registration. Before you build anything, follow How to Check SAM.gov Registration Status (Step by Step). If you need your UEI, see UEI Number Lookup.

How to Create a Grants.gov Workspace

You create a Workspace by entering the opportunity's Funding Opportunity Number (or Package ID) plus an Application Filing Name, then clicking Create Workspace. Grants.gov loads that package's forms automatically.

Steps, using the exact labels from Grants.gov's Apply Now help:

  1. Sign in and go to the Applicant Center.
  2. Click Apply Now under the Grant Applications heading. You can also click Apply on the opportunity's listing page.
  3. Fill in either the Funding Opportunity Number field or the Opportunity Package ID field. If you don't know either one, find the opportunity under Search Grants.
  4. If more than one package matches, click Select next to the right one. Check the competition ID and the opening and closing dates.
  5. Type a descriptive name in Application Filing Name.
  6. Click Create Workspace. Grants.gov opens the Manage Workspace page, and the Activity tab logs the action as "Created."

For example, a nonprofit applying to a hypothetical opportunity numbered "ABC-2027-001" would enter that number, name the Workspace "ABC-2027-001 Youth Literacy – Riverside Nonprofit", and click Create Workspace.

Two limits apply. You can't create a Workspace for a multi-project package through this flow (Apply Now help). And Grants.gov recommends a separate Workspace for each opportunity (Quick Start).

If you applied for something similar before, you can Copy that Workspace instead of starting fresh. Go to Manage Workspaces, run a Search, click Copy in the Actions column, enter the new Funding Opportunity Number, name it and click Copy Workspace (Copy Workspace help). The Copy link only shows up if you have access to all forms or to all non-budget forms. Grants.gov also warns that form versions often change between packages, so recheck every field after copying.

How to Find My Workspace in Grants.gov

Your existing Workspaces are on the Manage Workspaces page in the Applicant Center's left navigation. Search there, then click Manage in the Actions column to open one.

Steps (Manage Workspaces help):

  1. Sign in and open the Applicant Center.
  2. Click Manage Workspaces.
  3. Enter search criteria and click Search. You can filter and sort the results.
  4. Click Manage next to the Workspace you want.

Common mistakes and fixes:

  • "My colleague's Workspace isn't in my list." Grants.gov says the results depend on "the roles assigned to your profile and workspaces in which you are a participant" (Manage Workspaces help). Ask the Workspace Owner to add you on the Participants tab, or ask an Expanded AOR to do it.
  • Signed in under the wrong profile. If you belong to more than one organization, the list only covers the profile you're using right now. Switch profiles and search again.
  • Searching with filters you no longer need. Clear the status filter. Submitted and archived Workspaces drop out of view if you're only looking at in-progress ones.

Grants.gov Workspace Roles: Lead Applicant vs. Submitter

Grants.gov doesn't use the term "lead applicant." The person who builds and runs the application is the Workspace Owner, and only a participant with the Standard AOR or Expanded AOR role can submit. That separation is the most common reason a finished application never goes out.

Many guides use "lead applicant" loosely for whoever is driving the application. In Grants.gov terms that person is usually the Workspace Owner. Each Workspace has exactly one Owner, who needs either the Workspace Manager role or a custom role with the "Own Workspace" privilege (What Is Workspace?). The Owner can add people and fill in forms, but can't submit unless they also hold an AOR role.

Roles and what they allow, based on Grants.gov's Workspace Roles page and the Forms Tab help as of October 2026:

The handoff runs like this:

  1. The Workspace Owner gets every included form to Passed status.
  2. The Owner clicks Complete and Notify AOR. That button only becomes active when there are no alerts, the selected forms show "Passed," and the Workspace has a participant with the AOR role (Forms Tab help). Clicking it unlocks any locked forms and notifies the AOR participants.
  3. The AOR opens the Workspace and clicks Sign and Submit.

Ways an application gets stuck:

  • Nobody in the Workspace holds an AOR role. Grants.gov's Participants help says that "at least one Participant should have one of the core AOR roles so that the workspace can be submitted" (Participants Tab help). Fix: add the AOR before the deadline week, not on the day it's due.
  • The Owner thinks "Passed" means sent. Grants.gov's help states that "Passed" means the form has no errors. It does not mean the form was submitted to the grantor (Forms Tab help).
  • The AOR is unavailable. Grants.gov's Quick Start tells organizations to designate at least one person with the Expanded or Standard AOR role (Quick Start). Two AORs is safer, so a vacation or sick day doesn't block a deadline.
  • Sign and Submit stays gray even for the AOR. According to Grants.gov's support team, the button also needs every required form to pass, no alerts, an active SAM registration, and an opportunity whose Open Date has passed (Grants.gov blog, Oct 2018).

In a one-person organization you can be both Owner and AOR. Standard and Expanded AORs can create and own Workspaces as well as submit (Workspace Roles), so that setup has no handoff at all.

How to Set Up a Workspace in Grants.gov (Team & Permissions)

Set up the Workspace before anyone types data: add participants on the Participants tab, limit form access where needed, and confirm an AOR is attached.

Steps, using the labels from the Participants Tab help:

  1. Open the Workspace (Manage Workspaces → Manage) and click the Participants tab.
  2. Click Add from Workspace Organization to add colleagues inside your organization, or Add by Username or Email Address to add someone inside or outside it, such as a consultant or a partner organization's staff.
  3. For each person, click Manage Access and choose their form access: all forms (the default), "only non-budget forms," or "Subform(s) only" when the package has subforms.
  4. Check that at least one participant has the Standard AOR or Expanded AOR role.
  5. If ownership needs to move, for example because the grant writer is leaving, click Make Owner next to the new owner. That person must have the Workspace Manager role or the "Own Workspace" privilege.
  6. Go to the Forms tab, click Download Instructions and read the grantor's guidance before you assign anyone a form.

Only the Workspace Owner can use Remove, Make Owner and Manage Access. For everyone else those links are inactive. An Expanded AOR can also add participants (Participants Tab help).

Filling out forms on the Forms tab (Forms Tab help):

  • Use Webform to complete a form in your browser, or Download and later Upload to work on a PDF offline. Changes sync between the two formats (Grants.gov blog, Oct 2018).
  • Click Lock to stop others from editing a form while you work on it. The form then shows as "[Locked]."
  • Use Reuse to pull in a form from an earlier Workspace.
  • Complete the SF-424 first. Grants.gov says to do this "before any other form in the application package" because it fills in fields on the other forms. Field-by-field help is in SF-424 Instructions: Field-by-Field Table and Steps (2026).
  • If the agency updates the package after you start, click Get Updated Grantor Package so your forms match the current version.

For example, a small business working with an outside grant writer might give the writer "only non-budget forms" and keep the SF-424A budget form internal. The Owner stays in-house and the CEO holds the Standard AOR role.

Pitfall: a participant who downloaded a PDF and never uploaded it can leave a form sitting in "In Progress." Look at the Activity tab to see who touched it last, then ask that person to upload or unlock it.

What Does Grants.gov Workspace Status "Archived" Mean?

An "Archived" Workspace is read-only history. You can't reopen it, run Check Application on it, or reuse forms from it. If the opportunity is offered again, copy it into a new Workspace.

Grants.gov's help documents what archived status blocks, though not every reason a Workspace lands there:

  • The Reopen button is hidden when a Workspace is New, In Progress or Archived (Reopen a Workspace help).
  • The Check Application button is inactive for New, Submitted or Archived applications (Check Application help).
  • The Reuse link isn't available when the Workspace is Ready for Submission, Submitted or Archived (Reuse Workspace Form help).
  • Separately from archiving, once a package passes its closing date Grants.gov stops accepting submissions. It also deactivates the form action links and the Submit, Complete and Reopen buttons (Resolving Problems and Errors).

What you can do:

  1. If the opportunity repeats next cycle, use Copy from Manage Workspaces with the new Funding Opportunity Number. Then recheck every form, because versions change.
  2. If you think a system problem stopped you from submitting on time, call Grants.gov support at 1-800-518-4726 or email support@grants.gov, and keep the case number (Quick Start). Whether a late application is accepted is up to the funding agency and its NOFO, not Grants.gov. Contact the agency's listed grants contact as well.
  3. If you only need the record, archived Workspaces still show up in Manage Workspaces search, so you can look up what you filed.

Don't confuse the two meanings of "Archived" on Grants.gov. Opportunity listings also have "Archived" status for past grants kept for reference. That label applies to the funding notice, not your Workspace.

How to Apply for Grants on Grants.gov (End-to-End Steps)

Once every form passes, run Check Application. The Owner then clicks Complete and Notify AOR, and the AOR clicks Sign and Submit. Grants.gov returns a tracking number and sends status emails until the agency retrieves the package.

The full process, following the five progress-bar stages (Manage Workspace help):

  1. Created. Build the Workspace (see the section above). Check the SAM status shown on the Workspace before you go further.
  2. Fill Out Forms / Forms Passed. Complete each included form until it shows "Passed." Make sure the Include in Application selections cover the optional forms your NOFO requires. For writing the narrative, see How to Write Grants for Nonprofits.
  3. Check Application. On the Forms tab, click Check Application. It only works while the Workspace is "In Progress" or "Ready for Submission," there are no alerts, and the selected forms have passed. A clean result says: "No errors found in Application Package. Additional validations may occur once you submit your application and it is retrieved by the Federal Agency" (Check Application help).
  4. Complete and Notify AOR. The Owner clicks it, which unlocks any locked forms and emails the AOR participants.
  5. Submit → Agency Received. The AOR clicks Sign and Submit. A confirmation with a tracking number appears in Grants.gov, followed by a submission receipt email and then either a validation email or a rejection email listing errors (Check Application Status help).

After you submit, the statuses run in this order (Check Application Status help):

You can look up as many as five tracking numbers at once on Track My Application without signing in (Track My Application).

Common mistakes and fixes:

  • "Rejected" after submission. Read the rejection email, click Reopen (available to the Workspace Owner and AOR participants when the Workspace is Ready for Submission or Submitted and its forms are current), fix the errors and resubmit. Reopening sets the status back to "In Progress" (Reopen a Workspace help). This only works while the opportunity is still open.
  • "Action cannot be performed due to Workspace changes." Someone else changed the Workspace after your page loaded. Refresh and try again (Check Application help).
  • Submitting on deadline day. Grants.gov says to submit "well before the deadline, just in case a submission error occurs" (Quick Start). A rejection plus a fix needs hours, and sometimes days.

Small businesses should also read How to Apply for Grants for Small Business (2026 Guide) for opportunity-specific eligibility checks.

Grants.gov Workspace Application Filing Name: What to Enter

The Application Filing Name is a required label you choose for the Workspace. It's for your own use and isn't an official ID. It can be up to 240 characters, and you can change it later with Edit Name (Manage Workspace help).

Grants.gov only asks for "a descriptive application name" (Apply Now help). The agency identifies your application by the Grants.gov tracking number and its own number, so the filing name is mainly there to help your team find the right Workspace among many.

A naming pattern that sorts well (example only):

[Funding Opportunity Number] – [Short project name] – [Org or department] – [Cycle]

For example: "ABC-2027-001 – Youth Literacy – Riverside Nonprofit – FY27"

To rename:

  1. Open the Workspace.
  2. Click Edit Name next to the Application Filing Name field.
  3. Type the new name in the Edit Name pop-up and save (Manage Workspace help).

Pitfalls:

  • Copied Workspaces keep their old names. Rename the copy right away so nobody edits last year's version by mistake.
  • Names like "Grant app final v2." When an organization has several open Workspaces, an AOR searching Manage Workspaces can end up submitting the wrong one. Put the opportunity number in the name.
  • Mixing up the filing name with the SF-424 project title. They're separate fields. The SF-424 "Descriptive Title of Applicant's Project" goes to the agency, while the filing name stays in your Workspace list.

Next steps

You can finish the setup work today in under an hour, and if you get stuck, Grants.gov's Support Center is free (Quick Start).

Do it yourself today:

  1. Sign in, open Manage Workspaces, and check that every active Workspace has at least one participant with the Standard AOR or Expanded AOR role. If one doesn't, ask your EBiz POC to assign the role now.
  2. Check that your SAM.gov registration is active and won't expire before the deadline (SAM.gov status steps).
  3. Choose an internal deadline at least a few business days before the real one, and put a Check Application run and an AOR Sign and Submit session on the calendar.

If you'd rather not do it alone:

  • Grants.gov Support Center: free and available 24/7, except federal holidays, at 1-800-518-4726 or support@grants.gov (as of October 2026; check the official page). It suits anyone stuck on roles, buttons or errors. It won't help you write the proposal or judge eligibility.
  • SBA Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs): SBA describes free or low-cost counseling through a network of 63 lead centers with more than 800 locations (as of October 2026; check the official page). This is a good fit for small business owners who want one-on-one advice, and you can find a center with SBA's local assistance finder. It's less useful for university researchers, who should go to their institution's sponsored programs office, which often acts as the AOR anyway. Advisor availability and grant expertise vary by center.
  • A paid grant writer or consultant: they can be added as an external Workspace participant with "only non-budget forms" access. This makes sense if you have a large application and no staff time. If you just need to get through submission mechanics, the free support line covers that. Rates vary widely, so get a written scope and price. Even then, a consultant can't click Sign and Submit unless your organization gives them an AOR role, which most organizations shouldn't do.

FAQ

How do I log in to Grants.gov Workspace?

Sign in at grants.gov through Login.gov, which every Grants.gov user who works on forms online needs (Quick Start). Then open the Applicant Center and click Manage Workspaces. If the page is empty or Apply is gray, you probably don't have an applicant profile or role yet.

What is a Grants.gov Workspace Manager?

Workspace Manager is a basic organization role that can create and own Workspaces (Workspace Roles). A Workspace Manager can add participants and click Complete and Notify AOR. It can't click Sign and Submit, which requires the Standard AOR or Expanded AOR role.

What forms are in a Grants.gov Workspace?

Each Workspace loads the forms in the agency's package for that specific opportunity. That usually means the SF-424 plus agency forms, some mandatory and some optional. Grants.gov says to complete the SF-424 first because it fills in other forms (Forms Tab help). You can fill out each form as a Webform or as a downloaded PDF.

Where are the official Grants.gov Workspace instructions?

The main sources are the Workspace Overview, the Workspace Roles page, and the online help pages for Manage Workspace, the Forms tab and the Participants tab. Always read your opportunity's NOFO and the Download Instructions file on the Forms tab too, because agencies add their own rules.
