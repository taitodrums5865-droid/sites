Check SAM Registration Status: SAM.gov Guide & Lookup

To check your SAM.gov status, sign in and open Workspace, or use the Check Entity Status tool on the SAM.gov home page. Both need a role in the entity. If you don't have a login, search Entity Information by UEI or legal name. You can only apply and receive a federal grant when the status reads Active Registration. Inactive, Work in Progress and Submitted all mean you aren't eligible yet (2 CFR 25.200; EXIM).

Key points

  • Only Active Registration status allows receiving a federal grant award
  • Workspace shows status, UEI, expiration date, and lets you renew
  • Active status can take 24–48 hours to reach Grants.gov after update

Best for: Grant applicants and administrators managing SAM.gov registration

How to decide: Will you renew or edit the registration yourself? Use Workspace. Did you submit in the last few weeks? Use Check Entity Status. Are you only checking another organization? Use public search. Checking and renewing in SAM.gov is always free (GSA).

Method 1: Check status in your SAM.gov Workspace (signed in)

If you're an Entity Administrator or hold another role in the entity, Workspace gives you the most complete view.

  1. Go to SAM.gov and select Sign In. Sign-in goes through Login.gov, so have your Login.gov credentials and two-factor device ready.
  2. In the menu bar, select Workspace. It sits next to Sign Out (EXIM).
  3. Under Entity Management, select the status bubble that shows a number. A "1" next to Active, for example, means one active registration.
  4. For each entity, check three things:
  5. Status (Active Registration, Work in Progress Registration, Submitted Registration and so on)
  6. UEI (a 12-character alphanumeric ID that never changes once assigned, per DOJ)
  7. Expiration date
  8. If the expiration date is close, choose Renew Entity and submit. Renewal never happens automatically (EXIM).

Rule of thumb: If your status is Work in Progress, you still owe information. If it's Submitted, you're waiting on validation. When you manage several entities, check this screen for each one before every deadline.

Method 2: Use the Check Entity Status tool (best right after submitting)

Check Entity Status is the official tool for tracking a registration you just submitted or a UEI request that's still pending (GSA).

  1. Sign in to SAM.gov.
  2. On the home page, select Check Entity Status.
  3. Enter your UEI or CAGE code if you already have a registration. Enter your legal business name if you only have a UEI or are still getting one.
  4. Compare the status you see with the table below.

Limitation: Unless you're a federal government user, you need a role assigned by the entity to use this tool (GSA). If a consultant or former employee set up the registration, ask the current Entity Administrator to give you a role.

Method 3: Public entity search (no login)

Public search works without an account. It shows the status and expiration date of entities whose records are public.

  1. Open SAM.gov Entity Information. You can also select Search and choose the Entity Information domain.
  2. Enter the UEI or legal business name.
  3. Open the result to see the status and expiration date.

Not the right tool if: you're tracking a registration that isn't active yet, or the entity has set its record to private. Sensitive details such as banking information and Tax ID never appear in public results. If you get "no results," sign in and use Method 1 or 2.

What each SAM.gov status means for grant eligibility

Only Active Registration lets you submit through Grants.gov and receive an award. Federal rules require you to stay active for as long as you have an application under consideration or an active award (2 CFR 25.200).

SAM.gov doesn't use "Expired" or "In Review" as status labels. Those everyday terms match Inactive Registration and Submitted Registration (or Pending ID Assignment while validation is in progress).

Why "Active" isn't always usable the same day

After your SAM registration becomes active, or after you update it, Grants.gov says it takes 24–48 hours before the information reaches Grants.gov and you can submit (Grants.gov Help). Some agencies take longer. NSF, for example, needs up to two business days to receive SAM data (NSF SBIR/STTR). If your registration turns Active on the day of a deadline, you may not be able to submit in time.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Most status problems come from timing, access or contact details, not from the registration itself.

  • Renewing in deadline week. DOJ advises starting registration or renewal 30 days before any deadline (DOJ). Grants.gov says to allow up to 10 business days if your address and Tax ID are in order (Grants.gov). NSF says it can take up to a month (NSF).
  • Missing renewal reminders. SAM.gov emails the Entity Administrator 60, 30 and 15 days before expiration (EXIM). If the administrator has left, the reminders go nowhere. Keep the roles up to date.
  • Assuming renewal is automatic. Nothing renews until someone selects Renew Entity and submits.
  • Using different emails in each system. Grants.gov asks you to use the same email for your SAM.gov electronic business point of contact and your Grants.gov registration (Grants.gov).
  • Paying a "renewal" fee. SAM.gov registration is always free, and official emails come from .gov addresses (GSA; BBB).

FAQ

How long does it take for SAM.gov registration to become active?

If your documents are in order, Grants.gov says to allow up to 10 business days (Grants.gov). NSF says it can take up to a month if information is incomplete (NSF). Once you're active, allow another 24–48 hours before Grants.gov recognizes the registration.

Can I check SAM.gov registration status without logging in?

Yes. Use Entity Information search. Private records and registrations that aren't active yet may not appear, so sign in to see those.

How often do I need to renew?

Every year. A registration expires one year after it was submitted, and you must review and update your information each year (2 CFR 25.200).

Can I apply if my status is "Submitted"?

You can prepare your application, but you can't submit it through Grants.gov until registration is complete and your status is Active (Grants.gov Help).

What happens if my registration expires during an open award?

Your status changes to Inactive, and you no longer meet the federal requirement to stay active. NSF, for example, blocks award approval actions when a registration isn't revalidated (NSF). Renew right away and contact your program officer.

Next steps

The best step today is to confirm your status and put the expiration date on your calendar.

1. Do it yourself today

  • Sign in, open Workspace, and write down your status, UEI and expiration date.
  • If the expiration date is within 60 days, select Renew Entity now.
  • Give roles to at least two current staff members, so reminders and access don't depend on one person.

2. If doing it yourself is hard

  • Federal Service Desk (FSD.gov): Free. Suits: people with a specific error or a validation that's stuck. Not for: people who want the work done for them. FSD guides you but won't register for you.
  • APEX Accelerators (formerly PTACs): Free, in-person help with SAM.gov registration through local offices (SBIR.gov; NM APEX example). Suits: small businesses. Note: their focus is government contracting, so grant-only nonprofits and individual researchers should ask the local office whether it can help.
  • Paid services and grant consultants: Fees vary by provider (as of October 2026; check each provider's own pages). Suits: organizations with no staff time to spare. Not for: anyone on a tight budget. Paid help is never required (GSA). If you hire one, keep the Entity Administrator role inside your organization.

3. Come back to Outlay Grants Once your status shows Active Registration, your next step is finding a grant to apply for. Go to the Outlay Grants home page and pick the guide that matches where you are. Before each deadline, come back here to check your status again.
