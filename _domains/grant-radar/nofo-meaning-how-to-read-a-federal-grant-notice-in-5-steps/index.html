The NOFO meaning in federal grants is Notice of Funding Opportunity: a formal, public announcement that a federal agency has money available through a financial assistance program, as defined in 2 CFR 200.1. It tells you who can apply, how applicants are evaluated, what the application must contain and how to submit it. Read it in this order: basic information, eligibility, deadline, award size, then review criteria. Stop as soon as you hit a requirement you can't meet.

Key points

  • NOFO means Notice of Funding Opportunity, a federal funding notice
  • Read eligibility before deadline, money and scoring criteria
  • Agencies should generally allow 60 days; under 30 requires exigent circumstances

What is the meaning of NOFO in federal grants?

NOFO means Notice of Funding Opportunity, the public notice an agency uses to invite applications for a specific federal funding program. The Uniform Guidance at 2 CFR 200.1 says it can appear in paper or electronic form and must describe eligibility, evaluation, application contents and submission.

Three rules make a NOFO more predictable than it looks:

What is the meaning of NOFO in federal grants?. Source: Grants.gov
RuleWhat it saysSource
Time openAgencies should generally allow at least 60 calendar days, and no opportunity should run under 30 unless exigent circumstances justify it2 CFR 200.204
SummaryKey details go on Grants.gov, including a plain-language executive summary of no more than 500 words2 CFR 200.204
LayoutEvery NOFO should use the same or similar headings across eight sections, with a table of contentsAppendix I

A NOFO is an invitation, and it doesn't commit the agency to funding you. Under Appendix I, even a notice of selection is not authorization to begin work; the signed federal award notice is the official obligating document.

These rules may change. OMB proposed a rewrite of Part 200 on May 29, 2026, and law firm summaries say it would require NOFOs to be posted on Grants.gov, with limited exceptions, and push agencies to limit their length and complexity. The newest dated status report found for this guide, updated August 4, 2026, said no final rule had been published. Check the Federal Register for anything newer. Whichever rule applies, the notice you are reading sets your application's requirements.

NOFO vs FOA vs RFA vs RFP: what is the difference?

NOFO is the Uniform Guidance term for a funding notice, FOA and RFA are older or agency-specific labels, and RFP usually signals a procurement contract.

NOFO vs FOA vs RFA vs RFP: what is the difference?. Source: Grants.gov
LabelWhere you'll see itWhat it means for you
NOFO2 CFR 200.1, HUD, NIHThe standard notice
FOAGrants.gov terminology page, older noticesSame kind of document under an older name
NOFAGrants.gov terminology page (notice of funding availability)Same kind of document
RFA, PA, PARNIH funding noticesNIH types of NOFO; NIH replaced "FOA" with "NOFO" in 2023 but still uses PA and RFA in announcement numbers
BAAAFOSR's research announcementA broad research solicitation; read its award type before assuming it is a grant
RFPProcurementUnder 31 U.S.C. chapter 63, a procurement contract is for acquiring goods or services for the government

NIH. A NIH NOFO is the same concept with a type code attached. NIH's first notice in the plain-language SimplerNOFO format was reported as PAR-27-032, and NIH notices will gradually adopt more standardized layouts. Expect old and new layouts side by side for a while.

HUD. HUD announces competitive funding through NOFOs, posts all of them on Grants.gov, and may take applications through Grants.gov or esnaps.hud.gov. HUD sometimes posts a preview first, and one PRO Housing preview told applicants to treat the Grants.gov version as official.

The common mistake is trusting the label. Check the funding instrument field instead, which on a Simpler.Grants.gov listing reads "Funding instrument type." If it says cooperative agreement, the agency will have substantial involvement in your project. The grant vs cooperative agreement guide covers how that changes the work.

How to read a NOFO in 5 steps (the order that saves time)

Read a NOFO in this order: basic information, eligibility, deadline and submission, award size, then review criteria and required documents. Stop as soon as one step rules you out.

Section names vary by agency and by the age of the notice, so use this table to locate each step.

How to read a NOFO in 5 steps (the order that saves time). Source: Grants.gov
Reading stepCurrent Appendix I sectionHHS SimplerNOFO (wording varies)Common older label
1. Identify the notice1. Basic InformationStep 1Overview Information
2. Eligibility2. EligibilityStep 1Section C, Eligibility Information
3. Deadline and submission5. Submission Requirements and DeadlinesStep 5Section D, Application and Submission Information
4. Award size1. Basic InformationStep 1Section B, Federal Award Information
5. Review and documents6. Application Review Information; 4. Application Contents and FormatSteps 3 and 4Sections D and E

Older formats survive: AFOSR's FY2027 Young Investigator notice opens with "I. Overview Information." So search by topic rather than by letter.

  1. Identify the notice. Paste the number into Simpler.Grants.gov search or the Search Grants tab on Grants.gov. Open the listing and note the funding opportunity number, assistance listing, status and latest version. Then read the executive summary.
  2. Check eligibility. Find your applicant type, then any project or personnel limits. A clear no ends the exercise.
  3. Check the deadline. Find every due date, the time zone and the submission system. Count backward from the due date to see whether your registration can finish in time.
  4. Check the money. Note program funding, expected awards, award limits, project period and cost sharing. Ask whether the award justifies the work.
  5. Read the review criteria and document list. Use them as your outline. Only now is it worth reading the rest closely.

For example, if you run a nonprofit and step 2 lists only institutions of higher education, you are done after two steps and can move to the next notice.

How to check NOFO eligibility before you spend time applying

Check NOFO eligibility by confirming your applicant type first, then project limits, personnel rules, application limits and cost sharing. Appendix I puts all of these in the Eligibility section, and a required cost share counts because failing to commit to it makes the application ineligible.

How to check NOFO eligibility before you spend time applying. Source: Grants.gov
You areLook for this categoryWatch out for
Small business"Small businesses," meeting SBA size standardsSize rules are set by NAICS code and count affiliates; confirm with SBA's Size Standards Tool
Larger company"For-profit organizations other than small businesses"Some notices exclude for-profits entirely
NonprofitWith or without 501(c)(3) status, excluding higher educationThese are separate categories, so a notice may list only one
ResearcherUsually your employer's categoryPrincipal investigator rules sit on top of it
IndividualIndividuals, applying on their own behalfAn individual profile only reaches notices open to individuals; read Grants.gov's individual applicant rules before expecting personal financial help

A real example shows why the second layer matters. AFOSR's FY2026 Young Investigator listing (now archived) accepted small businesses, universities and nonprofits. The investigator, however, had to be a full-time, regular employee of the applicant, a U.S. citizen, national or permanent resident, and have earned a Ph.D. on or after April 1, 2018. A company could fit the category and still have no eligible investigator.

To check your own status, follow these steps:

  1. Write down your legal entity type exactly as it appears on your tax and registration records.
  2. Match it to the notice's listed applicant types. If your category isn't named, treat that as a no until the agency contact says otherwise.
  3. Read the additional restrictions, ineligible projects and application limits, all of which Appendix I places in this section.
  4. Look up your SAM.gov status if you apply as an organization.
  5. Email the listed contact about gray areas and keep the reply. Agencies should make answers to applicant questions publicly available, so check any posted Q&A first.

The pitfall is treating "nonprofit" as a synonym for "501(c)(3)." If you lack that status, read whether the notice lists non-501(c)(3) nonprofits before you write a word.

How to find the NOFO deadline and submission requirements

The NOFO deadline is in Submission Requirements and Deadlines, which must state due dates and times for all submissions. Older notices usually put it in the overview and Application and Submission Information.

Look for four things:

  • All due dates, not just the last. The FY2027 DURIP call, per a university summary, set pre-proposal questions for October 16, three weeks before full proposals on November 6, 2026, at 5:00 PM Eastern. Use it as an example of how a notice can stagger its dates, not as an opportunity to plan for.
  • The time zone. The DURIP summary above uses Eastern, and another summary gives 4:00 PM Central, the same moment. Convert to your local time once and write it down.
  • The system. It may be Grants.gov, an agency portal or both, and HUD's programs can use esnaps.hud.gov. Multi-project packages may send you to a separate agency system.
  • Late rules. If paper and electronic deadlines differ, the notice must say so. Don't assume a grace period exists unless your notice grants one.

Next, count your registration time. Grants.gov says to allow up to 10 business days for SAM.gov to become active, and weeks or months if information can't be verified. You can't start applying in Grants.gov until then, and your EBiz POC must still set up the organization profile and assign roles such as Authorized Organization Representative.

Example timeline, for illustration: if a notice closes in 30 calendar days and your organization has no SAM.gov registration, 10 business days (about two weeks) is the best case. That leaves roughly two weeks to write, so a complex notice may not be worth attempting this cycle.

Plan the last few days too. A Department of Labor attachment recommends allowing two business days for submission, plus time to fix errors. After you submit, check the status: Validated means Grants.gov found no field errors; Rejected with Errors means you must fix and resubmit, and the Details link in the Actions column shows why. Neither status means the agency has reviewed your application.

How to understand NOFO award size, number of awards and funding type

NOFO award size appears in Basic Information as expected total funding, number of awards and award values, which may be a range. Read these figures together, then confirm them against the full text, because a single field can mislead.

How to understand NOFO award size, number of awards and funding type. Source: Grants.gov
FieldWhat to ask
Program fundingIs it the total for all awards, for one year, or subject to appropriations?
Expected awardsHow many winners, and is this a firm count or an estimate?
Award minimum and maximumIs the figure per year or for the whole project?
Project periodDoes your project fit, and are later years guaranteed?
Cost sharingIs it required, how is it calculated, and what types of cost are allowed?
Funding instrument typeGrant or cooperative agreement?

The FY2026 AFOSR Young Investigator listing shows why this matters. Its fields read Program Funding $4,500,000, Expected awards 50, Award Minimum $150,000 and Award Maximum $450,000. The description says up to $150,000 per year for three years, so the "minimum" appears to be the annual cap. It also describes 37 traditional awards, and 37 × $450,000 is $16.65 million, well above the program funding field. The fields don't reconcile on their own, so budget from the narrative.

Secondary summaries can disagree as well. For FY2027 DURIP, Pitt lists $50,000 to $3,000,000 per award and NJIT lists up to $3,000,000, while an Oregon State page lists a $7,000,000 ceiling. In a case like this, only the agency's notice settles it.

Two more checks help your go/no-go call:

  • Cost share. Required match is an eligibility matter. Voluntary match is different: it isn't expected on federal research grants and generally can't be a merit factor there unless statute or agency regulation authorizes it and the NOFO specifies it. Other programs are discouraged from scoring it, and if they do, the notice must explain how. Don't offer match you weren't asked for.
  • Cash timing. Pre-award costs, if allowed, are at your own risk. Don't spend against a selection email.

A quick screen for illustration: divide the realistic award by the hours needed to apply and manage it, then compare the result with your other uses of that time. If the award is $50,000 over two years and carries reporting duties, a small team may reasonably pass.

How to use the NOFO review criteria to write your application

Use the NOFO review criteria as your outline by matching each criterion to a labeled part of your narrative. Appendix I places threshold criteria, review criteria, the selection process and risk review in Application Review Information.

Agencies must describe their merit review process in the funding opportunity, or incorporate it by reference. If criteria vary in importance, the notice must give the relative percentages, weights or other means of distinguishing them. Those weights tell you where to spend your pages.

  1. Copy every criterion into a blank document, in the notice's order and wording.
  2. Note the threshold or responsiveness checks separately. Missing one can stop an application before scoring.
  3. Add the weight beside each criterion.
  4. Open Application Contents and Format and list page limits, formatting, file names and types, signatures and third-party documents.
  5. Open the package: in the Grants.gov Package tab, click Preview in the Actions column, review the mandatory and optional forms in Preview Opportunity Package Details, and use Download Instructions.
  6. Assign an owner and a due date to each item.

How to decode a NOFO number like NOFOAFRLAFOSR20260001

A funding opportunity number is the number an agency assigns to its announcement, and this one appears to combine notice type, command, office, year and sequence. Treat the breakdown as an inference from AFOSR's listings, not an official key.

The year needs care. NOFOAFRLAFOSR20250006 is the FY2026 Young Investigator Program, so that number's year is one behind the program year. One example doesn't make a rule, so search the exact string on Simpler.Grants.gov to confirm which program and year any number belongs to, including the one in this section's heading.

One office can also use more than one format, and one program can carry several numbers. AFOSR's FY2027 Young Investigator notice file is FA955026S0003, and DURIP is listed under an AFOSR number, W911NF26S0095 for ARO and N0001426SF003 for ONR.

To confirm you have the current version:

  1. Paste the exact number, without spaces, into Simpler.Grants.gov or the Grants.gov Search Grants tab.
  2. Check the status. An "Archived" label, as on the FY2026 listing, means the opportunity is no longer open.
  3. Compare versions. Simpler listings show History with version and dates; Grants.gov's tabs include Version History and Related Documents.
  4. Open the newest attachment. File names can flag revisions, as in NOFOAFRLAFOSR20250006_Amendment_0001.

If you use an AI summary, verify five things by hand against the current notice: eligibility, every due date and time zone, award limits, cost sharing and review weights. An error in any of them can cost you a cycle, and the agency's notice controls over any summary.

Does NOFO mean something else? (slang, Samoan, Tongan, Long Island)

Yes: outside grants, nofo is a Polynesian word, and NOFO or NoFo is a nickname for Long Island's North Fork. This guide gives no slang meaning because it has no reliable source for one.

FAQ

Is a NOFO the same as a grant application?

No. The NOFO is the agency's announcement and rulebook, while the application is what you submit in response. On Grants.gov, clicking Apply on the package creates a workspace where you prepare the forms.

Where do I find the NOFO for a specific federal grant?

Search the program name or opportunity number on Simpler.Grants.gov or Grants.gov, then open the listing and its attached documents. On Grants.gov, also check the Related Documents tab and the instructions in the package. Some agencies, such as HUD, also list their notices on their own funding pages.

What does NOFO mean for NIH and HUD grants?

It means the same thing at both agencies. NIH adopted the term in 2023 in place of FOA and still uses PA and RFA in its announcement numbers. HUD uses NOFOs for its competitive funding and posts them on Grants.gov.

What happens if a NOFO is amended after I start my application?

Follow the newest version, and read what changed line by line against your draft. Check the listing's version history and any amendment files, and pay particular attention to due dates, forms, page limits and eligibility. If the amendment doesn't make clear whether work already done still counts, ask the agency contact and keep the answer.

Do I need SAM.gov registration before I can apply to a NOFO?

Organizations do. Appendix I says applicants must be registered in SAM.gov before submitting and provide a valid unique entity identifier, and Grants.gov says you can't start applying until your organization is registered. Registration can take up to 10 business days or longer, and you must keep it active while your application or award is active. Individuals applying on their own behalf should check the notice and Grants.gov's individual applicant rules.
