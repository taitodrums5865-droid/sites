What "open now" means here

This page lists 1,443 federal grant opportunities from Grants.gov that you can act on today. That includes posted opportunities, which are taking applications, and forecasted ones, where the agency has announced a grant but not opened it yet. Forecasts are worth watching: they give you weeks or months to line up partners and registrations before the clock starts.

111 of the listings close within 14 days. National Institutes of Health has the most listings at the moment, with 45% of the total. That usually follows the agency's program calendar and says little about your odds.

Registrations come first

To apply on Grants.gov, your organization needs an active SAM.gov registration with a Unique Entity ID (UEI) and a Grants.gov account with the right roles. Both are free. A first SAM.gov registration can take several weeks, longer if your details do not match IRS or bank records, so start before you find the perfect grant. Never pay a company that offers to register you; the government does not charge.

Reading a listing

Each grant has a notice of funding opportunity, usually called the NOFO. It is the rulebook. It tells you who is eligible, how much money is available, whether you must share costs, how applications are scored and what to submit. Our summary pulls out the closing date, award ceiling and eligible applicant types, but read the NOFO before you decide.

Check eligibility first, because it rules out the most. Many federal grants go to states, tribes, local governments, universities or nonprofits, and only some accept for-profit businesses or individuals. The "who can apply" codes on each listing come straight from Grants.gov. If a listing says "Others (see text field)", the real answer is in the eligibility section of the NOFO.

Then check the money. The award ceiling is the most one award can be, not what you will get, and the expected number of awards tells you how competitive it is. Cost sharing or matching means you must put in your own funds or in-kind support.

Deadlines

Grants.gov closing dates are usually set at a specific time in Eastern Time, and the system checks your package after you submit. Errors can bounce an application back, so aim to submit at least a few days early. Some agencies also ask for a letter of intent or a pre-application weeks before the main deadline; the NOFO lists these dates.
