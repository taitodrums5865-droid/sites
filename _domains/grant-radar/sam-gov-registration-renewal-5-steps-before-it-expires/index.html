To renew your SAM.gov registration, sign in to SAM.gov through Login.gov, open your Entity Workspace, choose Update from the entity's Actions menu, review every section, re-certify, and submit. It costs nothing and must happen every 365 days (SAM.gov). Start when the first reminder email arrives, 60 days before expiration, because IRS and CAGE validation alone can take up to 10 business days (DOJ JustGrants).

Key points

  • Renew every 365 days; SAM.gov emails at 60, 30 and 15 days
  • Renewal is free; processing can take up to 10 business days
  • Keep SAM active until final grant reports or final payment

Best for: Federal grant applicants and recipients renewing SAM.gov

What Is the SAM.gov Registration Renewal Timeline?

Your SAM.gov registration has to be renewed every 365 days, and you should start about 60 days before the expiration date shown in your entity record. SAM.gov states that "you must renew your registration every 365 days to keep it active" (SAM.gov). The federal grants rule says the same thing in legal terms: recipients must "review and update" their SAM.gov information "annually from the date of initial registration or subsequent updates" (2 CFR 25.200).

Your date is the expiration date shown in your entity record, not the calendar year or your fiscal year end. The Department of Justice tells applicants to "renew your registration prior to its expiration date, which is listed in the entity record on SAM.gov" (DOJ JustGrants).

Timeline pitfalls and fixes

  • The reminders go to a dead inbox. The emails go to the Entity Administrator's address. If that person left, nobody sees them. Fix: log in now and check who holds the Entity Administrator role. Add a second admin you trust.
  • You renew the week a grant is due. Grants.gov checks SAM status when you submit. A renewal that is still "Submitted" is not "Active." Fix: put your expiration date minus 60 days on a shared calendar.
  • You assume a mid-year update reset the clock. 2 CFR 25.200 counts the annual review "from the date of initial registration or subsequent updates." Don't guess, though. Read the expiration date in your Entity Workspace after every submission.

How to Renew Your SAM.gov Registration (Step-by-Step)

You renew by signing in, opening the entity's Update option in Entity Workspace, reviewing each section, re-certifying and submitting for validation. The labels below come from the Federal Service Desk's Quick Start Guide for Updating Entity Registrations (FSD). SAM.gov sometimes renames buttons, so if a label looks a little different, use the closest match.

Before you start: have these ready

  • Login.gov access for a user who holds the Entity Administrator role (or another role that can register)
  • Your UEI (12 characters). If you don't know it, use our UEI lookup guide
  • Legal business name and physical address exactly as the IRS has them for your EIN/TIN
  • Current bank routing and account numbers for EFT
  • Names, emails and phone numbers for your points of contact (POCs)

The 5 renewal steps

  1. Sign in. Go to SAM.gov and select Sign In in the upper right. Select Accept on the notice. You'll be sent to Login.gov to enter your credentials and complete multi-factor authentication.
  2. Open Entity Workspace. You land in your SAM.gov Workspace. In the Entities widget, select the Entities title to open the Entity Workspace.
  3. Choose Update. Find the entity record, then select the three-dot Actions menu next to its expiration date and choose Update. You'll get two choices: update only the points of contact, or update/renew the entire registration. Pick the full update/renew. Per FSD, only that option makes the registration active for another year. A POC-only edit doesn't renew it.
  4. Review every section and re-certify. Work through core data (name, address, TIN, banking/EFT), assertions, representations and certifications, and points of contact. For grant-only entities, check that your purpose of registration is still right. Read the financial assistance certifications in full, because their text can change between years (see the nonprofit section below).
  5. Submit and wait for validation. Submit the registration. It goes to IRS TIN and CAGE validation, which can take up to 10 business days (DOJ JustGrants). You can't edit a submitted registration until processing finishes (FSD), so check everything before you click.

When the notarized letter comes in

A routine renewal by an existing Entity Administrator usually needs no notarized letter. The letter is for giving "access to new Entity Administrators on existing registrations where there is no existing administrator to approve your role request" (Virginia APEX Accelerator). It is signed by someone with signing authority, notarized, and uploaded through a ticket at FSD.gov. Some third-party guides say a letter is needed for every renewal. If you're unsure, ask FSD before you pay a notary.

Example renewal (illustration only)

For example, picture a nonprofit whose record shows an expiration date of March 14. The 60-day email arrives around January 13. The finance director logs in that week and notices the bank changed after a merger. She updates the EFT details and submits on January 16. That leaves several weeks of buffer even if validation takes the full 10 business days.

SAM.gov Registration Renewal Cost: Is It Free?

Yes. SAM.gov registration renewal is free on the official government site, and any charge comes from a private company, never from the government. DOJ says plainly: "There is no cost to register with SAM.gov" (DOJ JustGrants). That covers new registrations, renewals and updates, as well as getting a UEI.

The Better Business Bureau warns about third parties that offer SAM.gov registration and renewal help for a fee and sometimes don't deliver (BBB Scam Alert). The most common version is a fake renewal notice. It's a letter or email with your business name and real expiration date, which is public, plus a payment link.

How to tell a real notice from a sales pitch

  • Real SAM.gov emails remind you to renew and point you to SAM.gov. They don't ask for payment.
  • Sales notices use words like "mandatory," "final notice" or "registry," quote a fee, and link to a domain that isn't sam.gov. Some say in small print that they aren't a government agency.
  • Check the domain. Federal sites end in .gov. Renewal happens only at sam.gov, and support only at fsd.gov.

If you already paid, the payment didn't renew anything. Log in to SAM.gov and renew it yourself. Then contact your card issuer about the charge.

SAM.gov Registration Fee: What You're Actually Paying For (If Anything)

There's no government fee, so any SAM.gov registration fee pays a private company for help. That help is optional. Some organizations pay because they don't have staff time, or because a messy record (name mismatch, lost admin access) needs hands-on fixing. Before paying, compare it with the free options.

Pitfalls with paid services

  • Your certifications are still yours. Whoever clicks through, your organization makes the representations. A consultant can't take on that liability.
  • Handing over Login.gov credentials to an outside firm puts your banking data at risk. If you hire help, add them through SAM.gov roles instead of sharing a password.
  • "Expedited processing" doesn't exist for a fee. IRS and CAGE validation runs on the government's timeline.

How to Check Your SAM.gov Registration Status

To check your SAM.gov registration status, search your UEI or CAGE code in SAM.gov's public entity status tracker, or open your Entity Workspace to see the status and expiration date. The status tracker is linked from the SAM.gov homepage and footer and needs no login.

Steps

  1. Go to SAM.gov and open the Entity Status Tracker link (homepage or footer).
  2. Enter your 12-character UEI or your CAGE code and search.
  3. Read the status and the expiration date. For more detail, sign in and open Entity Workspace.

For example, if you submitted a renewal six business days ago and still see "Submitted," that's normal. If it's still "Submitted" after 10 business days, open an FSD ticket with your UEI. Our step-by-step status guide shows the screens in more detail.

SAM.gov Entity Registration Renewal for Nonprofits and Grant Recipients

Nonprofits, universities and researchers with active federal awards must keep SAM.gov active until the award fully closes out, not just until the money is spent. Federal rules require an active registration "at all times during which it has an active Federal award… or an application under consideration" (2 CFR 25.200). The standard award term in 2 CFR Part 25 extends that duty until you submit all final reports or receive the final payment, whichever is later (eCFR 2 CFR Part 25, Appendix A).

Grant-specific things to check during renewal

  • Purpose of registration. Entities that only take grants and cooperative agreements can register for financial assistance awards only (FSD Quick Start Guide for Grants Registrations). If you've started bidding on contracts, you need a full registration. SAM.gov notes that a UEI-only entity "cannot apply directly for federal awards" (SAM.gov).
  • Financial assistance certifications. On January 28, 2026, GSA proposed adding three certifications to the financial assistance reps and certs, covering "illegal DEI," immigration-related conduct and support for violence or terrorism. They would apply at registration and at each annual renewal (91 Fed. Reg. 3726; comments closed March 30, 2026) (Feldesman). GSA estimated about 222,760 financial assistance registrants could be affected (Inside Government Contracts). Read the exact text on screen when you renew, and talk to counsel if any line doesn't fit your programs.
  • Downstream systems pull from SAM. DOJ's JustGrants uses SAM.gov "as the primary source" of applicant information (DOJ JustGrants). A wrong address or POC in SAM carries over into your grant records.
  • Grants.gov roles depend on SAM POCs. Your E-Business POC in SAM controls Grants.gov roles. See Grants.gov vs SAM.gov registration order for how the two connect.

Example (illustration only)

For example, a university lab's three-year award ends June 30, but the final financial report is due 120 days later. The SAM record must stay active through that final report, and through the final drawdown if that comes later. Letting it lapse in August because "the grant is over" breaks the award term.

Pitfalls

  • Multiple UEIs. A department may hold its own UEI apart from the parent university. Each registration has its own expiration date, so track them separately.
  • Single Audit data. Renewal doesn't touch your audit filings, but agencies check both. If you spent $1,000,000 or more in federal awards, see our single audit threshold guide.

What Happens If Your SAM.gov Registration Expires?

If your SAM.gov registration expires, its status turns Inactive. You can't receive new federal awards, and payments on existing awards may be held until you renew and the record is Active again. Your UEI doesn't change and never expires (GSA), so you renew the existing record rather than starting over.

What an expiration can cost you

  • New awards. If your registration isn't active with current information when an agency is ready to award, "the Federal agency may make a Federal award to another applicant" (2 CFR Part 25, via Justice Grants; eCFR 2 CFR Part 25).
  • Applications in progress. Grants.gov and agency systems check SAM status when you submit, so an expired record can get the application rejected (GrantSights).
  • Active award payments. Agencies can hold drawdowns while the registration is inactive. Third-party guides report that a lapse "prevents receiving award payments" even on existing grants (GrantSights). How strict this is varies by agency and payment system.
  • Compliance record. A lapse during an award breaks the 2 CFR 25 award term, and the agency may raise it at monitoring or closeout.

Steps to recover from an expired registration

  1. Confirm the status is Inactive in the Entity Status Tracker.
  2. Sign in and use Update in Entity Workspace, the same path as a normal renewal. Review every section, because an expired record often has stale bank or POC data.
  3. Submit, then email your grants officer: "Our SAM.gov registration lapsed on [date]; renewal submitted [date]; UEI [xxxx]." Ask whether any payment requests are on hold.
  4. If validation stalls past 10 business days, open an FSD ticket and mention the award deadline.
  5. Once the record is Active, resubmit any held payment requests or rejected applications if the deadline allows.

If nobody can log in, start with the Entity Administrator role request at FSD. A notarized letter may be required when there's no existing admin to approve you (Virginia APEX Accelerator).

SAM.gov Registration Renewal vs. New Registration: Key Differences

A renewal keeps your existing UEI and profile and asks you to review, update and re-certify. A new registration starts with entity validation and gets a UEI assigned. Don't create a second registration because your old one expired. Renew the record tied to your existing UEI.

Steps to decide which one you need

  1. Search your organization's name in the UEI lookup. If a UEI appears, even with an Inactive status, you renew.
  2. If no UEI appears and you're sure the organization never registered, start a new registration. Our federal grant application guide for small business covers the full order.
  3. If you changed legal structure (e.g., a sole proprietorship became an LLC with a new EIN), you probably have a new legal entity that needs its own registration. Ask FSD before creating one.

Next steps

Today you can look up your expiration date, confirm who gets the reminder emails, and put renewal on a calendar.

Do it yourself today:

  1. Open the Entity Status Tracker on SAM.gov, enter your UEI and write down the expiration date.
  2. Sign in and confirm the Entity Administrator is a current employee with a working inbox. Add a backup admin if you can.
  3. Create a calendar event for 60 days before expiration, titled "SAM renewal – allow 10 business days," and invite your finance lead.

If you'd rather not do it yourself: use the comparison table in the fee section above to pick a free or paid option, and book it the same week the 60-day email arrives so appointment waits don't eat into your validation buffer.

FAQ

Is there a SAM.gov registration renewal guide from the government?

Yes. The Federal Service Desk publishes a Quick Start Guide for Updating Entity Registrations that walks through Sign In, Entity Workspace and the Update action (FSD). SAM.gov's Entity Registration page links to a registration checklist and FAQs.

How much does SAM.gov registration renewal cost?

Nothing. SAM.gov doesn't charge for registration, renewal or updates (DOJ JustGrants). Fee requests come from private companies, and the BBB has warned about some of them (BBB).

Why did I get a SAM.gov registration fee notice?

Your business name and expiration date are public, so private firms send official-looking renewal notices. Paying them doesn't renew your record. Only submitting the update on sam.gov does.

How often is SAM gov entity registration renewal required?

Every 365 days (SAM.gov). For grant recipients, 2 CFR 25.200 requires an active registration for the whole time you have an active award or an application under review (2 CFR 25.200).

How do I check my SAM.gov registration status?

Enter your UEI or CAGE code in the public Entity Status Tracker on SAM.gov, or sign in and open Entity Workspace. "Active" means you're eligible. "Submitted" means validation is still running, and "Inactive" means the registration expired (GSA).
