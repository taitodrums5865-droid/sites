Fourteen days is not long for a public tender. If a notice here is the first you have heard of it, check how much work it needs before you start. A short quote for goods can be done in a day; a framework bid with method statements and social value answers usually cannot.

Which rules apply

Most new procurements started on or after 24 February 2025 run under the Procurement Act 2023. Older procurements that began before that date can still follow the previous regulations to the end. The notice tells you which regime applies, and that changes the process, the documents and the timetable.

Under the Procurement Act, suppliers register on the Central Digital Platform and share their core information there once, rather than repeating it in every bid. Registration is free.

Before the closing date

Look for the clarification question deadline. It usually falls several days before tenders close, and buyers rarely answer questions after it. Check the submission method as well: most buyers use their own e-tendering portal, and you may need to register there separately. Late tenders are normally rejected, even by a minute, so upload early and keep the portal's confirmation.

Read the award criteria and their weightings. Price is only part of the score on most contracts, and quality questions, including social value, often decide the result.

After the award

When the buyer publishes a contract award notice under the Procurement Act, a standstill period of eight working days usually follows before the contract can be signed. Bidders get an assessment summary explaining their scores. Our list of contracts in standstill now shows which awards are still inside that window, and the standstill calculator works out the end date for any notice.
