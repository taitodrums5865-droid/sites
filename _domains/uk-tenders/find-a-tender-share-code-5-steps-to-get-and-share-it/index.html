A Find a Tender share code is a code you create in your supplier account so a chosen public sector buyer can access your stored supplier information. To get one, complete your Supplier Information, select "Share my information", then "Create a share code". You then give it to the buyer in the way the tender documents specify, which is usually by entering it in their tendering portal.

Key points

  • Create it via Share my information, then Create a share code
  • Change your details and you must create a new share code
  • Find a Tender is free; no one should pay to view opportunities

Best for: UK SMEs, sole traders and VCSEs bidding for public contracts

What is Find a Tender service?

Find a Tender is the UK government service where public sector buyers publish procurement notices, and it is also the central digital platform that holds your supplier information. GOV.UK describes it as the UK's central digital platform for public procurement, opened in enhanced form on 24 February 2025.

You can search notices first and register later. A GOV.UK One Login is needed to register an organisation, save searches or receive email notifications, and GOV.UK says you only need to register when you want to bid.

Some official numbers help you read the service, as of November 2026. There are 17 notice types across the procurement cycle, and a UK1 pipeline notice is mandatory for in-scope authorities' intended requirements over £2 million.

Pitfall: an account from before February 2025 doesn't carry over automatically. Previously registered users must register again and enter their organisation details, although saved searches carry over if you use the same email address. What is a tender share code?

A tender share code is a unique code that lets a buyer or their eSender access the supplier information you hold on Find a Tender. The service's design history describes it as "a unique code that they can share with a Buyer and/or E-Sender". Its purpose is to spare you from completing the same information for each competition.

Buyers ask for it because the law gives them a duty to confirm you've done this. Under regulation 6 of the Procurement Regulations 2024, a supplier must register, submit up-to-date core supplier information and share it through the platform. For open and competitive flexible procedures, the buyer must have that confirmation before the end of the tendering period. Our competitive tendering documents guide on the selection questionnaire shows where this usually appears in a bid.

Two things it is not:

  • A bid. You must still complete your bid in the form and manner the buyer requires.
  • A quality mark. The platform doesn't verify your information, and buyers remain responsible for their own due diligence.

For example, a two-person catering company bidding to a council and an NHS trust in the same month keeps one set of details on Find a Tender. It gives each buyer a code, and writes each bid separately.

How to get a share code on Find a Tender

Create a GOV.UK One Login, register your organisation, complete your Supplier Information, make the declaration, then create the code. The labels below come from GOV.UK's three-step registration guide and detailed walkthrough. Live screens can change, so go by the current wording if they differ.

Have this ready

  • Your Companies House number or equivalent, and your registered address
  • A shared organisation email address, plus your own individual work email
  • Your VAT number if you're registered, and your website if you have one
  • Accounts for the last two years, if you have them
  • Details of anyone with significant control or influence over the business
  • Any qualifications or trade assurances, with awarding body and date
  • Details of any convictions or offences involving the organisation or connected people

The 5 steps

  1. Create your login. Open Find a Tender sign-in. Select "Sign in", or "Create your Gov.UK One Login". Enter your individual work email and the 6-digit code you're sent, then set a password and choose text message or authentication app for security codes.
  2. Register the organisation. Agree to the service privacy policy and enter your name. On "My account", select "Register an organisation", then "Supplier" (not Buyer). Answer the Companies House question, check the name and address, add the shared email, then select "Save".
  3. Complete Supplier Information. Select "View" next to the organisation, then "Complete Supplier Information". Work through Basic Information, Connected Persons, Qualifications, Trade Assurances, Exclusions and Financial Information.
  4. Declare and create the code. Select "Share my information", then "Create a share code". Read the declaration and tick the box. Enter your name, job title, work email and the organisation's postal address. Check your answers and select "Confirm and get share code".
  5. Share it the way the buyer asked. Copy the code into the buyer's tendering portal, or download the supplier information file if they want an email or attachment. The buyer decides the method.

Common mistake: entering a personal mailbox as the organisation email. Use a shared address your team can access, and keep your individual work email for your own login.

Where can I find my share code?

Sign in, select "View" next to your organisation, then select "Share my information", where previously generated codes are listed with their creation dates. If no code appears, you haven't created one yet, so finish the declaration steps above instead of searching old emails.

  1. Go to Find a Tender sign-in and sign in with your One Login.
  2. On "My account", select "View" next to the right organisation. Each organisation you register has its own administrators and users, so check the name.
  3. Select "Share my information" and compare the latest code's date with the date you last changed anything.
  4. If you've changed details since then, or you're unsure, update them and select "Create a share code".

Treat any code in an old email as a lead to check, and have at least two people with Editor or Administrator access so a holiday doesn't stall a deadline.

What does a share code look like?

A share code is what Find a Tender displays on the share code screen after you select "Confirm and get share code"; GOV.UK calls it "a number" entered into the buyer's tendering portal. None of the official guides publish a length or format, and the walkthrough shows no sample, so don't judge a code by its shape. Copy and paste it exactly as displayed.

What information does a share code share with buyers?

A share code lets a buyer see your core supplier information: basic details, financial standing, connected persons and exclusion grounds. Statutory guidance groups core supplier information into those four categories, and the account adds qualifications and trade assurances.

What information does a share code share with buyers?. Source: Find a Tender and Contracts Finder
Shared through your supplier informationNot part of it
Name, address, Companies House or equivalent number, VAT number if applicable, and SME, VCSE or public mutual statusYour method statements and quality answers
Accounts you uploaded, with audit status and year-end dateYour pricing

Mandatory or discretionary exclusions, for you or connected persons, and how they're being managedAnswers about associated persons and key subcontractors, which you may need to gather separately
Qualifications and trade assurances you addedYour information, for any buyer you didn't choose

The right-hand column follows from how the service was designed. Supplier information is used alongside any bid information you separately make available, and it is visible only to contracting authorities you choose. The declaration also asks for your name, job title and work email, so it's sensible to assume those details can reach the buyer as well.

Accuracy falls to you. You declare that the information is correct, and you remain responsible for keeping shared data accurate. If something changes after you've shared it but before award, the buyer must get further confirmation that you've submitted and shared the updated information. A new director with significant control, or a new set of filed accounts, is a good prompt to review everything.

How do I check a share code?

Sign in, review the information held against your organisation, and confirm the code you plan to send is the latest one on "Share my information". The factsheet says your information can't be accessed outside the sharing route, and the guides describe no public code checker, so your account is the place to check.

Supplier checklist before you submit

  • [ ] You're in the correct organisation, if you registered more than one.
  • [ ] Every one of the six sections is complete and current.
  • [ ] Your latest accounts are uploaded, with the right year-end date.
  • [ ] Connected persons and exclusions have been reviewed, including any recent changes.
  • [ ] The code's creation date is later than your most recent edit.
  • [ ] The tender documents have been read for the required method: code, file or both.
  • [ ] You've pasted the code and compared it with the screen, with no stray spaces.
  • [ ] You've met the buyer's deadline, because buyers can set an earlier date than the legal latest point.

GOV.UK only says you must create a new code when your information changes. Tenders Direct, a commercial tender service, goes further and suggests a new code for every bid. Creating one per bid is the choice to make: the process takes you through the declaration again and gives the buyer an unmistakably recent date.

What the buyer does with it

The buyer or their eSender uses the code to access your supplier information, and what they see depends partly on the portal. One buyer guide on the Find a Tender test site, seen only as a search excerpt, says a code that isn't the latest carries a warning triangle showing when it was created, and that supplier data is downloaded one supplier at a time. Treat that as indicative, but it's another reason to send your newest code.

Most share code problems come from an out-of-date or mistyped code, the wrong organisation, unfinished Supplier Information or a portal issue on the buyer's side. Who to contact, and in what order

  1. Create a new code and try once more. Do this as soon as the buyer raises a problem, because it rules out the most likely cause.
  2. Tell the buyer straight away. Use the contact route in the tender documents, and do it well before the deadline. Ask whether they will accept the supplier information file instead; only the buyer can agree to that.
  3. Use the portal's help for portal errors. If the code is right on Find a Tender and the portal rejects it, the buyer or their eSender is the place to ask.
  4. Use the Find a Tender contact form for account faults. The Contact us form takes your name, email, organisation and a message of up to 10,000 characters. It states no response time, so don't rely on it alone when a deadline is close. The Supplier Registration team can't help with Find a Tender queries.
  5. Use One Login support for sign-in faults. Call 0300 373 9020, Monday to Friday, 8am to 8pm UK time, or use webchat or the contact form.

Example message to a buyer, with the bracketed parts to replace:

We are bidding for [tender title, reference]. Our share code, created on [date], is [code]. You've told us it isn't working. We have checked it against our Find a Tender account and created it from [organisation name]. The file is available if you'd accept it instead. Please tell us what the system shows, and whether this affects the [date] deadline.

Keep the dated message. If a dispute later turns on your timing, it shows when you raised the problem.

Next steps

Your next job is to get your account bid-ready before a live deadline makes it urgent.

Do these today:

  1. Sign in at Find a Tender, or start registering, and agree who your first administrator will be.
  2. Collect your accounts, connected person details and exclusion information in one folder, then complete Supplier Information.
  3. Save one search for your sector and region so you have a real opportunity to aim at when the account is ready.

If an adviser helps you, add them through "Manage users" with their own login, and never hand over your One Login details. You still own the declaration and its accuracy.

FAQ

Do I need the Find a Tender FTS system in order to receive a share code?

Yes. The code is created inside your supplier account on Find a Tender, using "Share my information" and then "Create a share code". The Supplier Registration Service is a separate website and won't give you one. A buyer's portal only receives the code.

Is a Find a Tender service share code the same as a tender code or a tender reference number?

No. A tender reference identifies an opportunity, while a share code gives a buyer access to your supplier information. Your PPON is different again, since it identifies your organisation. If a form asks for a "code" and doesn't say which, ask the buyer before you submit.

Do sole traders, charities and social enterprises need a share code too?

If the tender requires core supplier information to be shared through the platform, yes, because the regulation 6 steps apply to "the supplier" with no carve-out by size in those steps. Sole traders can select "individual supplier", and charities can use a charity number as an equivalent. Below-threshold bids are the main exception, where only the winner registers.

Does it cost anything to get a share code or register to bid for public sector contracts?

No platform fee applies. GOV.UK says Find a Tender is free for everyone, and nobody should pay to view public sector opportunities (as of November 2026; check the official page). Paid tender alert or consultancy services are optional extras. A fee for one of those doesn't change your position with the buyer.
