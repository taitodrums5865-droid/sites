To win council tenders, register free on your council's e-tendering portal and on Find a Tender. Then aim first at below-threshold contracts (under £207,720 including VAT for council goods and services in 2026), where competition is lighter and councils can now limit bidding to local suppliers. Win them by answering every scored question in the order the council asks it, making social value commitments that are local and measurable, and pricing to the published price/quality split rather than simply going low.

The rest of this guide covers the council-only routes that general "win government contracts" advice tends to skip: local supplier portals, Meet the Buyer days, the £30,000 notice rule and how councils score local social value.

Key points

  • Councils must post notices for contracts of £30,000+ before advertising
  • English councils can reserve below-threshold contracts for local firms
  • Manchester weights social value at 20% plus 10% for zero carbon

Best for: UK SMEs, sole traders and VCSEs bidding for council work

Choose your first move based on your situation and capacity. Most small suppliers should start with their own council's portal, not with national frameworks.

How to Win Tenders: The Fundamentals Councils Expect

Winning any tender comes down to matching the scoring criteria exactly, evidencing past performance, and pricing realistically against the specification rather than guessing. Councils apply the same basics as central government. They just apply them to smaller contracts.

Under the Procurement Act 2023, which took effect on 24 February 2025, contracts are awarded to the "most advantageous tender" judged against award criteria the buyer has published, not simply the cheapest (Find a Tender factsheet, Cabinet Office). Our /most-advantageous-tender-5-steps-and-a-scoring-example/ guide works through the scoring maths.

Before any council-specific tactics:

  1. Register once on the Central Digital Platform. Go to Find a Tender, sign in with GOV.UK One Login, complete every Supplier information section, then choose Share my information and then Create a share code (Cabinet Office supplier FAQ). Buyers will ask for this code with above-threshold bids.
  2. Collect your evidence pack. That means insurance certificates, two or three short case studies with figures, your policies (health and safety, safeguarding, equality, environmental) and accreditations such as Cyber Essentials if you handle council data (see /cyber-essentials-public-sector-tender-5-steps-and-costs/).
  3. Make a bid/no-bid decision every time. Check the contract value, location, the insurance levels they want, the deadline and the scoring split before you write anything.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: bidding for everything in your sector. Fix: only bid where you can show at least one comparable job.
  • Mistake: letting the share code go stale. Fix: the Cabinet Office tells suppliers to create a new share code whenever their supplier information changes (Cabinet Office FAQ).

How to Win Government Tenders vs Council Tenders: What's Different

Central government tenders are usually larger and often run through Crown Commercial Service frameworks. Council tenders are smaller, frequently below the threshold, and put far more weight on local economic and social value. The legal thresholds differ as well.

There are three practical differences. The council threshold is higher, so a £150,000 council services contract is still below threshold, with lighter rules, while the same contract for a government department would be fully regulated. Each council writes its own social value policy, so the questions change from one council to the next. And councils usually run their own e-tendering portal alongside Find a Tender. How to Get on a Council's Local Supplier List

Most councils run a free e-tendering portal, such as ProContract, In-tend or Delta eSourcing, that works as their supplier list. Registering puts you in front of buyers sending out low-value quotes. A few councils still keep a separate approved list, but in most places the portal is the list.

Two published examples show how this looks in practice. Cambridgeshire County Council says suppliers "must register on ProContract" to respond to opportunities, and that registration is "free of charge and straightforward". It uses standard terms below £100,000, asks social value questions above £100,000, and publishes a pipeline of contracts over £500,000 (Cambridgeshire County Council). Bristol City Council also uses ProContract for contracts over £25,000 (Bristol City Council).

Steps to get listed

  1. Search "[your council name] supplying to the council" and open the procurement page. Look for the portal name and the value bands.
  2. Open the portal. For ProContract that's procontract.due-north.com. Choose Register and create a supplier account.
  3. Choose your categories carefully. Most portals ask for CPV codes or categories, and these decide which alerts you get. Our /cpv-codes-uk-5-steps-to-pick-codes-and-set-alerts-2026/ guide shows how to pick them.
  4. Register with neighbouring councils too. Many county areas share a single portal across districts. Under the 2026 local-reservation rules, "local" can include bordering counties (more on this below).
  5. Check the council's contracts register. The Local Government Transparency Code requires councils to publish contracts worth more than £5,000 and invitations to tender above £5,000 (GOV.UK, Local Government Transparency Code 2015). Expiry dates in that register tell you when contracts will come back to market.

Example (illustrative): a decorating firm in Ashford would register on Ashford Borough Council's portal (Ashford Borough Council), then on the portals used by Kent County Council and the neighbouring districts, choosing the painting and decorating categories on each.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: letting alerts go to a shared inbox nobody reads. Fix: send them to one named person who checks daily, because quote windows can be short.
  • Mistake: treating registration as the finish line. Fix: registration only gets you invited. You still need to answer each request well.

How to Find and Win Below-Threshold Council Tenders

Council contracts below the £207,720 threshold (in 2026) have lighter rules and smaller fields. From £30,000 up, the council must publish a below-threshold tender notice on Find a Tender before advertising anywhere else (below-threshold guidance). Under £30,000, opportunities often appear only on the council's own portal or come as direct requests for quotes.

The official rules (as of October 2026):

  • No shortlisting by suitability. For below-threshold contracts, councils can't restrict who submits a tender based on a suitability assessment (Procurement Act 2023, section 88). Every compliant bid gets assessed. That makes it a realistic entry point if you lack a long track record.
  • Find a Tender carries the notices. From 24 February 2025, Find a Tender publishes both above- and below-threshold notices for new procurements, except below-threshold notices in Scotland (Cabinet Office factsheet). Contracts Finder still holds notices for procurements started under the old rules. See /contracts-finder-vs-find-a-tender-2026-table-and-steps/.
  • Registration at award. From 1 April 2026, a supplier awarded a notifiable below-threshold contract must be registered on the Central Digital Platform and have a unique identifier, which goes into the contract details notice. This is required at award, not as a condition of bidding (Browne Jacobson).
  • Local reservation in England. Since 4 February 2026, the Local Government (Exclusion of Non-commercial Considerations) (England) Order 2026 lets councils reserve below-threshold competitions for UK-based suppliers or suppliers based in their local area (Sharpe Pritchard). The local area can be the council's own area plus "any of the areas of the counties or London boroughs that border that area". Councils can also reserve competitions for SMEs (fewer than 250 staff and turnover under £44 million), for VCSEs, or for both (GOV.UK guidance).

Steps

  1. On Find a Tender, search your keywords and narrow by location and notice type to see below-threshold tender notices from councils near you.
  2. Open the notice and read the Conditions of participation field. A reserved contract will say "This contract is reserved to suppliers based within [UK or specified local area]" (GOV.UK guidance).
  3. Follow the link to the council's portal, where the actual documents and clarification questions are handled.
  4. If you're registered on the Central Digital Platform, have your identifier ready. If you aren't, register before you could be awarded.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: assuming a reserved contract will take you because you're "nearby". Fix: "based within" means having substantive business operations there, regardless of where the owners are (GOV.UK guidance). Be ready to show a local address, staff or premises.
  • Mistake: looking only at Find a Tender. Fix: under £30,000 there is no notice duty, so the portal and direct invitations matter more.

How to Use Meet the Buyer Events to Win Council Work

Meet the Buyer events let SMEs build relationships with council procurement teams and hear early about upcoming tenders. You still have to win with a compliant, well-scored bid afterwards. Treat the event as research, not a sales pitch.

Events are usually free and need booking. For example, the Borough Council of King's Lynn & West Norfolk is running a Meet the Buyer event on Wednesday 18 November 2026, 9am to 1pm, at King's Lynn Town Hall (West Norfolk Council). In Scotland, the Supplier Development Programme ran its national Meet the Buyer event in Glasgow on 2 June 2026, with free tickets booked in advance (Scottish Government procurement blog). Councils also run formal pre-market engagement on specific contracts, which Cambridgeshire describes as "a good opportunity for suppliers to learn more about what is going to be procured" (Cambridgeshire County Council).

Steps to turn an event into a bid

  1. Before you go, read the council's contracts register and pipeline. Write down two or three contracts in your area that are close to expiry.
  2. Book any 1:1 slot offered, and prepare three questions. For example: "When is the grounds maintenance contract due to be retendered?", "Will it be split into lots?" and "Which portal will it be on?"
  3. Take down the name of the category manager, not just a general procurement inbox.
  4. Within a week, register on the portal they named and set alerts for those categories.
  5. When the tender comes out, use the clarification facility on the portal for questions. Once a procurement is live, buyers have to treat all bidders equally, so informal contact stops.

Example (illustrative): a cleaning social enterprise learns at an event that a library cleaning contract will be split into lots by area. It then bids for the single lot nearest its base instead of the whole borough.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: spending the day handing out brochures. Fix: collect information on timings, lots and portals. That's what helps you bid.
  • Mistake: lobbying during a live tender. Fix: use the portal's clarification route only.

How to Score Well on Social Value in Council Tenders

Councils often give social value a large share of the score, but unlike central government no national minimum applies to them. Each council sets its own weighting and measures. Winning answers make specific, measurable local commitments, not general CSR statements.

Real numbers (as of October 2026):

  • Central government: PPN 002 requires at least 10% of the total score for social value in procurements starting on or after 1 October 2025 (PPN 002). Other public bodies, including councils, can choose whether to follow it (Tussell).
  • Manchester City Council: applies 20% to social value in its tenders plus a further 10% weighting for zero carbon commitments (Manchester City Council Social Value Policy 2025; Social Value Portal).
  • Cambridgeshire: includes social value questions on contracts over £100,000 (Cambridgeshire County Council). Bristol uses its own Bristol Social Value Measures spreadsheet (Bristol City Council).
  • National TOMs: many councils use the National TOMs framework from the Social Value Portal, built with LGA support. It has 5 themes, 20 outcomes and 48 measures, each with a proxy value (LGA, National TOMs guidance). The 2021 framework values a local person in employment at £31,461 (Stroud District Council appendix).

Steps

  1. Find the council's social value policy and see whether it uses National TOMs, the government Social Value Model or its own list.
  2. Pick two or three measures you can actually deliver at this contract's size. A £60,000 contract can't credibly promise ten apprenticeships.
  3. For each, write: the measure, a number, a local place, a date and how you will report. For example: "One work placement of at least 6 weeks for a [council area] resident aged 16–24, through the council's employment hub, by month 4. Reported quarterly."
  4. Give evidence that you've done similar things before, such as local spend percentages or past placements.

For worked answers, see /social-value-in-tenders-ppn-002-steps-and-answer-examples/.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: "We are committed to our community." Fix: swap every adjective for a number, a place and a date.
  • Mistake: promising what you can't track. Fix: only commit to measures you can report on, because councils check delivery during the contract.

How to Write a Winning Council Tender Response (PQQ and ITT)

A winning response answers each scored question directly, in the same order as the invitation to tender (ITT). It uses the council's own wording and backs every claim with evidence such as case studies and figures. Assertions alone don't score.

A note on terms: "PQQ" is the old name for a selection stage. Above threshold, the Procurement Act uses a standard selection questionnaire that draws on your Central Digital Platform information (see /selection-questionnaire-procurement-act-2023-7-sme-steps/). Below threshold, councils can't shortlist on suitability (section 88), so you'll mostly see a single-stage request for quotation or ITT.

Steps

  1. Build a compliance checklist from the ITT: every document, word or page limit, format, signature and the deadline (date and time). Missing one can rule out your bid.
  2. Copy each question and its scoring guidance into your draft as headings. Write to the top band of the descriptor, which usually reads something like "comprehensive, with evidence, minimal risk".
  3. Open each answer with a direct answer, then describe how you will deliver it, who will do it, when, and the evidence.
  4. Mirror their language. If the specification says "reactive repairs within 24 hours", write "reactive repairs within 24 hours", not "quick response".
  5. Add a short case study per answer: the client type, the scope, a result in numbers and a contact if referees are requested.
  6. Ask clarification questions through the portal before the clarification deadline.
  7. Review against the checklist, then upload early. Portals can be slow in the last hour.
  8. Ask for feedback whether you win or lose. Above threshold, the Act requires an assessment summary for each bidder (Cabinet Office factsheet). Below threshold, ask politely.

Example structure (illustrative) for "Describe how you will mobilise this contract (10%)": a one-line commitment ("Fully mobilised in 3 weeks from award"), then a week-by-week table, the named lead, the risks and how you'll handle them, and a past mobilisation with dates. Templates are in /tender-writing-examples-5-uk-sme-answer-templates-2026/.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: reusing a generic answer from another council. Fix: rename and re-evidence it for this council's area, staff and specification.
  • Mistake: going over word limits. Fix: evaluators may stop reading at the limit, so cut background, not evidence.

How to Price a Council Tender Without Underbidding

Councils usually score price alongside quality, for example 60% quality and 40% price, so the lowest price doesn't automatically win. A very low price can also prompt the council to ask whether you can really deliver for that amount. Always use the split and formula published for that specific tender.

The Procurement Act lets buyers set their own award criteria and weightings, as long as they're published. Councils can also investigate abnormally low tenders and may disqualify them if you can't justify the price (Cabinet Office factsheet). Remember that in a council with a policy like Manchester's, social value and zero carbon together take 30% of the score (Manchester City Council). That leaves less weight for price than many SMEs assume.

The table below shows how weightings change the best pricing approach. The weightings are illustrative; always check the tender's own award criteria.

Example (illustrative): under a 40/60 price/quality split where the price score is (lowest price ÷ your price) × 40, bidding 10% above the lowest bid costs you about 3.6 points. You can win those back with one stronger quality answer.

Steps

  1. Find the price formula and any minimum quality threshold in the ITT.
  2. Cost the full specification: labour at the council's required rates (some require the Real Living Wage), insurance, travel, materials and mobilisation.
  3. Model your score at three price points against a realistic competitor price.
  4. Check payment terms. Under the Act, contracting authorities must pay within 30 days of a valid invoice (Blake Morgan). Plan your cash flow on that basis.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Mistake: leaving cells blank in the pricing schedule. Fix: fill every line, using "0" or "included" where needed, so the bid isn't marked non-compliant.
  • Mistake: putting price information in the quality answers. Fix: keep price only in the pricing document if the ITT says so.

Next steps

Start with one council, one portal and one well-matched contract. That's enough to get moving this week.

Do it yourself today

  1. Open your council's "supplying to the council" page and register free on the portal it names, choosing your categories carefully.
  2. Create or update your Central Digital Platform supplier information on Find a Tender and generate a share code.
  3. Download your council's contracts register and note three contracts that expire in the next 12 months.

If you would rather not do it all yourself

  • Supplier Development Programme (Scotland): free events and training for Scottish SMEs (SDP). It suits small firms in Scotland. Outside Scotland, check what your local growth hub or council offers.
  • Tender tracking software, e.g. Stotles: a free plan covers tender tracking. The paid Basic plan starts at about £75–£99 a month and Growth from £475 a month (as of October 2026; check the official pricing page). It suits suppliers who check many buyers at once. It's probably not worth paying for if you only bid with one or two councils, because their portal alerts are free. One limitation: it finds opportunities but doesn't write or price your bid.
  • A freelance bid writer: useful for a first large ITT if you have the evidence but not the time. It suits you less if you lack case studies, since no writer can make those up. Fees vary widely, so ask for a fixed quote per tender and samples of past answers.

FAQ

How do I find out which councils accept unregistered suppliers?

Under £30,000 there's no national notice duty, and many councils will ask for a quote from a supplier who isn't on their portal. In practice, though, you'll need a portal account to receive and return documents. From 1 April 2026, you must be registered on the Central Digital Platform if you're awarded a notifiable below-threshold contract (£30,000+ for councils), but not to bid (Browne Jacobson). Check each council's procurement page for its own rules.

What is the difference between Find a Tender and Contracts Finder?

Find a Tender is now the Central Digital Platform. It publishes above- and below-threshold notices for procurements started from 24 February 2025, except below-threshold notices in Scotland (Cabinet Office factsheet). Contracts Finder still holds notices for procurements started under the old regulations. See /contracts-finder-vs-find-a-tender-2026-table-and-steps/.

Do charities and social enterprises need to register differently to bid for council contracts?

No. VCSEs register on the same council portals and the same Central Digital Platform as businesses. The difference is that councils can reserve below-threshold competitions for VCSEs, SMEs or both (GOV.UK guidance), so look for that in the notice.

How often do councils update their local supplier lists?

Portal-based lists change whenever suppliers register, so there's usually no annual window to wait for. Contract information moves on a quarterly cycle, because the Transparency Code requires councils to publish invitations to tender over £5,000 every quarter (GOV.UK). Older-style approved lists may have set review dates, so check your council's page.

Can a sole trader bid for council tenders without being a limited company?

Yes. Nothing in the Procurement Act requires a limited company, and council portals accept sole traders. You'll be held to the same requirements on insurance, policies and capacity as everyone else. Check the insurance amounts in the ITT before you bid.
