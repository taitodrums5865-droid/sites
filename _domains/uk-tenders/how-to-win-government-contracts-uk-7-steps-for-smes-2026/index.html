How to Win Government Contracts UK: 7 Steps for SMEs (2026)

To win government contracts in the UK, first register for free on Find a Tender, the central digital platform. Then set up saved searches on your CPV codes and bid for small, local contracts where your evidence fits the published award criteria. Under the Procurement Act 2023, central government buyers must advertise contracts worth £12,000 or more including VAT, and other public bodies must advertise from £30,000 (GOV.UK below-threshold guidance). You can find most of these opportunities yourself, and searching, registering and alerts cost nothing (Central Digital Platform factsheet).

Key points

  • Central government must advertise contracts from £12,000 incl. VAT
  • Below-threshold tenders can't use a pre-qualification stage
  • Standstill lasts at least 8 working days after a UK6 award notice

Best for: UK SMEs, sole traders and VCSEs bidding for public contracts

What Government Contracts Can SMEs Actually Get in the UK?

SMEs can bid for almost any advertised UK public contract, and the most realistic wins sit below the procurement thresholds. Those contracts must still be published on Find a Tender once they pass £12,000 (central government) or £30,000 (other public bodies) including VAT (GOV.UK).

Contracts fall into two bands, and the band changes how you bid.

The below-threshold band is where most SMEs should start. GOV.UK states that buyers running a regulated below-threshold contract "cannot restrict the submission of tenders by reference to suppliers' suitability". In practice, nobody can knock you out at a pre-qualification stage for lacking three years of accounts. The same guidance gives these contracts implied 30-day payment terms (section 88) (GOV.UK).

Where the notices live (as of October 2026):

  • Find a Tender is the central digital platform. Every procurement started under the Procurement Act 2023 on or after 24 February 2025 is published there, above and below threshold, except below-threshold notices in Scotland (GOV.UK below-threshold guidance; Find a Tender notice types).
  • Contracts Finder still carries notices from procurements that began under the old Public Contracts Regulations 2015, such as later stages and awards. It still has a Procurement Stage filter (Early engagement, Future opportunity, Opportunity, Awarded contract) and a postcode radius of 5–50 miles (Contracts Finder). Treat it as a second check, not your main feed.

A pitfall to avoid: not everything is advertised. A buyer doesn't have to publish a below-threshold notice if it only invites quotes from a closed group of pre-selected suppliers and doesn't advertise the work any other way (GOV.UK). The fix is to get onto those quote lists. Join a DPS such as RM6237 (see below), or register on the supplier portal of the councils you want to work with.

The 7 Steps to Win Your First Government Contract (Beginner's Guide)

Here's the beginner path in short: register once, find your CPV codes, save searches, then bid small and local before you try national frameworks. Each step below uses only free official services.

Step 1: Create a GOV.UK One Login. On Find a Tender, choose Sign in. Create a GOV.UK One Login with your work email, enter the six-digit code sent to you, and set up two-factor authentication on a mobile phone (Find a Tender; registration steps summarised from the GOV.UK supplier user guide).

Step 2: Register your organisation. Go to My Organisations → Register an organisation. Have these ready: your Companies House or Charity Commission number (if you have one), registered and postal address, VAT number (if registered), website and organisation type. If you don't have a registration number, as many sole traders don't, the platform can generate a unique identifier (supplier user guide).

Step 3: Complete your core supplier information. This covers basic details, SME/VCSE status, financial accounts, connected persons, qualifications and trade associations, and any exclusion grounds. When a buyer asks for it, generate a share code and paste it into their e-tendering system. It's free, and you won't have to re-enter the same details for every bid (CDP factsheet). You don't need to do this on day one. GOV.UK says suppliers can register "at the point that they want to bid". Still, finishing it before your first deadline saves stress.

Step 4: Pick 3–6 CPV codes. See the next section for how.

Step 5: Build and save searches. On the Find a Tender search page, set Industry CPV code, Contract location, Value (minimum and maximum) and Suitability (SME and/or VCSE). Then choose Save search and turn on email notifications (Find a Tender search results).

Step 6: Qualify each notice before you write anything. Open the UK4 tender notice and check the closing date, the value, the award criteria and weightings, and any mandatory requirements such as insurance levels. If you can't evidence at least one similar job, skip it and wait for the next one.

Step 7: Bid, then learn from the assessment summary. Under the Act, every supplier whose tender was assessed gets an assessment summary with scores and reasons for each criterion, plus the winner's scored information (redacted where needed) (GOV.UK e-learning Module 7; Procurement Pathway). Use it to rewrite your weakest answer before the next bid.

Example (illustrative): a two-person grounds maintenance firm in Leeds could save a search on CPV 77310000, with Contract location set to Yorkshire and the Humber, Value £30,000–£207,720 and Suitability: SME. That would surface council parks and school grounds contracts without manual browsing.

How to Find CPV Codes for Your Business (and Why They Matter)

CPV (Common Procurement Vocabulary) codes classify what's being bought. Saved searches built on the right codes are how you see relevant live tenders without searching by hand. Find a Tender's own filter describes CPV as the system "used to classify contracts in a standardised way" (Find a Tender).

Codes are hierarchical. The first two digits are the division and each further digit narrows it down. A broad code like 72000000 catches all IT services. A narrow one catches far fewer notices, and if the buyer tagged the contract at a different level, you can miss it.

How to find yours:

  1. Search the full code list on SIMAP's CPV page using plain words for what you sell, e.g. "cleaning" or "training".
  2. Cross-check against real notices. On Find a Tender, search a keyword, open 5–10 relevant notices, and note the CPV codes buyers actually used.
  3. Keep one broad code and two to five narrow codes. Then add a keyword search to catch buyers who mis-tag.
  4. Use Procurement stage: Award or Contract with the same codes to see who already holds the work and when it ends.

A common mistake is choosing only the most specific code. If a council tags a cleaning contract as 90900000 (cleaning and sanitation services) and you only follow 90911200, you won't see it. Include the parent code in your search.

How to Write a Winning Bid Response (PQQ and ITT Basics)

Score against the published criteria, not against your brochure. Under the Act, buyers must publish award criteria that are "clear, measurable and specific" and show how much each one counts, through weightings, ranking or another method (GOV.UK Module 7).

The old terms still turn up. A PQQ (pre-qualification questionnaire) is now, above threshold, a set of conditions of participation answered through a selection questionnaire. The ITT (invitation to tender) is the tender documents themselves. For below-threshold contracts there's no pre-qualification stage, so you go straight to the tender response. Our Selection Questionnaire guide covers the above-threshold selection stage in detail.

A practical writing process:

  1. Build a scoring grid. List every question, its weighting and the word limit. Spend your time in proportion to the weighting. A question worth 30% deserves three times the effort of one worth 10%.
  2. Mirror the question's structure. If the question asks for "approach, resources and risk", use those three words as sub-headings in that order.
  3. Prove each claim. Give a named past job (with permission), dates, volumes and the result, e.g. "cleaned 4 primary schools, 2019–2025, no missed cleans reported". Swap "we are passionate about quality" for the process you actually follow.
  4. Answer the specification, not the sector. Copy the buyer's own terms: their site names, service hours, KPIs.
  5. Price exactly as instructed. Use their pricing schedule, check whether values are inclusive or exclusive of VAT, and don't add assumptions in a covering letter.
  6. Use clarification questions before the deadline the buyer sets. Answers are usually published to all bidders, so ask anything that changes how you'd price.
  7. Cover social value where it's scored. Central government contracts use the PPN 002 Social Value Model. See our social value answer examples.

The Act moved evaluation from "most economically advantageous tender" to "most advantageous tender", so cheapest doesn't automatically win (GOV.UK Module 7). If quality is weighted at 60% and price at 40%, a strong method statement can make up for a slightly higher price.

Example (illustrative): say a question asks how you'll handle staff absence, worth 15%. "We have a robust contingency plan" scores weakly. "Two trained relief staff within 25 miles, cover confirmed by 7am, and the buyer's site manager notified by text" gives the evaluator something they can score.

Tender periods are short, so plan backwards. The standard minimum for above-threshold competitive tenders is 25 days. That can drop to 10 days if the buyer published a qualifying planned procurement notice (UK3) or there's genuine urgency (Find a Tender notice types; GOV.UK competitive tendering guidance). That's why it pays to watch UK1 pipeline and UK3 planned procurement notices.

Which Government Contracts Are Easiest for New Suppliers to Win?

Lower-value contracts from councils, schools, NHS trusts and other local bodies are usually the most winnable. They're below threshold, they can't use pre-qualification, and they can be reserved for local SMEs and VCSEs. No contract is "easy", but competition and paperwork are lighter here than on national frameworks.

Under PPN 005 (updated February 2025), in-scope organisations may reserve below-threshold competitions by supplier location. They may also reserve them for SMEs and VCSEs, but only in combination with a location reservation. So a buyer can run a competition open only to SMEs based in a particular region.

Steps to find the easier ones on Find a Tender:

  1. Set Procurement stage to Open opportunities.
  2. Set Value maximum to £207,720, the sub-central threshold for procurements started from 1 January 2026.
  3. Tick Suitability: SME (and VCSE if relevant).
  4. Set Contract location to your region, then use Sort results → Closing (last first) so you see the notices that give you the most time.

This route doesn't suit everyone. If you need a single £1m+ contract to justify the bid cost, below-threshold work will feel small. If you can't show at least one comparable job, even low-value tenders will be hard to score well on. Some firms start with a private-sector or subcontract reference first, and that's a sensible order.

Do You Need to Register on a Framework Before Bidding?

No. Most tender notices on Find a Tender are open competitions anyone can bid on directly. Frameworks are a separate route where you compete once to become a pre-approved supplier, and buyers then call off work from that pool.

Crown Commercial Service became the Government Commercial Agency (GCA) on 1 April 2026. GCA lists four supply routes: frameworks, dynamic purchasing systems, dynamic markets and catalogues (GCA how to supply; GCA launch news). GCA says it connects suppliers to "20,000 UK public sector buyers spending over £37 billion a year" (GCA).

Here's the pitfall. Getting onto a framework takes effort and earns nothing until a buyer runs a call-off. Many SMEs win a framework place and then never receive a call-off. For a beginner, aim for open tenders first. Once you have two or three references, move on to frameworks using our framework agreement guide.

Common Mistakes That Get SME Bids Rejected

Missed deadlines, failed pass/fail requirements and generic answers lose more SME bids than price does. All three can be fixed before you submit.

  • Late or incomplete submission. E-tendering portals close at a fixed time, often midday, and won't accept a late upload. Fix: aim to submit 24 hours early, and check that every attachment has uploaded by viewing the submission summary.
  • Failing a pass/fail condition. Above threshold, conditions of participation can cover insurance levels, financial standing and technical ability. Fix: before writing, list every mandatory item in the tender documents and confirm you meet it. If a stated insurance level is out of reach, ask by clarification whether "willing to obtain on award" is acceptable.
  • Out-of-date core supplier information. Your share code reflects what's on the platform. Fix: update your details in Find a Tender (accounts, connected persons, exclusions) before each bid (CDP factsheet).
  • Generic, copy-pasted method statements. Evaluators score against the question, not the volume of text. Fix: reuse your evidence, but rewrite each answer around the buyer's specification and wording.
  • Pricing errors. Mixing up figures inclusive and exclusive of VAT, or leaving cells blank. Fix: complete the buyer's own pricing template and have a second person check the arithmetic.
  • Ignoring the assessment summary. Fix: compare your score for each criterion with the winner's, and rewrite the answer with the biggest gap.

Example (illustrative): a VCSE bidding for a £45,000 council youth service gives a strong answer, but it describes its work in general terms and doesn't name the council's three target wards. A competitor names them and scores higher on that criterion. Nothing about the service itself was different. The difference was how closely the answer fitted the specification.

What Happens After You Win a Government Contract?

Winning starts a formal sequence. Above threshold, it runs: assessment summary, then a UK6 contract award notice, then a standstill of at least 8 working days, then signature, then a UK7 contract details notice (Find a Tender notice types).

  1. Assessment summary. Every assessed bidder receives one before the award notice is published (GOV.UK Module 7).
  2. UK6 contract award notice. This starts the standstill period where one applies.
  3. Standstill period of at least 8 working days. The contract can't be signed until it ends. That gives unsuccessful bidders time to challenge (this replaced the old 10 calendar days). Use the time to line up staff, insurance and subcontractors. Don't start work yet.
  4. Contract signature and mobilisation. Agree start dates, contacts, KPIs and invoicing details such as purchase order numbers.
  5. UK7 contract details notice. Published within 30 days of signature for above-threshold contracts (120 days for light-touch). For contracts of £5 million or more, a redacted copy of the contract follows within 90 days (GOV.UK Module 7).
  6. Payment and performance. Public contracts carry implied 30-day payment terms: section 68 of the Act above threshold (legislation.gov.uk), section 88 below (GOV.UK). For larger contracts, buyers may publish performance (UK9-type) notices.

For below-threshold contracts, the buyer publishes a contract details notice, and a formal standstill isn't normally part of the process.

One planning tip: 30-day terms run from receipt of a valid, undisputed invoice. If cash flow is tight, find out on day one exactly what a valid invoice must include. A missing PO number is a common cause of delay.

Next steps

Do these today. You don't need a live opportunity to make progress.

  1. Create your GOV.UK One Login and register your organisation on Find a Tender. It's free, and it's the identity every Procurement Act bid will use.
  2. Pick three CPV codes (one broad, two narrow) from SIMAP. Save one Find a Tender search with Suitability: SME, your region and a £207,720 maximum value, and turn on email notifications.
  3. Save a second search with Procurement stage: Award on the same codes, so you can see who wins in your area and at what value.

If you'd rather not do it yourself: start with the free official option. GOV.UK publishes the official Procurement Act 2023 e-learning, and GCA has supplier guidance on how to supply. If you want structured bid-writing training, APMP Foundation is a recognised certification. BidCraft Academy, for example, lists the course alone at £211 and a non-member exam bundle at £770 (as of October 2026; check the official page) (BidCraft Academy). Membership of APMP is required to sit the exam. It suits people who'll write bids regularly or want a career in bid work. It doesn't suit a sole trader planning one or two small bids a year, who will get more from rewriting answers using assessment summaries. Freelance bid writers are another option, but their fees vary widely, and they still need your evidence and pricing to write a credible response.

FAQ

Do I need to be VAT registered to bid for government contracts?

Not as a general rule. Whether you must register for VAT depends on your taxable turnover under HMRC rules, not on who your customers are. The supplier registration on Find a Tender asks for a VAT number as part of basic information where you have one. Contract values in notices are shown including VAT, so check the pricing instructions in each tender to see how the buyer wants VAT handled.

How long do government tenders take to be awarded?

No single timescale applies. For above-threshold competitive tenders, the minimum tender period is usually 25 days, and evaluation takes however long the buyer needs. A standstill of at least 8 working days follows the award notice before signature (Find a Tender notice types). Many tender documents include an indicative timetable, so check it before you plan staffing.

Can a sole trader bid for government contracts?

Yes. Find a Tender registration works without a Companies House number, because the platform can generate a unique identifier for organisations that don't have one. Below-threshold contracts are especially accessible because buyers can't pre-screen suppliers on suitability (GOV.UK). Make sure you can meet any insurance or capacity requirements stated in the tender.

Is there a free alert system for new government tenders?

Yes. Find a Tender lets signed-in users save search criteria and sign up for email notifications at no cost (Find a Tender). GOV.UK states that you "should not pay to view opportunities in the public sector" (CDP factsheet).
