The most useful tender writing examples are short answers that match the buyer's scoring criteria point by point: what you will do, how you will do it, what evidence shows it works, and the result the buyer gets. Below are five annotated UK example answers covering quality, price and social value. Each is marked up against the kind of criteria buyers publish under the Procurement Act 2023, so you can reuse the structure for your own bid. All five are written for illustration, using a fictional company, and none of them is a real submission.

What Does Tender Writing Mean?

Tender writing means writing a structured, evidence-based response to a public buyer's procurement questions. Those answers are scored against published criteria, so a tender is not a general sales pitch.

The legal frame matters, because it tells you what the evaluator is allowed to reward. The Procurement Act 2023 came into force on 24 February 2025 (Lexology). Under the Act, buyers award contracts to the "most advantageous tender". They must set award criteria that relate to the subject matter of the contract and are "sufficiently clear, measurable and specific". They must also publish an assessment methodology showing how tenders will be scored and how much each criterion counts (Welsh Government guidance on assessing competitive tenders). Buyers can refine sub-criteria, but "they cannot add new criteria" (same source). In practice, everything you can score marks for is already printed in the tender pack.

That shapes how you write. A tender answer:

  1. Answers one question, in the order the question asks it.
  2. Gives evidence for every claim, such as a figure, a named process or a past contract.
  3. Stays inside the word or character limit.
  4. Ties each point back to the buyer's own wording.

For example, suppose a council asks: "Describe how you will ensure continuity of service during staff absence (20%)." A sales-style reply says "We pride ourselves on reliability." A tender-style reply says "We hold three trained cover staff for this contract (named in Appendix C). They are deployed within 4 hours under our absence procedure, and the contract manager confirms each deployment to you by email." Only the second one gives the evaluator something to score.

For a worked example of how quality and price percentages combine into a final score, see Most Advantageous Tender: 5 Steps and a Scoring Example.

5 Annotated Tender Writing Examples (Quality, Price & Social Value Answers)

Below are five short example answers, each mapped line by line to common UK scoring criteria. All of them follow the claim–evidence–outcome structure that evaluators reward. The company ("Brightway Facilities Ltd", a 12-person cleaning SME), the contract and every number are invented for illustration. Swap in your own facts. Never copy figures you can't prove.

Many buyers score on a descriptor scale. The scale below is an example only. Every buyer publishes its own, so always use the one in your pack.

Example scoreTypical descriptor
0No answer, or the answer does not address the question
1Major gaps; little evidence
2Partly meets the requirement; generic
3Meets the requirement with some evidence
4Fully meets it, with specific evidence
5Fully meets it, with specific evidence and added value relevant to this contract

The jump from 3 to 5 nearly always comes from specificity: names, numbers, timescales and proof.

Example 1: Quality, service delivery methodology

Question (example): "Describe your methodology for delivering the cleaning service across the 4 sites, including (a) scheduling, (b) quality monitoring and (c) handling complaints. Max 500 words. Weighting 25%."

(a) Scheduling. We will clean all four sites between 06:00 and 08:30, Monday to Friday, before your staff arrive. Each site has a fixed lead cleaner and a named reserve. Rotas are published in our scheduling app 14 days ahead and shared with your facilities manager. (b) Quality monitoring. Supervisors audit every site weekly against a 30-point checklist (Appendix A). Scores below 90% trigger a re-clean within 24 hours. You will receive a monthly dashboard of audit scores per site. (c) Complaints. Complaints go to one phone number and email address. We acknowledge them within 2 working hours and resolve them within 1 working day. Each complaint is logged with its root cause and reviewed at our monthly contract meeting with you.

Annotations:

  • The headings (a), (b) and (c) mirror the question, so the evaluator finds each sub-criterion without hunting. GCA's guidance asks for exactly this: "break down your answer into the same component parts and clearly mark them" (GCA).
  • Every claim explains how, with a time, a threshold or a document. GCA specifically warns against "lists of 'what' you do with no explanation of 'how'" (same source).
  • "You" and "your" keep the answer focused on the buyer.

Example 2: Quality, mobilisation and transition

Question (example): "Set out your mobilisation plan for the first 6 weeks, including TUPE and risks. Max 400 words. Weighting 15%."

Weeks 1–2: A named mobilisation lead (Sarah Ahmed, Operations Manager) holds a kick-off meeting with you and the outgoing contractor. We request TUPE employee liability information on day 1. Weeks 3–4: We hold one-to-one meetings with every transferring employee, issue uniforms and ID, and deliver induction training (COSHH, lone working). Weeks 5–6: We shadow-run the service on all sites, audit each site and sign off readiness with you before go-live. Top risk: late TUPE data. Mitigation: we escalate to you on day 5 if it has not arrived, and our reserve pool covers the gap in the meantime.

Annotations: A time-boxed plan with a named owner is easy to score. Naming the single biggest risk and its mitigation shows realism, which a generic "we have robust processes" never does.

Example 3: Quality, relevant experience (case study)

Question (example): "Provide one example of a comparable contract delivered in the last 3 years and the outcomes achieved. Max 300 words. Weighting 10%."

Situation: In 2024–25 we cleaned a 3-site GP federation (about 2,400 m²) under a 2-year contract. Task: The client needed infection-control audit scores above 95%. Action: We introduced colour-coded equipment, weekly supervisor audits and quarterly refresher training. Result: Audit scores averaged 97% over 12 months. The client extended the contract for a further year. A reference is available from the Practice Manager (contact details in Appendix D). Relevance to you: Your sites have the same mix of clinical and office space.

Annotations: This is the STAR shape (Situation, Task, Action, Result), covered in more detail below. The last line links the past contract to this buyer's need. Only include outcomes your client would confirm if contacted.

Example 4: Price, pricing narrative and assumptions

Price is usually scored by formula from your pricing schedule, not from prose. Some packs do ask for a short commentary, or let you state your assumptions.

Question (example): "Explain the basis of your pricing and any assumptions. Not scored; used to confirm the price is sustainable."

Our price is based on 1,820 cleaning hours a year across 4 sites, paid at the Real Living Wage, plus consumables at a fixed annual cost. We assume access from 06:00, as stated in the specification (section 3.2). Prices are fixed for Year 1. From Year 2 they are indexed as set out in the contract terms, with no other uplifts.

Annotations: Price answers lose contracts in two ways. One is an arithmetic error between schedules. The other is a price so low that the buyer doubts you can deliver. A clear calculation basis answers both concerns. Never add assumptions that change the specification, because that can make your bid non-compliant.

Example 5: Social value (PPN 002, Outcome 6)

Central government buyers in scope of PPN 002 must give social value at least a 10% weighting of the total score (PPN 002 summary, Tussell). Suppliers must provide "a method statement and project plan, the specific, measurable and time bound commitment(s)" against the outcome the buyer selected (same source).

Question (example): "Outcome 6, Employment and training for those facing employment barriers. Describe your commitment, how you will deliver it and how you will report. Max 500 words. Weighting 10%."

Commitment: Over the 3-year contract we will recruit 2 people who are long-term unemployed or leaving the care system into paid cleaning roles on this contract. We will also offer 6 two-week work placements. Method: We will recruit through the local Jobcentre Plus and a named care-leaver charity (letter of support in Appendix E). Each recruit gets a buddy and Level 2 cleaning training within 3 months. Plan: First recruit by month 4, second by month 12. Two placements each year. Reporting: Quarterly KPI report (hires, retention at 6 months, training completed) for your contract review meeting.

Annotations: Each commitment comes with a number, a deadline and a reporting route. All of it relates to this contract, not to your company's general charity work. Under PPN 002, social value commitments must appear in the contract as terms or KPIs (eSourcing Data summary of PPN 002), so promise only what you can deliver. For more answer examples, see Social Value in Tenders: PPN 002 Steps and Answer Examples.

Where to Find Tender Writing Examples PDF Downloads

The most useful PDFs come from buyers, not from template websites. Look for published contract documents, the specimen questions in tender packs, and your own assessment summaries.

Generic "winning bid" PDFs teach the structure but rarely match the criteria you'll be scored on. These four sources are more useful:

  1. Your own assessment summaries. After a competitive tendering procedure, buyers must give every supplier that submitted an assessed tender an assessment summary before the contract award notice is published (Procurement Pathway). The summary for unsuccessful suppliers includes the information given to the winner on how its tender scored against each criterion, with confidential details redacted (Ward Hadaway). That is the closest thing to a real, scored model answer you will get.
  2. Published contracts over £5m. For contracts worth £5 million or more, buyers must publish a copy of the contract within 90 days, subject to redactions under section 94 (DLA Piper). Schedules sometimes include the winner's method statements or social value commitments.
  3. Find a Tender. Open Find a Tender. Choose Contracts ("Awarded contracts, covering both active and expired agreements") or Notice Search, search your service keyword, then open the notice and its attached documents.
  4. Contracts Finder. For lower-value work, search Contracts Finder and filter to awarded contracts. The two portals are compared in UK Public Procurement: Contracts Finder vs Find a Tender.

Pitfalls:

  • Copying a competitor's text. It won't fit your evidence, and evaluators notice. Copy the structure, never the content.
  • Using PCR 2015-era templates without checking. Contracts started under the old rules carry older terminology. Check the dates on any document you borrow from.
  • Not keeping your own feedback. Save every assessment summary in one folder. After a few bids, your highest-scoring answers become your best template library.

Bid Writing Examples vs Tender Writing Examples: What's the Difference?

"Bid writing examples" usually means a whole proposal, including win themes and an executive summary. "Tender writing examples" focuses on answering the buyer's specific scored questions inside a fixed response template.

The bid profession's vocabulary comes largely from APMP, the Association of Proposal Management Professionals. APMP describes an executive summary as "a short abstract of the main points of the offer aimed at the senior-level decision-makers in the customer's organization". It defines win themes as parts of a win strategy that "consist of a feature, a benefit, and a corresponding proof point" (summary of APMP definitions, APMP Greater Midwest Chapter).

Bid writing (private sector or free-format)Tender writing (UK public sector)
FormatYour own document and designThe buyer's portal, question by question
Executive summaryCentral, often the most-read partUsually not scored unless the pack asks for one
Win themesCarried through the whole documentUsed inside each answer as proof points
LengthFlexibleStrict word or character limits
How it's judgedOverall impressionEach answer scored separately against published criteria

Example: a win theme such as "fastest response in the region" might fill a page of a private-sector proposal. In a public tender, the same theme becomes one evidenced line inside the relevant answer, for example "Average call-out response: 3.2 hours over the past 12 months (helpdesk log, Appendix B)". The evaluator scores each question on its own, so a theme that isn't evidenced inside the answer earns nothing.

Before the tender stage, many procurements also include a selection or conditions-of-participation stage. Those questions are mostly pass/fail and have their own logic, covered in Selection Questionnaire Procurement Act 2023: 7 SME Steps.

Who it suits: businesses with a strong track record but no time to write, and bids worth enough that a few marks matter. Who it doesn't suit: a first-time bidder with a £20k contract, where a £1,000+ fee could wipe out the margin. It also doesn't suit anyone who can't give the writer real evidence, because no writer can invent your case studies.

Pitfalls:

  • Success-fee-only deals. BidMate calls the percentage-of-contract-value model "rare, and worth scrutinising" because the incentives can be misaligned.
  • Very cheap fixed fees. The same source puts realistic output at around 150–250 finished words per hour once research and compliance checks are included. A 5,000-word tender priced at a few hours of work is a warning sign.
  • Handing over everything. You still own the facts, the pricing and the social value commitments, and they become contract terms.

How to Structure a Tender Answer Using the STAR or CAR Method

Buyers reward answers with a clear claim–evidence–outcome pattern, often called STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) or CAR (Challenge, Action, Result). Each paragraph should say what you'll do, how, and what measurable result it delivers.

Use STAR for experience and case-study questions (Example 3). Use claim–how–proof–benefit for method questions (Examples 1 and 2).

Steps for each question:

  1. Open the scoring guidance in the tender pack (often called "Instructions to Tenderers", "Award criteria" or "Evaluation methodology"). Copy the question, the sub-criteria, the weighting and the word or character limit into the top of your draft.
  2. Make one sub-heading per sub-criterion, in the buyer's order and using the buyer's words.
  3. Under each heading, write the claim in one sentence. For example: "We will resolve complaints within 1 working day."
  4. Add the how: the process, the named role, the tool or the timescale.
  5. Add the proof: a past figure, an accreditation, an appendix or a reference.
  6. Close with the benefit to this buyer, in their terms (fewer complaints, less risk, compliance).
  7. Check the length in the portal's own counter. GCA warns that "anything over the character count will be excluded from the assessment" (GCA).
  8. Read it against the score descriptors and ask: "Which word earns a 5 here?"

Example (CAR, for a 150-word answer):

Challenge: Your specification notes repeated missed bin collections at Site 2. Action: We will add a photo check-in at each collection, logged in our app, with daily exception alerts to our supervisor. Result: At a comparable school contract in 2025, missed collections fell from 9 to 1 per term after we introduced the same check (reference in Appendix F).

The figures above are illustrative. Use your own.

Pitfalls: writing STAR as a story with no number in the Result; answering a "how will you" question with a past case study only; using the same case study for every question. Each answer is scored separately, so repetition takes up space without adding marks.

Common Mistakes That Lose Marks in UK Tender Responses

The mistakes that lose the most marks are generic marketing language, going over the word or page limit, and not answering every sub-question in the evaluator's order.

Example of a fix: "We have a robust quality management system" scores close to nothing. "Our ISO 9001-certified quality system (certificate in Appendix A) includes weekly site audits, with re-cleans within 24 hours for any score below 90%" gives the evaluator a process, a threshold and proof. Only cite certifications you actually hold. Some buyers require specific ones, such as Cyber Essentials for IT-related contracts. See Cyber Essentials Public Sector Tender: 5 Steps and Costs.

Next steps

Start with one action you can finish today, then decide whether you need outside help.

Do it yourself today:

  1. Open your most recent tender pack, or any open notice on Find a Tender, and copy one quality question with its sub-criteria and word limit into a document.
  2. Rewrite your existing answer using the heading-per-sub-criterion layout from Example 1. Make sure every paragraph has a "how" and a piece of proof.
  3. Make a folder called "Evidence bank" and add three case studies in STAR format, each with one number you can prove.

If you'd rather not write it yourself: hire a freelance bid writer on a fixed fee for one bid. The 2025 average day rate was £602 (Bid Solutions), and a smaller tender typically costs £1,000–£3,500 (as of October 2026; check the provider's own page) (BidMate). This suits SMEs bidding for contracts worth £250k or more who have good evidence but little time. It's poor value for small contracts, and it can't replace your own facts, pricing or social value commitments. A cheaper middle route is to write the bid yourself and pay a writer for a one-day review.

FAQ

What is the difference between a tender and a bid?

A tender is the buyer's formal invitation and process, and also the response you submit to it. A bid is the wider term for any offer to win work. In UK public procurement the two words are often used interchangeably. The practical difference is that public tenders are scored question by question against published criteria.

Do I need a tender writer or can I write it myself?

Most SMEs can write small tenders themselves if they follow the scoring guidance and back up every claim with evidence. A writer earns their fee on large or complex bids, or when you don't have time. Even then, you have to supply the facts, because the commitments become contract terms.

How long should a tender answer be?

As long as the buyer's limit allows, and no longer. Text over a character limit can be excluded from assessment. Drafting at about 90% of the limit leaves room for edits and still gives you space to cover every sub-criterion.

What is social value in UK tenders?

Social value means the wider economic, social and environmental benefits a contract delivers, such as jobs, skills or emissions cuts. For central government, PPN 002 requires a minimum 10% weighting and a choice from 8 model outcomes, and suppliers must give specific, measurable and time-bound commitments.
