Most public sector tenders in Wales are published on Sell2Wales, the free national portal for Welsh buyers. Devolved Welsh authorities must publish their notices there, and Sell2Wales forwards the required notices to the Central Digital Platform (Find a Tender). The table below shows where to start for your situation.

Key points

  • Welsh buyers publish via Sell2Wales, which forwards to Find a Tender
  • Sell2Wales registration is free and needs your CDP Unique Identifier
  • Welsh sub-central threshold is £207,720 inc VAT from 1 January 2026

Best for: UK SMEs, sole traders and charities bidding for Welsh public contracts

Where are Welsh tenders published, and which portal do you use?

Use Sell2Wales for Welsh councils, Welsh Government, NHS Wales and other devolved Welsh buyers. Above-threshold Welsh notices also appear on Find a Tender. Contracts Finder is mainly for England, and the gov.uk page for it sends Welsh opportunities to Sell2Wales.

The legal plumbing explains why. Welsh Government guidance calls Sell2Wales the Welsh Digital Platform (WDP) and says "the WDP is the system which must be used by DWAs to publish or give a notice". DWAs are devolved Welsh authorities. The same page says the WDP "will forward correctly submitted notices to the CDP on behalf of DWAs" (gov.wales). This applies to below-threshold contracts too: covered and notifiable below-threshold contracts must be published "via the WDP".

Who the buyer is decides the portal, not where the work is done. A UK government department with an office in Swansea is a non-devolved body. It publishes on Find a Tender like any other UK department. Swansea Council is a devolved Welsh authority, so the WDP rule above means its notices go through Sell2Wales.

Find a Tender is free to view and carries all regulated notices under the Act (GOV.UK). Sell2Wales registration is free (Sell2Wales).

For example, say you run cleaning services from Newport. Bristol City Council will not show up in your Sell2Wales alerts, because it is an English buyer. Monmouthshire County Council is a devolved Welsh authority, so under the WDP rule its notices come through Sell2Wales. If you work on both sides of the Severn, you need two feeds. Our comparison of Contracts Finder vs Find a Tender covers the English side.

The most common slip is ignoring the submission portal. A notice on Sell2Wales often sends you somewhere else to bid. Welsh Government runs procurements over £25,000 on eTenderWales, and Cardiff Council uses Proactis. So check the "how to submit" section of every notice before the deadline gets close.

What are the 2026 tender thresholds in Wales?

Thresholds tell you when a notice is above threshold, so it has to reach Find a Tender as well. WPPN 017 confirms the values below apply to devolved Welsh authorities from 1 January 2026. The Welsh amending regulations came into force at 12.05am that day.

How to find tenders on Sell2Wales in 5 steps

Register a free supplier account on Sell2Wales, search live notices by keyword, buyer and region, then open each notice to check the deadline, value, lots and submission route. Shortlist only the ones you can realistically deliver.

  1. Get your Unique Identifier from Find a Tender. Sell2Wales asks for the Unique Identifier (UI) issued by the Central Digital Platform during registration (Sell2Wales). Our Find a Tender registration guide walks through it. To actually bid, you also have to complete your core supplier information on the CDP (gov.wales).
  2. Click "free registration" on Sell2Wales. Enter your UI when prompted. Sell2Wales says the process should take no more than 20 minutes. Use a shared inbox such as tenders@yourbusiness, not a personal address, so alerts don't stop when a staff member leaves.
  3. Complete your Public Profile and Alerts Profile. Select "My account" in the top menu to open the Supplier Control Panel. Under "My Profile" you'll find both. Buyers can see your Public Profile, so write it plainly: what you supply, where, and any accreditations.
  4. Use "Search Contracts". It's in the menu and under the "Search contracts" heading on the homepage. Filter by region and business category. For a specific notice, open the advanced search and use Buyer's Name, Keywords or the reference number.
  5. Open the notice and run a quick bid/no-bid check. Look for the closing date, the estimated value, whether it's split into lots, any minimum turnover or insurance levels, and the submission portal. If documents sit behind "register interest", register early. Ambition North Wales, for example, requires bidders to register their interest on Sell2Wales before they can access tender documents.

If something doesn't work, the Sell2Wales Helpline is 0800 222 9004, open 8:30am to 5pm, Monday to Friday. Welsh-language calls are welcome (Sell2Wales suppliers page).

How to set up tender alerts with CPV codes and buyer names

Set up email alerts with CPV codes that match your goods or services, and track the buyers you target. Start with a few precise codes and widen them if too few notices come through.

On Sell2Wales the alerts live in the Alerts Profile, which has two parts (Sell2Wales):

  1. Product categories. Pick the categories (CPV codes) that describe your work. Only matching contracts will reach you. A CPV code has nine digits. The first two give the division, each further digit narrows the category, and the last digit is a check digit.
  2. Geographic locations. Choose the areas where you'll actually work. A Wrexham sole trader who won't travel south of Powys should not tick South East Wales.

Sell2Wales's guide doesn't describe a separate buyer-name alert. Two workarounds fill that gap. On Sell2Wales, run an advanced search by Buyer's Name once a week for your top five buyers. On Find a Tender, select 'Search', then set the 'notice type' filter (for example Tender Notice). Under 'Contract location' click 'Select regions', choose the Welsh regions and click 'Add regions to filter'. Click 'Update results', then select 'save search'. You can name the search and choose how often the emails arrive (GOV.UK). The same guide describes a 'Suitability' filter where you can tick 'Small and medium-sized enterprises'.

Example: a small IT support firm in Bangor might start with 72250000 (system and support services) and 72611000 (technical computer support services). It would limit locations to North Wales and save a Find a Tender search with the buyer name "Betsi Cadwaladr" as a keyword. If two weeks bring nothing, it could widen to the 72000000 division. Those codes are illustrations, so check them against the official list before using them. Our CPV codes guide shows how to pick codes from the official list without ending up with 300 irrelevant alerts a week.

One warning belongs here. Buyers sometimes give a notice a broad or slightly wrong CPV code. Add one or two keyword searches (for example "grounds maintenance") alongside your codes to catch them.

How to find NHS Wales tenders

NHS Wales health boards and trusts advertise on Sell2Wales, and much of their shared purchasing goes through NWSSP Procurement Services. Check which NHS your buyer belongs to before choosing a portal.

NWSSP (NHS Wales Shared Services Partnership) says its agreements are available to all NHS trusts and health boards across Wales. It publishes a work plan each financial year listing key projects with supplier opportunities and adds new projects during the year. It uses Sell2Wales for notices and a separate e-Tendering portal, where suppliers register, express interest and receive formal tender invitations (NWSSP: Working with us).

Steps for NHS Wales work:

  1. Read the current NWSSP work plan and the Our suppliers page to see which categories are coming up.
  2. Add NHS buyer names to your Sell2Wales Buyer's Name searches. Use "NHS Wales Shared Services Partnership" plus the health boards you can serve, such as Swansea Bay University Health Board or Hywel Dda University Health Board.
  3. Register on the NWSSP e-Tendering portal before a tender opens, so you aren't creating an account on deadline day.
  4. If you sell catalogue products, plan ahead. Getting set up to sell through NWSSP's catalogue is a separate step from winning the agreement, so ask NWSSP for the current lead time before you promise a start date to anyone.

How to find Welsh Government tenders and council tenders in North and South Wales

Filter Sell2Wales by buyer (Welsh Government or a named council) and by region. Then follow the regional collaborations that tender on behalf of several councils. When you search the wider web, make sure you mean Wales and not New South Wales in Australia.

Welsh Government. Welsh Government spends around £675 million a year on goods, services, works and digital. It advertises all contracts above £25,000 on Sell2Wales and runs procurements over £25,000 on eTenderWales (gov.wales). You need both accounts: Sell2Wales to see the notice, eTenderWales to submit.

North Wales. A Denbighshire supplier guide says tenders are advertised on Sell2Wales and Proactis (Denbighshire). The guide is older, so confirm current practice on the council's site. Ambition North Wales, the regional economic body, tenders all goods, services or works over £50,000 including VAT (Ambition North Wales FAQ). Joint frameworks covering several of the six North Wales councils (Anglesey, Gwynedd, Conwy, Denbighshire, Flintshire and Wrexham) come up regularly. Winning one place can open work with all of them.

South Wales. Cardiff Council publishes opportunities through Proactis or Sell2Wales and asks suppliers to join its corporate supplier directory via Proactis (Cardiff Council). Its regional arrangement, Ardal, "primarily seeks organisations to provide a quotation or to invite to tender via Proactis or Sell2Wales" and recommends registering on both (Ardal guide).

Steps:

  1. In Sell2Wales "Search Contracts", set the region filter (for example North Wales) and leave the category broad for a first look.
  2. In advanced search, enter one buyer name at a time ("Cyngor Gwynedd", "Cardiff Council") to see what each buys and how often.
  3. Open the procurement page of each target council and register on its e-tendering portal if it uses one.
  4. Look at Award notices as well as live tenders. They show who won, roughly at what value and when the contract might come back to market.

How to find Transport for Wales tenders

Transport for Wales publishes its open opportunities on Sell2Wales, so a Sell2Wales account and alerts are the starting point. TfW says: "We advertise all our open opportunities here, including those below the procurement threshold" (TfW). Its guidance follows the same registration order as the five Sell2Wales steps above, so there's no separate process to learn. Check each TfW notice for where bids are submitted.

For questions, TfW lists supplychain@tfw.wales and procurement@tfw.wales. TfW's guidance doesn't publish a subcontracting list. If its large rail or infrastructure contracts are out of reach, look at the Award notices on Sell2Wales to see which primes won. Then approach those primes about supply-chain work.

How to win government tenders as a small business or charity

To win government tenders, pick contracts that fit your size, read the selection and award criteria carefully, and write answers that prove quality, social value and price against them. If prime contracts are too big, start with lots, frameworks and subcontracting.

Welsh rules give smaller suppliers some specific openings. Under the Act and Welsh regulations, Welsh contracting authorities must have regard to the barriers SMEs face and consider how to remove them. A 30-day payment term is implied into public sub-contracts. Authorities must set KPIs for contracts over £5 million and publish performance against them (gov.wales short guide). Devolved Welsh authorities expecting to spend more than £100 million in a financial year must publish a pipeline notice on Sell2Wales (gov.wales). That's a free preview of what larger buyers plan to tender.

Wales also has its own social value law. The Social Partnership and Public Procurement (Wales) Act 2023 requires authorities to carry out procurement in a socially responsible way. Statutory guidance published in March 2026 says the duty applies to all public procurement under that Act, with no exceptions for smaller contracts (Welsh Government SRP guidance). In practice, expect questions on local jobs, fair work and environmental impact even on modest contracts. A charity or social enterprise employing local people has real evidence to offer there.

A practical sequence:

  1. Filter hard. Compare any minimum turnover, insurance levels and contract value stated in the notice with your own accounts and policies. Skip notices whose stated requirements you can't meet.
  2. Bid for lots. Larger Welsh tenders are often split by lot or by region. One lot for one county is a realistic first win.
  3. Get onto frameworks. Many council and NHS purchases are call-offs from frameworks, so you can't win them unless you are on the framework. Our framework agreement guide covers when to apply.
  4. Answer the published criteria, nothing else. Match each answer to the scoring guidance. Use names, numbers and dates from your own work.
  5. Ask for feedback. Section 50 of the Procurement Act 2023 requires the buyer to give each supplier that submitted an assessed tender an assessment summary before publishing a contract award notice (legislation.gov.uk). Use it for the next bid.

Business Wales runs free 1-2-1 advice sessions, Meet the Buyer events and six support videos on tendering portals, frameworks, Sell2Wales and bid writing (Business Wales Supply Chain Support).

Are there tenders for specific sectors like PPE or mental health services in Wales?

Yes. Search by sector CPV codes, such as medical consumables or health and social care services, and by buyer, such as NHS Wales bodies and councils. Check whether the buyer is Welsh or English to choose the right portal, and look for lots and frameworks suited to smaller providers.

Health and social care services usually fall under the light touch regime. The threshold for that regime is £663,540 including VAT (WPPN 017), far above the £207,720 for ordinary council services. A lot of community mental health work sits below that line, so it is advertised under lighter rules or by quotation.

Example: a mental health charity in Rhondda Cynon Taf would add the 853 social work codes and the community health code to its Alerts Profile and set its location to South Wales. It would then search by Buyer's Name for Cwm Taf Morgannwg University Health Board and Rhondda Cynon Taf County Borough Council. If it also delivers in Bristol, it needs a separate Find a Tender saved search, because those notices come from English buyers.

For PPE, most volume goes through NWSSP agreements, so check its work plan and expect a framework or catalogue route rather than one-off orders. Smaller suppliers often have better odds with councils buying PPE for their own services.

You can set up everything you need today, without any paid service.

Do these today:

  1. Work through steps 1 to 3 of the Sell2Wales section above, then save one Find a Tender search if you also bid outside Wales.
  2. Make a list of five target buyers, for example your local council, one health board, Welsh Government, TfW and a regional framework owner. Note which submission portal each one names in its recent notices and register on it.

If you'd rather not do it alone:

  • Business Wales Supply Chain Support offers free 1-2-1 sessions with specialist advisers, plus Meet the Buyer events. Call 03000 6 03000, 10am to 4pm on weekdays (Business Wales). It suits businesses in Wales at any stage. It suits English-based firms less, because its guidance is aimed at companies in Wales, and it won't write your bid for you.
  • A paid aggregator such as Tenders Direct collects notices "from every buyer from across the UK and Republic of Ireland" and gives subscribers an account manager. Its plans page doesn't list prices (as of November 2026; check the official page or call 0800 222 9009) (Tenders Direct). It suits firms bidding across several nations. It isn't worth it if you only bid in Wales, because Sell2Wales alerts are free and cover Welsh buyers directly.

FAQ

Is Sell2Wales free to use for suppliers?

Yes. Sell2Wales offers free registration with full use of its search and alert tools (Sell2Wales). Some Welsh buyers ask you to submit through a separate portal, such as Proactis for Cardiff Council, so check each portal's own registration terms.

Do I need to register to see tenders on Sell2Wales?

You can use "Search Contracts" to look at notices. To get email alerts, a public profile, and often the tender documents themselves, you need the free account. Some buyers, such as Ambition North Wales, only release documents after you register your interest on Sell2Wales.

What is a CPV code and how do I find mine?

A CPV code is a nine-digit code from the Common Procurement Vocabulary that classifies what is being bought. The first two digits give the division and the last digit is a check digit. Find yours by looking at the codes on past notices for work like yours, then choose the most specific one that fits in your Sell2Wales Alerts Profile.

Do small contracts below the thresholds get advertised?

Often, yes. Welsh Government advertises all contracts above £25,000 on Sell2Wales, Ambition North Wales tenders everything over £50,000 including VAT, and TfW advertises open opportunities below threshold too. Below each buyer's own limit, work may go out as a quotation to a few suppliers, so keeping your Sell2Wales Public Profile and council supplier directory entries up to date matters.

Can sole traders and charities bid for public sector contracts?

Yes. Any supplier can register on Find a Tender and Sell2Wales, and Welsh contracting authorities must consider the barriers SMEs face (gov.wales). Charities and social enterprises can also score well on the socially responsible procurement questions required in Wales.

Can a company based in England or Scotland bid for Welsh tenders?

Yes. Sell2Wales registration asks for your Central Digital Platform Unique Identifier, which suppliers get by registering on Find a Tender (GOV.UK). Nothing in the Sell2Wales registration steps limits accounts to Welsh businesses (Sell2Wales). Expect Welsh social value questions on local jobs and fair work, and plan how you'll answer them as a non-Welsh firm.

Which rules apply to a Welsh tender: the Procurement Act 2023 or the old regulations?

It depends on when the procurement started. The new rules applied in Wales to procurements started from 24 February 2025. A long-running framework or contract that began before that date may still follow the earlier rules, so check the dates and legal basis stated in the notice.

How do I find out who won a Welsh public contract?

Search Sell2Wales for Award notices from the buyer. Under section 50 of the Procurement Act 2023, buyers publish a contract award notice and give each supplier that submitted an assessed tender an assessment summary (legislation.gov.uk). For Welsh buyers, that notice goes through Sell2Wales and is forwarded to Find a Tender (gov.wales).
