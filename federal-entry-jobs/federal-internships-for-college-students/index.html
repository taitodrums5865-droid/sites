Federal Internships for College Students: Pathways Guide 2026

By First Grade · Published October 31, 2026 · 10 min read

  • Pathways Internship
  • Usajobs
  • Students
  • Government Jobs

The main federal internship for college students is the Pathways Internship Program. It pays you as a federal employee while you're enrolled at least half-time in a qualifying school or program (OPM: Students & Recent Graduates). Agencies post these jobs on USAJOBS, where you can find them with the Students filter. If you complete at least 480 hours and finish your degree, the agency may convert you to a term or permanent job without a new competition (USAJOBS: Students). There are other options too, including unpaid Semester of Service roles and agency-specific programs.

What a Pathways internship is

The Internship Program is one of the two Pathways Programs; the other is the Recent Graduates Program (5 CFR 362.101). The regulation describes its purpose as giving students the chance to explore federal careers "as paid employees while completing their education" (5 CFR 362.201).

Key facts:

  • Paid. OPM's overview describes the program as paid work in agencies (OPM). The announcement shows the starting salary, which the regulation requires every posting to include.
  • Seasonal or longer. USAJOBS says your internship "may be seasonal, temporary or longer-term" (USAJOBS: Students).
  • Linked to your studies. The duties should relate to your academic or career goals (5 CFR 362.203(d)).
  • Excepted service. Interns are appointed under Schedule D of the excepted service (5 CFR 362.203).
  • Agency-run. Each agency runs its own program and posts its own openings. You and the agency sign a Participant Agreement that sets out expectations.

Who can apply

You're typically eligible if you are either of the following (5 CFR 362.202):

  1. Accepted for enrollment or enrolled and seeking a degree, diploma, or certificate at a qualifying educational institution, at least half-time as your school defines it, or
  2. Enrolled or accepted into a qualifying career or technical education program that awards a recognized postsecondary credential, such as a Registered Apprenticeship Program.

A few details help many students:

  • You don't have to attend in person. Online students can qualify if they meet all the other requirements.
  • Final-term exception. If you need less than a half-time course load to finish right before graduating, you still count as a student.
  • High school through grad school. Qualifying institutions include accredited high schools, technical and vocational schools, 2- and 4-year colleges, and graduate and professional schools (5 CFR 362.102).
  • You have to stay a student. Interns must meet the student definition for the whole appointment. Agencies can approve breaks at their discretion (5 CFR 362.203(b), (h)).

Agencies can add eligibility rules of their own. OPM's FAQ gives examples like being able to work a set number of hours each week, staying in good academic standing (a minimum GPA), or confirming you can work at the listed location (OPM FAQ).

Citizenship. OPM says there's no blanket citizenship requirement for excepted service jobs. However, many agencies are restricted from paying non-citizens by appropriations or other laws. Non-citizens must be lawful permanent residents or otherwise authorized to work. To be converted to a permanent competitive service job at the end, an intern must be a U.S. citizen (OPM FAQ; 5 CFR 362.204(a)). Many postings are limited to citizens, so check the announcement.

Agencies can appoint interns in two ways (5 CFR 362.203(d)):

Hours. The regulation sets no limit on weekly hours, as long as overtime and hours-of-work laws are followed. You and the agency should agree on a formal schedule so work doesn't interfere with classes and both of you understand the conversion requirements (5 CFR 362.203(g)).

Training. Agencies must give interns training and development regardless of how long the internship lasts. For longer appointments, OPM recommends writing planned training into a plan or agreement within 45 days (5 CFR 362.203(i)).

Grade. You can be appointed at the highest grade you qualify for. OPM's Pathways Internship standard gives an education ladder that agencies may use (OPM group standards):

Agencies can also use their own standards or the regular competitive service standards, so the posting's Qualifications section is what applies.

Conversion to a permanent job

This is the main reason to pick a Pathways internship over a one-off summer job. An agency may noncompetitively convert an intern who is a U.S. citizen to a term or permanent competitive service job if the intern has (5 CFR 362.204):

  1. completed at least 480 hours of work through the Internship Program while enrolled (an agency can set a higher minimum),
  2. completed the degree, certificate, or career and technical education program,
  3. received a favorable recommendation from an agency official,
  4. met the qualification standard for the target position, and
  5. met any agency-specific requirements in the Participant Agreement.

Reducing the hours. An agency can credit up to half of the 480 hours for comparable non-federal internship work, certain military service, or a Registered Apprenticeship at a federal agency. It can also waive up to half for interns with exceptional performance who show high potential, such as a 3.5+ GPA, top 10 percent class standing, an honor society, or a strong letter of recommendation. Either way, the intern must still work at least 320 hours under the Pathways appointment, and credits plus waivers can't total more than 320 hours (5 CFR 362.204(c)–(e)).

Timing. An intern appointment expires 180 calendar days after you finish your course of study unless you're selected for conversion (5 CFR 362.205). If your agency can't convert you, USAJOBS says you have up to 180 days to convert at another agency (USAJOBS: Students).

Conversion is at the agency's discretion. Strong work matters, since a favorable recommendation is one of the conditions, but nothing in the rules promises an offer.

Where federal internships are posted

Agencies have to give public notice of Pathways internships. They can do it with a searchable USAJOBS announcement, or with a USAJOBS custom announcement linked from the agency's own careers page (5 CFR 362.203(a)). Custom postings can be reached only through their unique link and aren't searchable on USAJOBS (OPM FAQ). That's why it's worth checking the careers pages of agencies you care about.

Places to look:

  • intern.usajobs.gov, the Early Careers: Internships search, with grade filters from GS-1 to GS-15.
  • The USAJOBS Students hiring path page at usajobs.gov/hiringpath/students, which also lists agency programs. Examples include Department of Defense student opportunities, Department of State internships, NASA internships, and Department of Energy Office of Science STEM internships.
  • This site's daily list. First Grade publishes a daily list of [open listings](../jobs/) for student and entry-level announcements. Always apply through the official posting.

Other student options on the USAJOBS Students page (USAJOBS):

There's also a separate competitive service authority for students pursuing a bachelor's or graduate degree at least part-time. Under OPM's final rule, effective March 23, 2026, agencies can make time-limited appointments of eligible post-secondary students at GS-11 or below. Agencies may use USAJOBS for public notice of these jobs, or post them on their own home page (Federal Register, Feb. 19, 2026).

How to search USAJOBS for student jobs

  1. Create a USAJOBS profile. USAJOBS lists this as step one (USAJOBS: Students).
  2. Search a keyword or location, or leave both blank to see everything.
  3. Open the "Who can apply" filter and choose "Students." The same filter also has "Recent graduates" and "Open to the public" (USAJOBS Early Careers search).
  4. Set a grade range that matches your education (see the table above).
  5. Save the search and set up a job alert. USAJOBS recommends job alerts as part of preparing to apply.
  6. Open each announcement and read "This job is open to." USAJOBS says this section tells you whether you're eligible (USAJOBS).

About timing. There isn't one government-wide posting season. Each agency decides how long its announcement stays open. OPM's FAQ points out that a GS-4 summer administrative internship near a campus "may not need to be open as long" as a GS-11 technical internship at headquarters (OPM FAQ). Some postings may only be open for a short time, which is why an alert matters more than checking once a month.

Hiring context: Executive Order 14356 (October 15, 2025) requires each agency's Strategic Hiring Committee to approve the creation or filling of each vacancy, with listed exceptions (Federal Register). As of October 2026, an OPM final rule describes agencies as already operating under that requirement (Federal Register, Oct. 29, 2026). That can affect how many internships a given agency posts.

How to prepare before you find the right posting

Build a two-page resume. Resumes submitted through USAJOBS have been capped at two pages since September 27, 2025 (OPM news release). OPM's guidance asks for these items for each relevant job (OPM applicant guidance):

  • job title and employer (with series and grade for any federal job)
  • start and end dates as month/year
  • hours per week
  • duties that match the announcement's required qualifications

For education, include the school name, completion date (or expected date), degree type, and cumulative GPA.

Count your work honestly. OPM prorates part-time work, so 20 hours a week for a year counts as six months. Unpaid and volunteer work gets the same credit as comparable paid work (OPM qualification policies). Campus jobs, club leadership, and volunteer roles can all count if they fit the posting.

Get your enrollment proof ready. OPM says agencies may accept an official or unofficial transcript or a letter confirming enrollment or acceptance (OPM FAQ). Upload these to your USAJOBS profile ahead of time.

Apply early in the senior-year cycle if you're close to graduating. OPM allows applications from students who expect to finish qualifying education within 9 months, with verification before appointment (OPM qualification policies). If you'll graduate soon, also look at the Recent Graduates Program.

Have questions? USAJOBS lists earlycareers@opm.gov for questions about early career programs. For questions about a specific job, use the agency contact listed on that announcement (USAJOBS: Students).

FAQ

Are federal internships paid?

Pathways internships are paid. OPM describes the program as paid work in agencies, and every posting must state the starting salary (OPM; 5 CFR 362.203). Semester of Service is an unpaid volunteer option (USAJOBS).

Can community college students apply?

Yes. Two-year colleges are qualifying institutions if they're accredited by a body recognized by the U.S. Department of Education. Two academic years of post-high school study or an associate's degree typically lines up with GS-4 under OPM's internship standard (5 CFR 362.102; OPM).

Do I have to be a full-time student?

No. You need to be enrolled or accepted at least half-time, as your school defines it. Enrollment in a qualifying career or technical education program also counts (5 CFR 362.202).

Will my internship turn into a full-time job?

It might. After at least 480 hours, completing your degree, a favorable recommendation, and meeting the job's qualifications, the agency may convert you. It's not automatic (5 CFR 362.204).

Can I intern at more than one agency?

The regulation lets an agency count comparable experience toward the hours requirement, within limits, and OPM notes that time under earlier Internship Program appointments may count toward the required hours (5 CFR 362.204; OPM). Ask the hiring agency how it treats your earlier internships.
