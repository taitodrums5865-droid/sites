Pathways Recent Graduates Program Eligibility: 2-Year Rule

By First Grade · Published October 31, 2026 · 10 min read

  • Recent Graduates
  • Federal Jobs
  • Usajobs
  • Opm

You're typically eligible for the Pathways Recent Graduates Program if your application is received no later than two years after you completed all requirements for a qualifying degree or certificate, or a qualifying career or technical education program. Veterans whose military service kept them from applying get a full two-year window after release from active duty, but never more than six years from completion (5 CFR 362.302). Qualifying credentials include associate's, bachelor's, master's, professional, doctorate, vocational, and technical degrees and certificates. Each job still has its own qualifications, so check the announcement.

What the Recent Graduates Program is

The Recent Graduates Program is one of two Pathways Programs. The other is the Internship Program (5 CFR 362.101). The program gives people who recently finished school an entry-level, developmental job in a federal agency. OPM describes it as one- to two-year developmental experiences (OPM: Students & Recent Graduates).

A few basics:

  • It's a real, paid federal job. You're appointed to a position, not a training seat.
  • It's in the excepted service. Agencies appoint Recent Graduates under Schedule D of the excepted service (5 CFR 362.303).
  • It's run by each agency. OPM writes the rules, but each hiring agency runs its own program and posts its own jobs (OPM).
  • It can lead to a permanent job. If you complete the program, the agency may convert you without a new competition. That's up to the agency, not automatic.

The program normally lasts one year, unless the job's training needs call for a longer, more structured program. Under the regulation, employment under the program can't exceed two years plus an agency-approved extension of up to 120 days (5 CFR 362.301).

The 2-year eligibility window, step by step

The regulation ties the window to one date: when you completed all requirements of your course of study. Your application has to be received no later than two years after that date (5 CFR 362.302(b)(1)).

How to work out your own window:

  1. Find your completion date. This is usually the date your school says you finished all degree or certificate requirements. It may not match your graduation ceremony date. Your transcript or diploma is the best place to look.
  2. Add two years. That gives you the last day an application can be received.
  3. Compare it with the announcement's closing date. You need to apply while the posting is open and before your two years run out.
  4. Re-check for each degree. Each qualifying credential has its own completion date, so a newer degree can start a new window.

Example (illustration only): you completed all bachelor's requirements on May 15, 2025. Your application generally has to be received by May 15, 2027. An announcement that closes on June 1, 2027 is too late for that degree, even though it's still open.

For a quick date calculation, use the site's [Recent Graduates window checker](../tools/gs-grade-checker/#recent-grad). It gives an estimate from the date you enter. The hiring agency makes the actual decision based on your documents.

The 6-year rule for veterans

The regulation has one extension built in. A veteran (as defined in 5 U.S.C. 2108) who couldn't apply during any part of the normal two-year window because of a military service obligation gets a full two-year period after release or discharge from active duty (5 CFR 362.302(b)(2)).

There's a hard ceiling. That eligibility period can never extend beyond six years from the date you completed your degree, certificate, or program.

The 6-year rule for veterans
SituationTypical window
Not a veteran2 years from completing all requirements
Veteran, service didn't block the window2 years from completing all requirements
Veteran kept from applying by a military service obligation2 full years after release from active duty, capped at 6 years from completion

USAJOBS describes it the same way. Veterans who couldn't apply because of service obligations have up to six years after getting their degree or certificate to apply (USAJOBS: Recent Graduates). If this applies to you, keep your discharge paperwork ready, since the agency will need to verify the dates.

What counts as qualifying education

The program is broader than "a four-year college degree." USAJOBS notes that a four-year degree is not universally required (USAJOBS: Recent Graduates). Under the regulation, a Recent Graduate is someone who, within the window, obtained one of the following:

  • an associate's, bachelor's, master's, professional, doctorate, vocational, or technical degree, or a certificate, from a qualifying educational institution, or
  • a completed qualifying career or technical education program (5 CFR 362.302(a)).

Qualifying educational institutions include technical or vocational schools, 2-year and 4-year colleges and universities, graduate and professional schools, and post-secondary homeschool curricula. Each must be accredited by an accrediting body recognized by the U.S. Secretary of Education (5 CFR 362.102).

A certificate program counts when it is either:

  • post-secondary education at a qualifying institution equal to at least one academic year of full-time study, as part of an accredited post-secondary, technical, trade, or business school curriculum, or
  • a qualifying career or technical education program of at least one year that awards a recognized postsecondary credential.

Qualifying career or technical education programs come in three types (OPM FAQ; 5 CFR 362.102):

What counts as qualifying education
TypeKey condition
Federally administered educational programRigorous academic content and technical skills, a recognized postsecondary credential, and at least one year of substantially continuous participation
Registered Apprenticeship ProgramMeets the Department of Labor rules in 29 CFR part 29
Federally administered volunteer service programWritten proof of at least 52 weeks of service, or at least 1,700 work hours if service ran less than a year

A short online course or a weekend bootcamp usually won't meet the one-year certificate definition. Programs from schools without recognized accreditation don't qualify either.

Can you apply before you graduate?

Yes, in many cases. OPM's FAQ says students who haven't finished yet may apply and be considered for Recent Graduates jobs in the weeks or months before the job is filled. They must meet the Recent Graduate definition before appointment (OPM FAQ).

Agencies can also set their own cutoff for finishing your education. OPM gives examples such as the announcement closing date, the date the certificate is issued, or the appointment date. That cutoff has to be in the agency's Pathways policy and application procedures. Each announcement may handle this differently, so read its eligibility section closely.

If you're still a student, also look at the Pathways Internship Program, which is open to people enrolled at least half-time (OPM FAQ).

Citizenship and documents

Citizenship. OPM says there's no blanket rule that excepted service applicants be U.S. citizens. Many agencies, however, are barred from paying non-citizens by appropriations laws or other agency-specific statutes. A non-citizen can be appointed only if lawfully admitted for permanent residence or otherwise authorized to work. Noncompetitive conversion to the competitive service requires full U.S. citizenship by the end of the program (OPM FAQ; 5 CFR 362.305). Many announcements limit eligibility to citizens, so check the posting.

Documents. OPM says agencies may accept an official or unofficial transcript, a copy of the diploma, or other written documentation from your school or program. It has to show enough for the agency to confirm eligibility, including:

  • your name
  • the name of the degree or program
  • the date it was or will be completed
  • for certificates and volunteer service programs, the length of the program

USAJOBS tells applicants to submit transcripts or proof of enrollment with the application (USAJOBS: Recent Graduates). Upload these to your USAJOBS profile before you start, so you aren't hunting for files on closing day.

How the program works once you're hired

The regulation sets a minimum structure that each agency has to provide (5 CFR 362.301):

How the program works once you're hired
RequirementDeadline or amount
Individual Development Plan (IDP) approved by your supervisorWithin 45 days of appointment
A mentor from outside your chain of commandWithin 90 days of appointment
Formal interactive training tied to your IDPAt least 40 hours per year (mandatory annual trainings like ethics don't count)

Starting grade. Agencies can generally appoint Recent Graduates up to GS-11, with narrow exceptions. A GS-11 appointment is possible for STEM positions with a directly related PhD, and for research positions with a related master's. A GS-12 appointment is possible for research positions with a related PhD. Positions must have career-ladder advancement (5 CFR 362.303(b)(3)). Your actual grade still depends on OPM's qualification standards for the occupation.

The appointment is a trial period. The regulation states that the excepted service Recent Graduates appointment is a trial period (5 CFR 362.303(f)).

Conversion. An agency may noncompetitively convert a Recent Graduate who is a U.S. citizen to a term or permanent competitive service position after the person has done all of the following (5 CFR 362.305):

  1. completed at least one year of continuous service plus all program requirements,
  2. earned a rating of record of at least Fully Successful, with a conversion recommendation from the first-level supervisor, and
  3. met the OPM qualification standard for the target position.

Conversion is up to the agency. If your agency can't convert you, USAJOBS says recent graduates have up to 180 days after completing the program to convert to a term or permanent position at another agency (USAJOBS: Recent Graduates). OPM's overview also notes that completing a Pathways Program can lead to a permanent position under Schedule Policy/Career (OPM).

How to find Recent Graduates announcements

Agencies have to give public notice of these jobs. They do it either with a searchable USAJOBS announcement or with a USAJOBS "custom" announcement linked from the agency's own careers page (5 CFR 362.303(a)). Custom postings are stored in USAJOBS but don't show up in USAJOBS search, so it's worth checking the careers pages of agencies you're interested in (OPM FAQ).

Where to search:

  • recentgrad.usajobs.gov, the Early Careers: Recent Graduates search, linked from OPM's page.
  • USAJOBS search with the "Recent graduates" filter. On the search results page, open the Who can apply filter and choose Recent graduates (USAJOBS Early Careers search).
  • The Recent Graduates hiring path page at usajobs.gov/hiringpath/recentgraduates, which explains the path and links to current searches.

USAJOBS also suggests creating a profile, making your resume searchable, joining the Early Career Talent Network, and setting up a job alert (USAJOBS: Students). An alert makes it less likely you'll miss a short open period.

Before you apply, read the This job is open to section. If "Recent graduates" isn't listed, the Pathways eligibility doesn't apply to that job, though you might still be able to apply through another path, such as open to the public. Your resume has to fit OPM's two-page limit and should list your school, completion date, degree type, and cumulative GPA (OPM applicant guidance).

On the hiring climate: Executive Order 14356 (October 15, 2025) requires each agency's Strategic Hiring Committee to approve the creation or filling of each vacancy, with listed exceptions (Federal Register). As of October 2026, an OPM final rule describes agencies as already operating under that requirement (Federal Register, Oct. 29, 2026). The number of Recent Graduates postings can vary from agency to agency for that reason.

FAQ

Does the 2-year clock start at graduation or when I finished my classes?

The regulation uses the date you completed all requirements of the degree or certificate (5 CFR 362.302). That's often close to, but not always the same as, your ceremony date. Use the date on your transcript or diploma, and check the announcement for how the agency verifies it.

Does a community college associate's degree qualify?

Yes. Associate's degrees from qualifying, accredited institutions are listed in the regulation. You still need to meet the grade's qualifications, which an associate's typically puts at GS-4 under OPM's general education table (OPM qualification policies).

If I earn a master's later, do I get a new 2-year window?

The regulation counts from completing a qualifying degree, and master's degrees are on the list. So a newly completed master's typically starts its own window (5 CFR 362.302). Each announcement explains which documentation it wants.

Is the Recent Graduates Program a permanent job?

Not at first. It's an excepted service appointment that typically lasts one year and can't exceed two years plus up to 120 days. After that, the agency may convert you to a term or permanent competitive service job if you meet the conditions (5 CFR 362.301, 362.305).

Can I switch agencies during the program?

Yes. The regulation allows a Recent Graduate to accept a new Recent Graduates appointment at another participating agency, with no break in service. Time already served counts toward conversion (5 CFR 362.304).
