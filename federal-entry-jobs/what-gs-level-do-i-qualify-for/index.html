What GS Level Do I Qualify For? Grades by Degree and Experience

By First Grade · Updated November 25, 2026 · 15 min read

  • Usajobs
  • Students
  • Recent Graduates
  • Opm

Under OPM's general policies, a bachelor's degree typically meets GS-5, and a bachelor's with Superior Academic Achievement or one year of graduate school typically meets GS-7 (OPM General Schedule Qualification Policies). A master's typically meets GS-9, and a PhD typically meets GS-11. The job announcement has the final word, because some occupations add course requirements.

Key points

  • A bachelor's degree typically lines up with GS-5 and a master's with GS-9
  • Specialized experience must equal the next lower grade
  • The announcement's Qualifications section has the final word

Best for: Students and recent grads applying on USAJOBS

Which option is right for you?

Start with your highest completed education (or education you'll finish within 9 months), then check the case that fits you below.

  • High school student or recent high school grad: Look at GS-2 to GS-4 jobs and Pathways internships. USAJOBS says a high school diploma or three months of general work experience generally qualifies you for entry-level jobs between GS-1 and GS-4 (USAJOBS: Students).
  • Trade or technical school student: 36 weeks of study at 20 or more classroom hours a week counts as one year above high school, so check GS-3 postings (OPM).
  • College student without a degree yet: Count your semester hours. 30 hours is one year (GS-3), and 60 hours is two years (GS-4). If you'll graduate within 9 months, you can usually apply for GS-5 now.
  • Bachelor's grad with a strong GPA or honors: Apply at GS-7 through Superior Academic Achievement.
  • Bachelor's grad without SAA: Target GS-5. GS-7 is possible if you have one year of work at the GS-5 level that matches the job's specialized experience.
  • Master's grad: Target GS-9.
  • Your target job needs a specific major (accounting, engineering, science, and similar): Check the Individual Occupational Requirements in the posting before you think about the grade.
  • Not a good fit for an education-only estimate: People aiming for GS-12 and above. Education alone generally doesn't qualify anyone at GS-12 and up (except some research jobs), and GS-13 and above always requires specialized experience.

How the GS grade system decides your starting level

Your starting grade is the highest grade where your education, your experience, or the two combined meet OPM's minimum qualifications for the job's occupation. The General Schedule (GS) runs from GS-1 to GS-15. A federal HR specialist compares your resume and documents against the qualification standard for that occupation and grade.

There are three ways to meet a grade:

  1. Education alone. Your degree or years of college match what the grade requires.
  2. Experience alone. Your past work matches the type and level of experience required.
  3. A combination. Some education plus some experience, added together as percentages.

OPM's policy says "education alone is not qualifying for work at the GS-12 grade level and above," except for research positions at GS-12. At GS-13 and above, "appropriate specialized experience is required for all positions" (OPM General Schedule Qualification Policies). That is why the education table below stops at GS-11, or GS-12 for research positions.

OPM's wording matters here. The education has to be in a course of study that "directly reflects" the knowledge and skills the job needs. For many administrative jobs, any field of study works. For others, your major has to fit. See the section on occupational requirements below.

How OPM counts an "academic year":

  • Undergraduate: 30 semester hours or 45 quarter hours is one year. A bachelor's is 120 semester hours or 180 quarter hours.
  • Business or technical school: 36 weeks of study at 20 or more classroom hours per week is one year above high school.
  • Graduate school: one year is whatever your school treats as a full-time year. If the school can't say, OPM uses 18 semester hours or 27 quarter hours. Part-time study is credited proportionally.

All of these come from the same OPM policy page. For example, a sophomore who has finished 60 semester hours has two academic years, which typically lines up with GS-4.

Superior Academic Achievement (the GS-7 route for bachelor's grads)

You meet Superior Academic Achievement if you have finished, or will finish within 9 months, a bachelor's degree from an accredited school and you meet one of these:

  1. Class standing: you are in the upper third of your graduating class, in the college, the university, or a major subdivision.
  2. GPA: you have a 3.0 or higher (out of 4.0) overall, or a 3.5 or higher in your major. The GPA can be computed over all four years or over your final two years. OPM rounds GPAs to one decimal place.
  3. Honor society: you are a member of a national scholastic honor society recognized by the Association of College Honor Societies. A freshman honor society doesn't count.

Source: OPM General Schedule Qualification Policies.

Entry grades for Administrative and Management jobs

Many first federal jobs use OPM's Administrative and Management Positions group standard, which starts at GS-5 for a bachelor's degree. It covers two-grade interval series such as Human Resources Management (GS-0201), Miscellaneous Administration and Program (GS-0301), Management and Program Analysis (GS-0343), Budget Analysis (GS-0560), Public Affairs (GS-1035), Writing and Editing (GS-1082), Grants Management (GS-1109), and General Supply (GS-2001) (OPM group standards).

Two details from the standard matter most for students:

  • For many positions it covers, a bachelor's in any field meets GS-5.
  • The Superior Academic Achievement provision applies to the occupations in this standard.

Clerical and support jobs at GS-1 through GS-4 use a different group standard, covered in the next section.

Pathways internships follow their own education ladder, from GS-1 (enrolled in a high school or GED program) up to GS-11 (OPM group standards). Agencies don't have to use that standard. They can apply it, their own requirements, or the regular competitive service requirements, so read each internship posting.

How specialized experience can raise your grade

Specialized experience can lift you above your education grade, but only if it equals at least the next lower grade in the occupation's normal line of progression (OPM Administrative and Management standard). Specialized experience is work that gave you the particular knowledge and skills for the job. It is usually in or related to the job's line of work.

In practice:

  • To qualify for an administrative GS-7 on experience, you need one year of specialized experience at least equal to GS-5.
  • To qualify for GS-9, you need one year at least equal to GS-7.

"Equal to GS-5" doesn't mean you had to work for the government. It means your work was about as hard and responsible as GS-5 work in that field. The announcement usually describes what that looks like with a short list of tasks.

General experience is different. Under the administrative standard, GS-5 needs three years of progressively responsible work that shows you can analyze problems, plan and organize work, and communicate well. The standard also says general clerical work, such as typing, filing, routine processing, and maintaining records, is not creditable for these positions (OPM). A part-time campus job may help at the clerical grades and still not count toward an administrative GS-5.

Combining education and experience

If neither your education nor your experience is enough on its own, you can sometimes add the two as percentages and qualify at 100 percent or more. OPM's method:

  1. Turn your qualifying experience into a percentage of the experience the grade requires.
  2. Turn your qualifying education into a percentage of the education the grade requires.
  3. Add the two. You need at least 100 percent.

OPM's administrative standard gives an example for a Quality Assurance Specialist at GS-5. An applicant with two years of general experience (67 percent) and 45 semester hours of college (38 percent) reaches more than 100 percent and qualifies (OPM).

There's a catch at GS-9 and GS-11. You can only combine graduate education beyond what the next lower grade requires. In OPM's example, someone with six months of GS-7 experience and one year of graduate school doesn't qualify for a GS-9 Management Analyst job. The one year of graduate school is already "used up" by GS-7, so the total is only 50 percent.

Individual Occupational Requirements: the big caveat

If your target series has Individual Occupational Requirements (IORs), you have to meet its course or major rules before any grade applies. OPM marks those series with an asterisk in its coverage lists. In the administrative list, they include Financial Management (GS-0505), Tax Specialist (GS-0526), and General Business and Industry (GS-1101) (OPM).

According to the administrative standard, IORs generally require one of two things:

  • specific course work that makes up a major in a particular field, or
  • at least 24 semester hours of course work in the named field or fields.

Closely related courses can count if they clearly give you the needed background.

The Professional and Scientific Positions standard goes further. Every series it covers has an IOR, and you must meet that "basic requirement" first. For those jobs, the basic requirement generally qualifies you for GS-5. GS-7 and above also need graduate education, Superior Academic Achievement, or specialized experience (OPM group standards). If OPM's standard doesn't set a number of hours for the major, your school's definition applies, with 24 semester hours as the fallback.

Agencies can also add selective factors. These are skills the job can't be done without and that you can't reasonably learn during normal orientation. If an announcement lists one, you must meet it as well as the grade minimums.

How to read the Qualifications section of an announcement

Read eligibility first, then the qualifications for each grade, then the required documents. Every USAJOBS announcement states its minimum qualifications, and Pathways regulations require postings to include them (5 CFR part 362). Work through a posting in this order:

  1. Check "This job is open to" first. USAJOBS tells applicants to read this section to see whether they're eligible to apply (USAJOBS: Students). Meeting the grade won't help if the job isn't open to your hiring path.
  2. Find the series and grade. The title line usually shows them, for example GS-0343-07. If a posting lists more than one grade, read the qualification paragraph for each grade listed.
  3. Read each grade's paragraph separately. Postings usually give an education route and an experience route for each grade, often joined by "or."
  4. Underline the specialized experience tasks. For the experience route, your resume has to show those duties.
  5. Look for course or credit-hour rules, such as "24 semester hours in…". These point to an IOR.
  6. Check the Education and Required documents sections. If you qualify on education, you'll usually need transcripts. If you claim Superior Academic Achievement, have proof of your GPA, class standing, or honor society membership ready.
  7. Note the timing rules. OPM allows applications from students who expect to finish qualifying education within 9 months of applying, and the agency must verify completion before appointing you (OPM policies). A posting may set its own cutoff.

Your resume must back up everything you claim. Under OPM's Merit Hiring Plan, resumes are limited to two pages. Include month/year dates, hours per week for each job, and series and grade for any federal job (OPM applicant guidance on the two-page limit). Also check the announcement for any education details it asks for.

Education and experience details that can change your answer

A few rules in OPM's qualification policies decide whether your credits and work count at all. All of the points below come from the OPM General Schedule Qualification Policies.

  • Your school must be accredited. Education used to qualify generally has to come from a school accredited by an accrediting body recognized by the U.S. Department of Education. Credits from an unaccredited program may not count toward the grade table above, even if you completed a degree. If you transferred, the credits your accredited school accepted are the ones to count.
  • Pass/fail credits can block the GPA route to GS-7. For Superior Academic Achievement, OPM says that if more than 10 percent of your total undergraduate credit hours are non-graded (such as pass/fail, CLEP, or military credit), you can't qualify on GPA. You can still qualify through upper-third class standing or a recognized honor society, so check whether your school can document class rank.
  • Volunteer work counts. OPM credits unpaid experience and volunteer work, such as community, cultural, social service, and professional association activities, on the same basis as paid experience. A volunteer role where you planned events, managed a budget, or wrote materials can support general or specialized experience if it matches the posting. List it on your resume with dates and hours per week, as you would a paid job.

Two policy changes to know about (they don't change your starting grade)

Neither of these changes the education rules above. They affect how fast you can move up and how many jobs get posted.

  • Time-in-grade is ending. OPM published a final rule removing the time-in-grade restriction on advancement in competitive service GS positions (Federal Register, Oct. 19, 2026). The two official sources give different effective dates: the Federal Register document says January 19, 2027, while OPM's news release says January 14, 2027 (OPM news release). We have not confirmed which date is correct or whether a correction has been issued, so check both pages before relying on either date. Qualification standards still apply after the change, and agencies can still set other job-related requirements.
  • Hiring committees approve vacancies. Executive Order 14356 (signed October 15, 2025) requires each agency's Strategic Hiring Committee to approve creating or filling each vacancy, with listed exceptions (Federal Register). In practice, an open announcement on USAJOBS is the clearest sign a job is actually being filled.

FAQ

Can I apply for a GS-7 job with just a bachelor's degree?

Often, yes, if you meet Superior Academic Achievement or have one year of specialized experience at the GS-5 level. SAA means a 3.0+ overall GPA, a 3.5+ GPA in your major, upper-third class standing, or membership in a recognized national honor society. Without either route, a bachelor's typically lines up with GS-5 (OPM).

Does my major matter for my GS level?

It depends on the series. Many administrative jobs accept a bachelor's in any field for GS-5. Series with Individual Occupational Requirements may need a specific major or a set number of credit hours, often 24 semester hours in the field (OPM).

Do part-time jobs count as experience?

They can, if the work is the right type and you list hours per week so HR can credit it (OPM). OPM credits part-time experience according to its relationship to a full-time workweek, so a year at 20 hours a week generally counts as about six months of full-time experience (OPM policies). Routine clerical work isn't creditable as general experience for administrative GS-5 jobs, though.

Can I apply before I graduate?

Usually, yes. OPM allows applications from students who expect to finish qualifying education within 9 months, and the agency must verify completion before appointing you (OPM). Check the announcement for its own cutoff.

What's the highest grade a degree alone can get me?

Typically GS-11, with a PhD or three years of progressively higher graduate study. Research positions can go to GS-12 with a doctorate. Above that, specialized experience is required (OPM).

Does a degree from a school outside the U.S. count?

It can. OPM's policies allow education completed in foreign colleges or universities to be used if you can show it is comparable to education from an accredited U.S. school. In practice, that usually means getting your transcript evaluated by an organization that specializes in foreign credential evaluation and submitting the evaluation with your application (OPM). Check the announcement's Education section for what it accepts.

How long after graduating can I use the Pathways Recent Graduates program?

Under the Pathways regulations, a recent graduate is generally someone who completed a qualifying degree or certificate program within the previous two years. Veterans who couldn't apply during that window because of military service can have up to six years after completing the program (5 CFR part 362). The grade you qualify for still comes from the qualification rules above, not from the program itself.

Next steps

If you're doing this yourself, today's step: open one USAJOBS announcement at the grade the table above suggests for you. Check "This job is open to," then the Qualifications paragraph for your grade, then Required documents. If you'll qualify on education, request an unofficial transcript now so it's ready when you apply. If you want a quick education-based estimate first, our [GS grade checker](../tools/gs-grade-checker/) applies the education table in this article. It is not built for series with Individual Occupational Requirements, for applicants qualifying on experience or a combination of education and experience, or for GS-12 and above. In those cases, go straight to the announcement's Qualifications section.

If doing it alone is hard:

  • Your school's career center (usually free for current students, and sometimes for recent grads): good for resume reviews and for practice reading announcements. Staff may not specialize in federal hiring, so check the details against the posting.
  • The HR contact listed in each announcement (free): best for questions about that specific job, such as which documents count.
  • Paid federal resume writers (prices vary by provider, and we haven't checked current rates): can help with the two-page format and with describing specialized experience. They can't change your eligibility or guarantee a grade or an offer. They're usually unnecessary if you're qualifying on education alone. Check the price and refund terms before you pay.
