What GS Level Do I Qualify For? Grades by Degree and Experience

By First Grade · Published October 31, 2026 · 12 min read

  • Usajobs
  • Students
  • Recent Graduates
  • Opm

Most students and recent grads can estimate their starting GS level from education alone. Under OPM's standards, high school graduation typically lines up with GS-2, a bachelor's degree with GS-5, a bachelor's with Superior Academic Achievement or one year of graduate school with GS-7, a master's with GS-9, and a PhD with GS-11 (OPM General Schedule Qualification Policies). Work experience can qualify you instead, but only if it's the right kind and at the right level. The job announcement decides in the end, because many occupations add their own course requirements.

How the GS grade system decides your starting level

The General Schedule (GS) runs from GS-1 to GS-15. Each grade has minimum qualifications, and those minimums come from qualification standards that OPM publishes. A federal HR specialist compares your resume and documents against the standard for the job's occupation and grade.

There are three ways to meet a grade:

  1. Education alone. Your degree or years of college match what the grade requires.
  2. Experience alone. Your past work matches the type and level of experience required.
  3. A combination. Some education plus some experience, added together as percentages.

OPM's general policies say education alone stops being enough at a certain point. Specialized experience is required at GS-13 and above for all positions, and education alone generally doesn't qualify anyone for GS-12 or higher, except for certain research positions at GS-12 (OPM General Schedule Qualification Policies). For a first federal job, that rarely matters. Students and new grads usually aim for GS-3 through GS-9, where a degree can carry you.

Qualifying for a grade is not the same as being hired at it. Qualifying puts you in the eligible pool for that grade. The agency still rates candidates and picks from among them.

OPM's wording matters here. For GS-4 and above, the education has to be in a course of study that "directly reflects" the knowledge and skills the job needs. For many administrative jobs, any field of study works. For others, the major has to fit. More on that in the section on occupational requirements below.

How OPM counts an "academic year":

  • Undergraduate: 30 semester hours or 45 quarter hours counts as one year. A bachelor's is 120 semester hours or 180 quarter hours.
  • Business or technical school: 36 weeks of study at 20 or more classroom hours per week counts as one year above high school.
  • Graduate school: one year is whatever your school treats as a full-time year. If that can't be found, OPM uses 18 semester hours or 27 quarter hours.

All of these come from the same OPM policy page. So a sophomore who has finished 60 semester hours has two academic years, which typically lines up with GS-4.

Entry grades for Administrative and Management jobs

Many first federal jobs fall under OPM's group standard for Administrative and Management Positions. It covers two-grade interval work in series such as Human Resources Management (GS-0201), Miscellaneous Administration and Program (GS-0301), Management and Program Analysis (GS-0343), Budget Analysis (GS-0560), Public Affairs (GS-1035), Writing and Editing (GS-1082), Grants Management (GS-1109), and General Supply (GS-2001) (OPM group standards).

Two details from the standard are useful for students:

  • For many positions it covers, a bachelor's in any field meets GS-5.
  • The Superior Academic Achievement provision applies to all occupations in this standard.

This standard starts at GS-5. Clerical and support jobs at GS-1 through GS-4 use a different group standard (next section).

What about GS-2, GS-3, and GS-4 jobs?

Lower grades usually fall under OPM's Clerical and Administrative Support Positions standard, which covers one-grade interval work. Its basic pattern for most positions:

USAJOBS sums it up the same way. If you have a high school diploma or three months of general work experience, you can generally qualify for entry-level jobs between GS-1 and GS-4 (USAJOBS: Students).

Pathways internships follow their own education ladder, which runs from GS-1 (enrolled in a high school or GED program) up to GS-11 (OPM Pathways Internship standard). That standard is optional. Agencies can use it, use their own agency-specific requirements, or use the regular competitive service requirements, so read each internship posting.

How specialized experience can raise your grade

Specialized experience is work that gave you the particular knowledge and skills for the job, and it is usually in or related to the job's line of work. To count, OPM says it must equal at least the next lower grade level in the occupation's normal line of progression (OPM Administrative and Management standard).

In practice:

  • To qualify for an administrative GS-7 on experience, you need one year of specialized experience at least equal to GS-5.
  • To qualify for GS-9, you need one year at least equal to GS-7.

"Equal to GS-5" doesn't mean you had to work for the government. It means the work you did was about as hard and responsible as GS-5 work in that field. The announcement usually describes what that looks like with a short list of tasks.

General experience is different. For GS-5 under the administrative standard, it means three years of progressively responsible work that shows you can analyze problems, plan and organize work, and communicate well. The standard also says that general clerical work, such as typing, filing, routine processing, and maintaining records, is not creditable for these positions (OPM). A part-time campus job may help at the clerical grades and still not count toward an administrative GS-5.

Combining education and experience

If neither your education nor your experience is enough on its own, the two can sometimes be added together. OPM's method works like this:

  1. Turn your qualifying experience into a percentage of the experience the grade requires.
  2. Turn your qualifying education into a percentage of the education the grade requires.
  3. Add the two. You need at least 100 percent.

OPM's administrative standard gives an example for a Quality Assurance Specialist at GS-5. An applicant with two years of general experience (67 percent) and 45 semester hours of college (38 percent) clears 100 percent and qualifies (OPM).

There's a catch at GS-9 and GS-11. Only graduate education beyond what the next lower grade needs can be combined. OPM's own example: someone with six months of GS-7 experience and one year of graduate school doesn't qualify for a GS-9 Management Analyst job. The single year of graduate school is already "used up" by GS-7, so the total comes to only 50 percent.

Individual Occupational Requirements: the big caveat

Some occupations add their own entry rules on top of the group standard. These are called Individual Occupational Requirements (IORs). OPM marks series that have them with an asterisk in the coverage list. In the administrative list, that includes series such as Financial Management (GS-0505), Tax Specialist (GS-0526), and General Business and Industry (GS-1101) (OPM).

According to the administrative standard, IORs generally require one of two things:

  • specific course work that makes up a major in a particular field, or
  • at least 24 semester hours of course work in the named field(s).

Closely related courses can count if they clearly give you the needed background.

The Professional and Scientific Positions standard goes further. Every series it covers has an IOR, and you must meet that "basic requirement" before any grade applies. For those jobs, meeting the basic requirement generally qualifies you for GS-5. GS-7 and above also need graduate education, Superior Academic Achievement, or specialized experience (OPM Professional and Scientific standard). If OPM's standard doesn't list a set number of hours for the major, the school's own definition applies, with 24 semester hours as the fallback.

Agencies can also add selective factors. These are specific skills the job can't do without, which you can't reasonably learn during normal orientation. When an announcement lists one, you have to meet it as well as the grade minimums.

How to read the Qualifications section of an announcement

Every USAJOBS announcement states its minimum qualifications. For Pathways jobs, the regulations require that the posting include them (5 CFR part 362). Here's how to work through one:

  1. Check "This job is open to" first. USAJOBS says to read this section to see whether you're eligible to apply at all (USAJOBS: Students). Being qualified for the grade won't help if the job is closed to your hiring path.
  2. Find the series and grade. The title line usually shows them, for example GS-0343-07. Multi-grade postings (like "GS-5/7/9") let HR place you at the highest grade you meet.
  3. Read each grade's paragraph separately. Postings typically spell out an education route and an experience route for each grade, often joined by "or."
  4. Underline the specialized experience tasks. Those are the duties your resume has to show for the experience route.
  5. Look for course or credit-hour rules. These signal an IOR, such as "24 semester hours in…"
  6. Check the Education and Required documents sections. If you're qualifying on education, you'll usually need transcripts.
  7. Note the timing rules. OPM allows applications from students who expect to finish qualifying education within 9 months of applying. The agency must verify completion before you're appointed (OPM policies). The posting may set its own cutoff.

Your resume must back up whatever you claim. OPM's two-page resume guidance asks for month/year dates, hours per week, and series and grade for any federal job. It also asks you to include school, completion date, degree type, and cumulative GPA (OPM applicant guidance on the two-page limit).

To get a quick estimate from your own numbers, try the site's free [GS grade checker](../tools/gs-grade-checker/). It applies the education rules above and points you to the parts of an announcement to confirm. It's an estimate only. HR's review of your documents is what counts.

One more change worth knowing if you plan to move up quickly. OPM issued a final rule eliminating the time-in-grade restriction for advancement in competitive service GS positions. That restriction generally required 52 weeks at the lower grade before advancing to a covered job at GS-6 or above. The rule is effective January 19, 2027 (Federal Register, Oct. 19, 2026). Qualification standards still apply after that date.

Hiring context also matters. Executive Order 14356 (October 15, 2025) requires each agency's Strategic Hiring Committee to approve the creation or filling of each vacancy, with listed exceptions (Federal Register). As of October 2026, an OPM final rule describes agencies as already operating under that committee requirement (Federal Register, Oct. 29, 2026). In practice, a posted announcement is the clearest sign a job is actually being filled.

FAQ

Can I apply for a GS-7 job with just a bachelor's degree?

Often, yes, if you meet Superior Academic Achievement or have one year of specialized experience at the GS-5 level. Without either, a bachelor's typically lines up with GS-5. Many announcements list several grades, such as GS-5/7, so you can be considered at the level you meet (OPM).

Does my major matter for my GS level?

It depends on the series. Many administrative jobs accept a bachelor's in any field for GS-5. Series with Individual Occupational Requirements may need a specific major or a set number of credit hours, often 24 semester hours in the field (OPM).

Do part-time jobs count as experience?

They can, if the work is the right type and you list hours per week so HR can credit it. OPM's resume guidance asks for hours per week for each job (OPM). Routine clerical work isn't creditable as general experience for administrative GS-5 jobs, though.

Can I apply before I graduate?

Usually, yes. OPM allows applications from students expecting to finish qualifying education within 9 months. The agency must verify completion before appointment (OPM). Check the announcement for its own cutoff.

What's the highest grade a degree alone can get me?

Typically GS-11, with a PhD or three years of progressively higher graduate study. Research positions can go to GS-12 with a doctorate. Beyond that, specialized experience is required (OPM).
