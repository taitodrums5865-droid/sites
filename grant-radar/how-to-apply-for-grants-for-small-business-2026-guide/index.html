How to Apply for Grants for Small Business (2026 Guide)

By Outlay · Published October 16, 2026 · 9 min read

  • Small Business Grants
  • Federal Grants
  • Sam.gov
  • Uei

To apply for a federal grant as a small business, do the steps in this order: register your entity on SAM.gov, which gives you a Unique Entity ID (UEI); create a Grants.gov account and add an organization profile using that UEI; then search for open opportunities with the Eligibility filter set to your applicant type and submit through a Workspace. Each step depends on the one before it. SAM.gov can take 7–10 business days to process, so start now, even before you've picked a grant. Also check your expectations: the SBA says it "does not provide grants for starting and expanding a business". Most federal grants go to specific purposes, such as research (SBIR/STTR), exporting or nonprofit programs.

Key points

  • Register on SAM.gov first to get UEI, then Grants.gov account
  • SAM.gov processing takes 7–10 business days before you can apply
  • SBIR/STTR reauthorized through 2031, Phase I awards up to $323,090

One-line reasons:

  • Beginner business: you need full registration, not just a UEI, before you can apply for any federal award.
  • Startup doing research: SBIR/STTR is the main federal grant path written for for-profit small businesses.
  • On a budget: registration costs nothing, and anyone asking you to pay for it is not the government.

What federal grants actually exist for small businesses?

Federal grants for small businesses are narrow and tied to a specific purpose. They are not general startup money.

The SBA lists these as its main grant programs (SBA):

  • SBIR and STTR: research and development funding for work that could become a product.
  • STEP (State Trade Expansion Program): money goes to state and territory governments, which then help established small businesses export.
  • Empower to Grow (E2G) Manufacturing grant: applications were closed when the SBA page was checked.
  • Grants to organizations that support veteran-owned businesses, Small Business Development Centers (SBDCs) and microenterprise lenders (PRIME).

SBIR/STTR deserves a closer look. Its legal authority lapsed on October 1, 2025, and the program was reauthorized on April 13, 2026 through September 30, 2031. SBIR.gov says 11 federal agencies take part. As of April 2026, Phase I awards go up to $323,090 and Phase II awards up to $2,153,927 (SBIR.gov). The reauthorization also adds proposal submission limits, which agencies will phase in during later funding cycles (IEDC). Check each solicitation for its current cap.

Who SBIR is not for: a business with no research or development work, such as a typical retail shop or service firm. Those businesses will usually do better with state or local programs, or with loans.

Step 1: Register your entity on SAM.gov and get your UEI

To apply for a federal award, you need a full entity registration on SAM.gov. A UEI on its own is not enough.

SAM.gov offers two paths. "Get a Unique Entity ID" only asks for your legal business name and physical address. However, it does not let you apply for federal awards directly. It is meant for sub-awardees and similar cases (SAM.gov). First-time applicants often pick this shortcut by mistake.

Gather these before you start (from the SAM.gov Entity Registration Checklist):

  • EIN/TIN, with your legal name exactly as the IRS has it
  • Bank account details for receiving payments
  • NAICS code (the industry classification code for your business)
  • Points of contact (POCs)

Steps:

  1. Go to SAM.gov and click Sign In in the upper right. Accept the terms. If you don't have an account, create one through Login.gov (DOL quick start guide).
  2. In your Workspace, find the Entities widget and click Get Started.
  3. Choose Register Entity, not "Get a Unique Entity ID" (SAM.gov).
  4. Enter the information you gathered. Name an Electronic Business Point of Contact (EBiz POC): this person will control Grants.gov access later (Grants.gov).
  5. Submit, then wait. Allow up to 10 business days for the registration to become active. If something can't be verified, it "could take weeks or months" (Grants.gov).

Key facts:

  • Registration is free.
  • You must renew every 365 days (SAM.gov).
  • To confirm your registration went through, use our guide: How to Check SAM.gov Registration Status (Step by Step).

Step 2: Create your Grants.gov account and organization profile

Once SAM.gov shows your registration as active, set up Grants.gov and link it to your UEI.

Grants.gov says it can take 24–48 hours after SAM activation for your data to appear there. If Grants.gov doesn't recognize your UEI, wait a day before you contact support.

  1. On Grants.gov, click Register in the top banner and follow the on-screen instructions. This step is usually done the same day (Grants.gov).
  2. The EBiz POC should create the account with the same email address used in SAM.gov (Grants.gov).
  3. Add an organization applicant profile and enter your UEI. Each profile represents one applicant organization (Grants.gov FAQs).
  4. The EBiz POC assigns roles to the people who will work on applications. The main roles are:
  5. Authorized Organization Representative (AOR): the person who can submit applications for the organization.
  6. Workspace Manager: the person who can manage application Workspaces.

To check who has which role, open My Account and go to the Manage Profiles tab (Grants.gov).

Individuals can register with an individual profile. However, with that profile you can only apply to opportunities open to individuals, and "most of the funding opportunities on Grants.gov are for organizations" (Grants.gov).

Step 3: Find grants you're actually eligible for

To avoid wasted applications, filter by eligibility first. Leave keywords for later.

Grants.gov Search Grants has these filters (Grants.gov):

  • Opportunity Status: Forecasted, Posted, Closed, Archived
  • Funding Instrument Type
  • Eligibility
  • Category
  • Agency

Grants.gov eligibility types include government, education, public housing, nonprofit (501(c)(3) and non-501(c)(3)), for-profit organizations other than small businesses, small businesses, and individuals. A "small business" is defined by SBA size standards for your industry (Grants.gov).

A practical filter sequence:

  1. Set Opportunity Status to Posted. Add Forecasted if you want to see grants coming later.
  2. Under Eligibility, check only your applicant type, for example "Small businesses."
  3. Narrow down by Category or Agency.
  4. Open the funding notice (NOFO) for each result. Read the eligibility, cost-sharing and deadline sections before anything else.

Example: if a nonprofit filters only by keyword, such as "workforce," the results can include grants open only to state governments. Adding the Eligibility filter removes those before you spend time reading them.

Simpler.Grants.gov is a newer search interface for the same opportunities. It's useful for browsing. Before you apply, still confirm the instructions in the official notice.

Step 4: Apply in Workspace and submit early

Fill out the application in a Grants.gov Workspace and submit it 24–48 hours before the deadline.

  1. From the opportunity page, start a Workspace (a shared online folder for one application) and add your team members.
  2. Complete the forms. Most include the SF-424, the standard federal application form (SF-424 instructions). Upload attachments in the format the notice asks for.
  3. An AOR submits the application. Grants.gov recommends submitting at least 24–48 hours before the close date, and validation can take up to two business days.
  4. Watch your inbox for two emails: a submission receipt, then either a validation email or a rejection email listing errors. If neither arrives, check your spam folder and contact support@grants.gov (Grants.gov Help).
  5. Track progress on Track My Application until the status says "Agency Tracking Number Assigned" (Grants.gov FAQs).

Common mistakes and fixes

Almost all first-time blockers come from doing the steps out of order, or from a mismatch between your SAM.gov record and your other records.

MistakeFix
Got a "UEI only" and tried to applyGo back to SAM.gov and complete Register Entity (SAM.gov)
Started SAM.gov a week before the deadlineStart registration 3–4+ weeks ahead. Verification issues can take weeks (Grants.gov)
Legal name or address doesn't match IRS recordsEnter your name and address exactly as the IRS has them. New EINs may need time before they match (SAM checklist)
Nobody has the AOR roleThe EBiz POC assigns it under Manage Profiles (Grants.gov)
SAM.gov registration expiredRenew every 365 days (SAM.gov)
Paid a "SAM registration service"Registration is always free. Treat payment requests as possible fraud (GSA)
Submitted 10 minutes before the deadlineSubmit 24–48 hours early to leave time to fix errors

Next steps

You can finish the setup work today and have your registration processing before you even choose a grant.

Do these today:

  1. Gather your EIN, bank details, NAICS code and the name of your EBiz POC. Then start Register Entity on SAM.gov.
  2. Create your Login.gov account and Grants.gov account so they're ready when SAM.gov activates.
  3. Save a Grants.gov search with Posted + Forecasted status and your eligibility type, and check it weekly.

If you'd rather not do it yourself (costs as of October 2026; check the official pages):

  • APEX Accelerators (formerly PTACs): free. They help with SAM.gov registration and work out of 300+ locations.
  • Good fit: businesses that also want to sell to the government under contract.
  • Limitation: they focus on contracting, so they're less specialized in writing grant proposals.
  • Federal Service Desk: free help with SAM.gov problems (GSA).
  • Limitation: it fixes registration issues only and gives no strategy advice.
  • Small Business Development Centers: individual advising on planning and access to capital (SBA).
  • Good fit: owners still deciding whether a grant fits their business at all.
  • Limitation: cost and grant expertise vary by center, so ask your local center first.
  • Paid grant writers or consultants: prices vary widely, so get a written quote.
  • Good fit: SBIR applicants or nonprofits facing complex federal notices.
  • Not for: anyone who hasn't finished SAM.gov yet. Do the free registration first.

FAQ

Can I get a federal grant to start a small business?

Usually not. The SBA says it does not provide grants for starting or expanding a business (SBA). Federal grants for for-profit companies mostly fund research (SBIR/STTR) or specific public goals. Start-up money more often comes from loans, state or local programs, or investors.

How long does it take to be ready to apply?

Plan on about 2–4 weeks. SAM.gov takes 7–10 business days, or longer if your details need extra verification, and Grants.gov needs 24–48 hours after that to sync (Grants.gov). Your Grants.gov account itself is usually set up the same day.

Is the UEI the same as SAM.gov registration?

No. You can get a UEI with only your name and address, but that does not let you apply for federal awards directly. To apply, you need a full entity registration (SAM.gov).

Do I have to pay to register on SAM.gov or Grants.gov?

No. SAM.gov registration, updates and renewals are free, and the government will never ask you to pay for them (GSA).

Can I apply as an individual without a company?

Yes, but only for opportunities open to "Individuals." Most Grants.gov opportunities are for organizations (Grants.gov).
